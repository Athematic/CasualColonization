# The Longships

**Logline:** Around the year 1000, two longships from Greenland arrive in a green bay full of timber, carrying seed, a few goats and a chest of precious iron. The Norse settlers start building on what turns out to be someone else's salmon camp. Then fog traps the second ship among the rocks at the bay mouth, and its only way in is the knowledge of the people who were there first.

**Cover moment:** The first longship's crew wade ashore in knee-deep water: Thóra with her basket of seed, Kolbeinn with his axe on his shoulder, Ulf hauling the mooring rope, and the second ship gliding in behind. It is the splash on **page 2** (panel 4).

**Setting:** A bay on the northern tip of Newfoundland, in a cool summer around the year 1000.

**Cast**
- **Thóra Hallvardsdóttir:** a Norse settler in her early twenties, the skipper's daughter. A long fair braid with a blue headband, a blue wool dress, a cream apron held by round bronze brooches, a basket of barley seed she guards like treasure. Practical and warm, and the first to ask a question when others shout.
- **Kolbeinn:** a woodworker of about thirty. Light brown hair and beard, a rust-red tunic, grey trousers with leg-wrappings, a long-handled axe always on his shoulder. Came to Vinland for one reason: trees.
- **Ulf:** a sailor of about thirty-five. A dark beard, a brown headband, a green tunic and a belt with a knife pouch. Always hauling a rope; he believes everything can be solved by pulling harder.
- **Hallvard:** the skipper and Thóra's father, in his fifties. A grey beard and a blue cloak. Commands the second longship.
- **Grím the Loud:** a young crewman with a big voice and bigger opinions. A round shield he likes to bang.
- **Nuta:** a young woman of about seventeen, of the people who had lived on these shores for generations (ancestors, perhaps, of the Beothuk). Long black hair, clothes of soft caribou hide with fringed seams touched with red ochre, a crescent-shaped birch-bark canoe she paddles faster than anyone. Bold, curious, teasing.
- **Kesik:** Nuta's grandfather, an elder. White hair tied back, a cloak of caribou hide, a calm voice. Knows every rock in the bay by the sound the water makes on it.

*Nuta and Kesik's people speak their own language, which the Norse do not understand. Their lines are given in English in angled brackets, < >, so the reader can follow them; the Norse hear only sounds.*

## PAGE 1

**Panel 1.** Wide panel. Open grey-blue sea under low cloud. Two longships with red-and-cream striped square sails cut through long swells, round shields in plain colours along their sides, carved prows rising. Spray.
CAPTION: Far west of Greenland, around the year one thousand.
CAPTION: In Greenland there was grass and ice and sea. What there was not, was trees.

**Panel 2.** On the first ship's deck, crowded with cargo: a few goats in a pen, sacks, barrels, a wooden chest bound with rope. Kolbeinn sits on the chest, sharpening his axe with a whetstone.
KOLBEINN: Every nail, every rivet, every axe head we own is in this chest. If we lose it, we build a whole settlement with our teeth.
ULF: My teeth are not very good.

**Panel 3.** Thóra holds her basket of seed tight on her lap, a cloak over it against the spray.
THÓRA: And if we lose this, we eat our teeth.
GRÍM (from the bow): Land! I smell LAND!

**Panel 4.** Close on Grím, standing on the prow, nose in the air like a hound. Ulf rolls his eyes.
ULF: You smell Grím. It's been three weeks.

**Panel 5.** The cloud tears open ahead. Through the gap: a long green coast with dark forest, sunlight on it.
CAPTION: And then, at last...

## PAGE 2

**Panel 1.** The two ships ride into a calm bay. A rocky headland with grassy turf on the right; green meadows, a brook running into the sea, and dark spruce forest beyond.
CAPTION: Grass for the goats. A brook for water. And trees: more trees than all of Greenland had ever seen.

**Panel 2.** Close on Kolbeinn, gripping the rail, face lit with love.
KOLBEINN (whisper): Look at them. Every one a ship's mast. Every one a roof beam.
THÓRA: Every one somebody's shade, too, Kolbeinn. Don't cut them all on the first day.

**Panel 3.** Hallvard's second ship stays back at the bay mouth, where the water is broken with rocks. Hallvard waves from the stern.
HALLVARD (distant): Go in! I'll anchor here and come in on the tide!

**Panel 4.** SPLASH PANEL, the cover moment, most of the page. The first longship, striped sail still drawn, grounds in the shallows. Thóra wades ashore in her blue dress and cream apron, her fair braid swinging, basket of seed held high. Beside her Kolbeinn strides with his axe on his shoulder, and Ulf leans back hauling a heavy rope towards the shore. Crewmen lean over the shields on the ship's side. In the background the second longship glides across the bay. Seabirds wheel over the water; the green headland rises at the right.
CAPTION: The first steps in a new land.
ULF: Pull, everyone! Pull... why am I the only one pulling?
KOLBEINN: Because you're the only one holding the rope, Ulf.

## PAGE 3

**Panel 1.** On the shore. Thóra kneels and runs her fingers through the dark, damp earth. She smiles.
THÓRA: Good soil. The barley might even like it here.

**Panel 2.** Up by the brook, on a low grassy terrace, Kolbeinn and Ulf find things: a ring of hearth stones, a frame of birch poles for drying fish, a pile of cleaned fish bones, and the curved mark in the sand where a canoe has been dragged.
ULF: Somebody's had a fire.
KOLBEINN: And somebody's been drying fish on these poles. Salmon, by the bones.

**Panel 3.** Grím arrives, banging his shield cheerfully with his fist.
GRÍM: Nobody here now! First come, first served. This flat ground is perfect. We build here!
THÓRA: Grím, someone *was* here first. They built these racks.
GRÍM: And they left them. Finders keepers.

**Panel 4.** Wide. The crew, eager and tired, start building booths of turf and timber right on the terrace, around the drying racks. Kolbeinn fells a spruce at the edge of the forest.
SFX: TOK! TOK! TOK!
CAPTION: So they built where it was easiest. It is what tired people do.

**Panel 5.** Unseen by any of them, in the reeds on the far side of the brook, two faces watch: Nuta and Kesik. Nuta looks indignant. Kesik looks amused.
NUTA: < They're putting a house on our salmon! >
KESIK: < Big boats. Small sense. Let's see what they do next. >

## PAGE 4

**Panel 1.** Next morning. Kolbeinn swings his axe at a big spruce. The axe head, loose on its handle, flies off.
SFX: TOK! TOK! TOK-SHWING!

**Panel 2.** The iron axe head spins through the air in a high arc over the shore...
SFX: whirrrrrr...

**Panel 3.** ...and lands in the tidal shallows of the brook mouth with a small, sad splash.
SFX: plip.

**Panel 4.** Kolbeinn on his knees at the water's edge, holding the empty handle, desperate. The water is dark and peaty; he can't see the bottom.
KOLBEINN: My axe! My best axe! There are only four in the whole chest!
ULF: Then you have three. That's still a lot of axes.
KOLBEINN: It's three *fewer than four*, Ulf!

**Panel 5.** Thóra looks up from the beach and freezes. Around the headland come three crescent-shaped birch-bark canoes, high at both ends, paddled swiftly. In the first, Nuta. In the second, Kesik. The bay goes very quiet.
THÓRA: ...Kolbeinn. Stop shouting about the axe.

## PAGE 5

**Panel 1.** The canoes come in to the far side of the brook, opposite the Norse booths. Nuta's family step out: men, women, two children, all in caribou hide touched with red ochre. They look at the booths built on their drying ground.
CAPTION: The people of the bay had come back to their summer fishing place.

**Panel 2.** The two groups face each other across the narrow brook. Nobody moves. Ulf, startled, drops his rope.
SFX: flump.
ULF (whisper): I dropped the rope.
KOLBEINN (whisper): Leave the rope.

**Panel 3.** Grím steps forward and bangs his shield, chest out, to look important. The bay people take a step back.
SFX: BONG! BONG!
GRÍM: We are Norsemen! We have come from over the sea!
THÓRA: Grím. *Put. It. Down.*

**Panel 4.** Thóra walks to the edge of the brook alone. She sets her precious basket of seed down on a stone, carefully, and shows her empty hands.
THÓRA: We are Thóra and Kolbeinn and Ulf and... the loud one is Grím. We are tired and we come in peace. And I think we've built on your fishing place. I'm sorry.

**Panel 5.** On the other bank Nuta looks at her grandfather. Kesik nods. Nuta steps forward, sets a large fresh salmon on a stone on her side, and shows her empty hands too.
NUTA: < I am Nuta. That is my grandfather's fish rack your friend is using as a clothes line. >
THÓRA: I don't understand a word. But I think she's angry about the washing.

## PAGE 6

**Panel 1.** Grím, offended at being ignored, bangs his shield again. Kesik frowns. The bay people turn back towards their canoes, unhappy.
GRÍM: Don't turn your backs on a Norseman!
SFX: BONG!
NUTA (to Kesik, over her shoulder): < Is he always this noisy? >
KESIK: < I hope not. It must be very tiring for his friends. >

**Panel 2.** Hallvard's horn sounds from the bay mouth. Everyone turns. A grey bank of fog is rolling in over the sea, swallowing the headland.
SFX: HOOOOOOOOMMMM
THÓRA: Father's horn!

**Panel 3.** Wide panel, half white with fog. The second longship, barely visible, has started to come in on the tide, and it is among the rocks at the bay mouth. Dark rocks stick up through the water around it.
SFX (distant): HOOOOMM! HOOOOMM!
HALLVARD (distant, tiny lettering): ...can't see! ...rocks on all sides!

**Panel 4.** On the shore, panic. Ulf is already dragging the small ship's boat to the water. Kolbeinn grabs an oar.
ULF: We go out and pull them clear!
KOLBEINN: In this fog? We'll find the rocks before we find them.

**Panel 5.** Close on Thóra, white-faced. Then she looks across the brook to where Kesik stands, head tilted, eyes closed, listening.
THÓRA: ...Somebody here knows this bay better than we do.

## PAGE 7

**Panel 1.** Thóra splashes straight across the brook, skirt held up, to the bay people's side. Grím shouts behind her.
GRÍM: Thóra! Come back!
THÓRA: Be quiet, Grím! For once in your life!

**Panel 2.** Thóra stands before Kesik and Nuta, out of breath. She points out to the fog, makes a ship shape with her hands, mimes rocks, mimes the ship striking them, and puts her hands together, pleading.
THÓRA: My father. The ship. The rocks. Please. I don't know how to say it.
NUTA: < She's saying the second boat is going to hit the Teeth. >

**Panel 3.** Kesik opens his eyes and smiles slightly. He holds up one hand: listen. Faintly, from the fog, the sounds of water on rocks: a slap, a hiss, a gurgle, different in each place.
SFX (small, scattered): slap... hssss... glulk...
KESIK: < Every rock talks. The Teeth hiss. The Old Woman gurgles. The safe channel says nothing at all. >

**Panel 4.** Nuta grins, already running for her canoe.
NUTA: < I'll go! Grandfather, ride in my canoe and listen! >
KESIK: < I always do. You paddle too fast to hear anything. >

**Panel 5.** Thóra calls back across the brook to the Norse.
THÓRA: Ulf! Kolbeinn! Follow their canoe! Exactly where it goes, not one stroke wide!

## PAGE 8

**Panel 1.** Into the fog. Nuta's crescent canoe slides ahead, Kesik sitting very still in it with his head tilted. Behind it, close, the Norse ship's boat with Ulf and Kolbeinn rowing, Thóra in the bow, a coil of rope at her feet.
CAPTION: Into the white.

**Panel 2.** Close on Kesik in the canoe, eyes closed. He points left with two fingers. Nuta turns the canoe at once.
SFX (around them): hssss... hssss...
KESIK: < The Teeth. Go wide. >

**Panel 3.** The Norse boat follows the canoe's exact path. A big black rock looms out of the fog close beside them, water hissing on it.
ULF (whisper): By all the... that was close enough to kiss.
KOLBEINN (whisper): Don't kiss the rock, Ulf. Row.

**Panel 4.** Out of the fog, the second longship's carved prow appears, high above them. Hallvard leans over the side.
HALLVARD: Thóra?! What are you doing out here, and who...
THÓRA: Father, throw us your rope and do *exactly* what she does!

**Panel 5.** Ulf catches Hallvard's thrown rope and ties it to the ship's boat. Nuta's canoe is ahead of them, Kesik's hand pointing into the silence of the channel.
ULF: Finally. A rope worth pulling.

## PAGE 9

**Panel 1.** A long horizontal panel. The procession through the fog: Nuta's canoe in front; the Norse ship's boat behind it, rowing hard; the rope; and the great longship following on the tow, its crew pushing off rocks with oars on both sides.
SFX: slap... hssss... (silence)... slap...

**Panel 2.** Close on Nuta, paddling, enjoying herself enormously.
NUTA: < Big boat follows little boat! Grandfather, they'll tell stories about this! >
KESIK: < They'll tell it wrong. They'll say *they* found the way. >

**Panel 3.** The fog thins. The longship slides into the calm inner bay, safe, its striped sail glowing as the sun breaks through.
CAPTION: And the fog lifted, as fog does, as if it had never been there.

**Panel 4.** On the shore, both groups, the Norse crew and the bay people, cheer and wave together without thinking. Grím, in the middle of them, cheers loudest of all.
ALL: HAAAAY!
GRÍM: HURRAH!
NUTA'S COUSIN: < He's even loud when he's happy. >

**Panel 5.** Hallvard climbs down into the shallows and wades to Kesik. The old skipper and the old listener look at each other, then clasp forearms.
HALLVARD: I don't know your name. But my ship and my people owe you their lives.
KESIK: < I don't know what he said. But he looks like a man who'll move his house off my salmon. >

## PAGE 10

**Panel 1.** The next day. The Norse take down their half-built booths on the fishing terrace. Grím, grumbling, carries an armful of turf across the brook.
CAPTION: The next day, the Norse moved house.
GRÍM: All this turf. Twice.
THÓRA: It's only turf, Grím. Next time, ask before you build.

**Panel 2.** Wide. On the open meadow across the brook, near the shore and the forest edge, the first proper Norse long houses begin to rise, with turf walls and timber frames. The fishing terrace is clear again, and Nuta's family's drying racks are hung with salmon.
CAPTION: There was plenty of room. There usually is, once somebody asks.

**Panel 3.** Hallvard and Kesik sit on a rock drawing in the sand with sticks: a line for the brook, the fishing place on one side, the Norse houses on the other, a path down to the shore between them.
HALLVARD: Your fish there. Our houses here. The path for everyone.
KESIK: < Fish, houses, path. And no more banging shields. >

**Panel 4.** Thóra and Nuta trade on the beach. Thóra offers a length of red-and-cream striped wool, sail-cloth. Nuta looks at the iron knife at Ulf's belt, shakes her head (she has her own fine stone and bone tools), and points delightedly at the cloth.
NUTA: < The stripes! Like the big sails! >
THÓRA: You like it? Then it's yours. For the salmon. And for my father.

**Panel 5.** In return, Nuta leads Thóra up the slope behind the bay, to bushes heavy with dark berries. She pops one in her own mouth, then one in Thóra's.
THÓRA (mouth full): Mmmf! Sweet! Like... like wine-berries!
CAPTION: The Norse would later call this whole land after berries like these.

## PAGE 11

**Panel 1.** Low tide at the brook mouth, the water drained to a sheet of wet sand and pebbles. Kolbeinn walks along, sadly, with his empty axe handle.
KOLBEINN: Somewhere in all this mud lies the best iron in Vinland.

**Panel 2.** A splash behind him. Nuta's little cousin, a boy of about eight, comes running up from the shallows holding something heavy and muddy above his head with both hands.
COUSIN: < Hey! Hey! Big-axe man! >

**Panel 3.** Close: the iron axe head, dripping mud, in the boy's small hands. Kolbeinn's face, astonished.
KOLBEINN: My axe! You found it! You... you give it back?

**Panel 4.** Kolbeinn, overcome, tries to give the axe head to the boy as a gift. The boy looks at it, puzzled, tries to lift it, nearly falls over with the weight, and pushes it back with a laugh.
COUSIN: < Too heavy! What would I do with it? You need it more. You're always chopping. >
NUTA (laughing): < He's right. You chop like a woodpecker with a headache. >

**Panel 5.** Kolbeinn, grinning, carves a small wooden toy longship for the boy instead, with a tiny striped sail.
KOLBEINN: Then you get a boat. A little one. With stripes.
COUSIN: < Now *this* is a good trade. >

## PAGE 12

**Panel 1.** Late summer. Thóra kneels in a small dug field beside the long houses, scattering her barley seed in the furrows. Hallvard stands beside her, looking out to sea.
CAPTION: By late summer there were houses, a field and a smithy.
HALLVARD: The ship from Greenland comes next summer, if the ice allows. Until then we have only what's in the chest.
THÓRA: We have more than that, Father.

**Panel 2.** Grím sits on the shore with Nuta's cousins, teaching them to bang his shield like a drum. They are better at the rhythm than he is.
SFX: bong-ba-bong! bong-ba-bong!
GRÍM: Not bad! You're almost as loud as me!

**Panel 3.** Ulf and Nuta's uncle haul a net together through the shallows, both leaning back on the rope, faces red.
ULF: You see? Everything is solved by pulling harder.
NUTA'S UNCLE: < Pull harder! >

**Panel 4.** Kesik sits by his fire on the fishing terrace, and Hallvard comes to sit beside him. Neither speaks. They share a piece of smoked salmon and watch the sea.
CAPTION: Some friendships need no language. Only salmon.

**Panel 5.** FINAL PANEL, wide, golden evening. On the beach, side by side, the two great longships with their striped sails furled, and the three crescent birch-bark canoes drawn up beside them, one with a little toy longship on its seat. Thóra and Nuta sit on the upturned edge of a canoe, laughing, Thóra's seed basket between them, now full to the brim with dark berries. Behind them, smoke rises from the turf houses on one side of the brook and from the fish fires on the other.
NUTA: < Next year, you'll learn our words. >
THÓRA: Next year, I'll learn your words.
CAPTION: She didn't understand a word of it. She answered it anyway.
CAPTION: Two peoples, one bay, and the start of a story.
