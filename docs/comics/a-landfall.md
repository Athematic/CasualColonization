# Landfall

**Logline.** A small ship's company lands on a wild Atlantic shore and sets out to found their colony on the greenest, flattest meadow they have ever seen. Their scout and the women and men of the longhouse town upriver know something they don't: the meadow is green because the river visits it. One stormy night, a floating crate and a borrowed canoe later, the newcomers learn to choose their ground the way their neighbours do: by asking first.

**Cover moment.** The longboat grounding on the beach: Elias Fenwick stepping ashore with his crate, Corporal Hobb steadying the boat, Will Saddler looking inland with his spade, and Tomah on his bay horse on the rise, pointing towards the interior. It is the splash panel on **page 2** (panel 1), and the rise Tomah points from becomes the colony's site on page 11.

**Setting.** A river mouth on the Atlantic coast of North America, below the hill country of the Haudenosaunee. Late spring, 1662.

## Cast

- **Elias Fenwick**, leader of the settlers. Forties, brown beard, brown knee-length coat, grey waistcoat, broad-brimmed brown hat, buckled shoes. Never lets go of a small wooden crate whose contents he will not discuss.
- **Corporal Nathaniel Hobb**, the expedition's only soldier. Grey coat with white cross-belts, black tricorn, white stockings, a canvas satchel on his hip. Solemn, fond of rules and "defensible positions".
- **Will Saddler**, pioneer. Early twenties, brown felt hat, buff waistcoat over white shirt, green breeches, high boots, a huge bedroll pack with tools sticking out, and a spade he leans on. Cheerful, always hungry.
- **Tomah**, scout and interpreter. Wabanaki, from the coasts further north; has sailed with Captain Penhallow before and speaks several languages of the coast. Long black hair, red headband, fringed buckskin hunting shirt, leggings, rides a bay horse called Biscuit. Dry humour.
- **Kahentinetha**, a senior woman of the longhouse town upriver. Fifties, calm, sharp-eyed, ochre-brown wrap dress and leggings, hair in a long braid. She decides where the town's fields go.
- **Ohkwari**, her nephew, about eighteen. Name means "bear"; he is tall and thin as a heron, which everyone finds funny except him. Ochre tunic, leggings, quick canoe paddler.
- **Captain Agnes Penhallow**, master of the caravel *Kestrel*. Weathered, short grey hair under a knitted cap, sea-coat. Appears mostly from the ship.

## PAGE 1

**Panel 1.** Wide establishing panel, top third of the page. Dawn over a calm blue bay. The small two-masted caravel *Kestrel* rides at anchor, triangular sails half furled, a plain red pennant at the masthead. Beyond: a pale beach, a river mouth, green meadows, forest, and far blue hills with snow on the highest peaks. Gulls low over the water.

CAPTION: Spring, 1662. After nine weeks at sea, the *Kestrel* drops anchor off an unknown shore.

**Panel 2.** On deck, medium shot. Settlers crowd the rail: Elias Fenwick holding his small crate against his chest, Will Saddler with his huge pack, Corporal Hobb straightening his tricorn. Captain Penhallow stands at the helm behind them, arms folded.

PENHALLOW: There's your new country, Master Fenwick. Mind you don't drop it.

FENWICK: Captain, I have carried this crate across an ocean. I shall not drop it on the last ten yards.

**Panel 3.** Close on Will, nose lifted, sniffing the wind happily.

WILL: Smell that! Pine and grass and... is that wood smoke?

**Panel 4.** Tomah leading his bay horse up from the hold on a wooden ramp; the horse plants its hooves and refuses. Two sailors push from behind.

TOMAH: Biscuit agrees with you, Will. He has smelled the grass. He would just prefer to arrive without swimming.

SFX: HRRNNHH!

**Panel 5.** Small panel. Hobb peers through a short telescope towards the river, very serious.

HOBB: Smoke upriver. Longhouses. We are not the first people here.

TOMAH (off-panel): No. Nobody ever is.

## PAGE 2

**Panel 1.** Splash panel, half the page: THE COVER MOMENT. The longboat grounds on pale sand. Fenwick steps out with the crate, looking inland; Hobb, knee-deep in the shallows, steadies the boat's gunwale; Will, already ashore, leans on his spade and gazes at the meadows. On a low grassy rise at the right, Tomah sits on Biscuit, pointing towards the interior. Far off on the river bank, a cluster of bark longhouses with smoke rising, and two small figures at the water's edge, watching calmly.

SFX (the boat on sand): SKRRRSH

CAPTION: Landfall.

**Panel 2.** Fenwick, boots now on dry sand, holds the crate up like a trophy.

FENWICK: Dry land! Mark the hour, Corporal.

HOBB: Twenty past seven, Master Fenwick. Approximately.

**Panel 3.** Will kneels and scoops up a handful of dark soil at the edge of the meadow, beaming.

WILL: Black as a pudding. You could grow anything in this.

**Panel 4.** Tomah, still on the rise, lowers his arm and frowns slightly at the meadow, which runs flat and green all the way to the river.

TOMAH (small, to himself): Hm. Very flat. Very green.

## PAGE 3

**Panel 1.** Wide. The meadow by the river mouth: knee-high lush grass dotted with flowers, a few lonely willows, the river curling past. Fenwick strides into the middle of it, crate under one arm, the other arm sweeping wide. Will and Hobb follow. Tomah rides behind, slower.

FENWICK: Here. No trees to clear, no stones to dig. The river at our door for the mill. Gentlemen, this is where we build.

**Panel 2.** Hobb paces, measuring with his strides, counting on his fingers.

HOBB: Open ground on every side. A poor defensive position, but a very fine parade ground.

**Panel 3.** Tomah dismounts and crouches by a willow. Close on his hand: a thin line of dried reeds, sticks and a small crab shell caught high on the willow's trunk, at about the height of his shoulder.

TOMAH: Master Fenwick. Come and look at this tree.

**Panel 4.** Fenwick glances over without stopping, already pushing a stake into the ground with his boot.

FENWICK: It's a willow, Tomah. We have them at home.

TOMAH: At home, do your willows wear crabs for hats?

**Panel 5.** Close on Fenwick, distracted, waving it off. In the background, two figures walking along the river bank towards them: a woman (Kahentinetha) and a tall thin young man (Ohkwari) carrying a paddle.

FENWICK: Birds, I expect. Will, the first stake goes here!

## PAGE 4

**Panel 1.** Medium shot. Kahentinetha and Ohkwari arrive at the edge of the meadow. She stands calmly, hands folded; he holds the paddle and looks at Biscuit with great interest. The settlers turn, a little startled. Hobb straightens and removes his tricorn politely.

KAHENTINETHA: Shé:kon.

TOMAH: She says hello. Say it back. Shé:kon.

**Panel 2.** Fenwick, Will and Hobb, all at once, all slightly wrong, Will loudest.

FENWICK / HOBB / WILL: SHAY-KONE!

**Panel 3.** Ohkwari covers a grin behind his hand. Kahentinetha's mouth twitches.

OHKWARI: (whispering to Tomah) Are they always this loud?

TOMAH: Only when they're happy. Also when they're frightened. Also at supper.

CAPTION: Tomah speaks the trade language used along this coast. Kahentinetha speaks it rather better.

**Panel 4.** Kahentinetha looks around the meadow, at the stake in the ground, at Fenwick. She speaks to Tomah, gesturing to the grass and then to the river.

KAHENTINETHA: Your friends want to build their houses here? On the river's meadow?

**Panel 5.** Tomah translates; Fenwick nods eagerly, hat in one hand, crate in the other.

FENWICK: Tell her yes, and that we come in friendship, and that we would be honoured to have them as neighbours, and that it is the finest meadow I have ever seen!

TOMAH: He says yes.

**Panel 6.** Kahentinetha, gravely, to Tomah. Ohkwari behind her nods along with every word.

KAHENTINETHA: It is a fine meadow. The river thinks so too. It comes to visit when the moon is big and the wind is from the sea.

## PAGE 5

**Panel 1.** Fenwick, puzzled, as Tomah translates.

TOMAH: She says the river floods this meadow. Like the crab on the willow said.

FENWICK: Once in a hundred years, perhaps! Every land has its old stories.

**Panel 2.** Kahentinetha points at the willow, then at the low rise where Tomah first stood, then up the river towards her town on its higher bank.

KAHENTINETHA: My grandmother's grandmother put our fields up there. Not because she was fond of walking uphill.

**Panel 3.** Ohkwari, enthusiastic, mimes a wave with his whole body and the paddle, making a splashing noise. Will laughs and copies him.

OHKWARI: WHOOOSH! Up to here! (holds his hand to his chin)

WILL: Up to there? On you, that's very high.

**Panel 4.** Hobb leans to Fenwick, quiet, sensible for once.

HOBB: Master Fenwick, the rise is better ground. Higher. One can see the whole bay from it.

FENWICK: And twice as far to carry the water, Corporal.

**Panel 5.** Fenwick, polite but stubborn, bows to Kahentinetha.

FENWICK: Please thank her for her kindness. We will be careful. But we have only one summer to build before winter, and here the work is half done already.

**Panel 6.** Kahentinetha and Tomah exchange a long look. She shrugs, not offended, only patient.

KAHENTINETHA: Then the river will teach him. It is a good teacher. Strict, but fair. Come to the town when you are wet; we will have a fire.

TOMAH: She wishes you luck.

## PAGE 6

**Panel 1.** Montage panel, wide. Two days of work on the meadow: longboats bringing crates and barrels ashore, a canvas tent village, the frame of a first timber store-house rising, Will digging a ditch, Hobb drilling three bored settlers with long poles. Fenwick sits on his crate, drawing a plan of streets on a board.

CAPTION: For two days, the new settlement rises on the meadow.

**Panel 2.** Tomah, on Biscuit, on the rise at the edge of the meadow, looking out to sea. The sky over the sea has a long flat band of dark cloud. Gulls wheeling inland.

TOMAH: Biscuit, the gulls are coming ashore. When gulls walk on the grass, sensible people walk uphill.

**Panel 3.** At the tents, evening. Will stirring a big pot. Fenwick checks a moon rising, enormous and orange, over the bay.

WILL: Big moon tonight.

FENWICK: Splendid. We shall have light to work by.

**Panel 4.** Tomah rides up, urgent but calm.

TOMAH: Master Fenwick, it is a big moon AND the wind has turned. From the sea. Remember what she said?

FENWICK: Tomah, I remember everything anybody says. I simply don't always agree with it. Goodnight!

**Panel 5.** Small panel. Tomah, alone, leads Biscuit up the rise and ties him to a tree. He sits and pulls his blanket around himself, looking down at the dark tents.

TOMAH: We'll wait up here, Biscuit. Somebody will need a dry horse.

## PAGE 7

**Panel 1.** Night. Wide. Wind and rain sweep in from the sea in diagonal lines, flat dark blues and greys. The *Kestrel* tugs on its anchor in the bay. On the meadow, tent canvas snaps in the wind.

SFX: WHOOOOOOM

**Panel 2.** Inside a tent: Will wakes up as cold water trickles over his blanket and into his boot.

WILL: Mmf... Who's put the sea in my bed?

**Panel 3.** Outside, Hobb with a lantern, standing in water up to his ankles, then looking down as it rises visibly to his knees. Barrels bob behind him.

HOBB: ALL HANDS! THE RIVER HAS ARRIVED! THE RIVER HAS ARRIVED!

**Panel 4.** Chaos panel, wide: settlers in nightshirts wading with bundles held above their heads, a barrel floating past, a goose sitting on a floating crate looking pleased. In the middle, Fenwick in his nightshirt and hat, up to his waist, reaching desperately.

FENWICK: My crate! WHERE IS MY CRATE?

**Panel 5.** Close: the small crate drifting away on the dark current towards the river mouth and the open sea, turning slowly.

SFX: blub... blub...

## PAGE 8

**Panel 1.** A long, low elm-bark canoe slides out of the rain upstream, paddled hard by Ohkwari at the stern and two people of the town. A torch is lashed at the bow. Kahentinetha sits in the middle, wrapped in a blanket, perfectly composed.

OHKWARI: There! The little box!

**Panel 2.** Ohkwari leans far out over the side, thin arm stretched like a heron's neck, and snatches the crate by its rope handle just before it reaches the waves at the river mouth.

SFX: SPLOSH!

**Panel 3.** Up on the rise, Tomah stands in the rain, swinging his own lantern in wide arcs, calling down.

TOMAH: UP HERE! EVERYBODY UP HERE! THE GROUND IS DRY AND THE HORSE IS GRUMPY!

**Panel 4.** More canoes arrive from the town. Wide panel: people of the longhouse town and the settlers together, ferrying sacks and barrels and soaked settlers out of the flood towards the rise. Hobb, very wet, is lifted into a canoe by two women, still holding his lantern and his dignity.

HOBB: Thank you, madam. Most... most kind. Niá... niá...

WOMAN IN CANOE: Niá:wen. You're welcome.

**Panel 5.** Fenwick, still waist-deep, looks up as Kahentinetha's canoe glides alongside. She holds out his crate. He stares at her, water dripping from his hat brim.

KAHENTINETHA: Your box was going home without you.

## PAGE 9

**Panel 1.** Grey dawn, wide. The rise: a dripping huddle of settlers under blankets around a big fire built by the townspeople. Below, the meadow is a shining sheet of water with only the tops of the willows and the half-built store-house frame sticking out. The *Kestrel* is safe in the bay.

CAPTION: By morning, the meadow is a lake.

**Panel 2.** Will, wrapped in a blanket, gratefully eating cornmeal cakes from a bark tray handed to him by Ohkwari.

WILL: These are wonderful. What are they?

OHKWARI: Corn. Grown up there, where it stays dry. (points upriver, deadpan)

**Panel 3.** Fenwick sits on a stone, crate on his knees, staring down at the drowned meadow. Tomah sits beside him with Biscuit's reins.

FENWICK: You told me. She told me. The crab on the willow told me.

TOMAH: The crab was very clear.

**Panel 4.** Close on Fenwick, rueful, beginning to smile at himself despite everything.

FENWICK: I have been an ass, Tomah.

TOMAH: A wet one. That is the best kind. A wet ass learns quickly.

**Panel 5.** Fenwick stands and walks to Kahentinetha by the fire, hat in hand, crate under his arm. She watches him come with polite interest.

FENWICK: Tomah, please tell her I was wrong, and that I am grateful, and that I would like to ask her advice. Properly, this time.

---

## PAGE 10

**Panel 1.** Kahentinetha, listening to Tomah's translation, raises her eyebrows. Ohkwari, behind her, mouths "properly" to Will, who grins.

KAHENTINETHA: Advice is free. Listening is the expensive part. Come and walk.

**Panel 2.** Wide panel. Kahentinetha, Fenwick, Tomah (leading Biscuit), Hobb and Will walk along the top of the rise in morning sunshine. She points out features as they walk: the springs coming out of the hillside, a stand of oaks, the line of drift and reeds below which the flood never reaches.

KAHENTINETHA: Water here comes out of the hill, clean. The river stays down there where it belongs. The oaks tell you the ground drains well.

**Panel 3.** She stops and gestures at the cleared ground between the oaks and the edge of the rise, overlooking the bay.

KAHENTINETHA: And from here, you can see who comes from the sea.

HOBB: (moved almost to tears) A defensible position.

**Panel 4.** Tomah, translating Fenwick's question with a slightly worried look.

FENWICK: Ask her: is this ground anyone's? We don't want to build where it isn't welcome.

**Panel 5.** Kahentinetha regards Fenwick for a moment, approvingly, as if he has finally said something sensible.

KAHENTINETHA: Good question. It is between our town and the sea. It belongs to nobody's fields. If you live here, you are our neighbours. Neighbours help when the river comes. Both ways.

**Panel 6.** Fenwick nods firmly, and offers his hand. She looks at it, then clasps his forearm instead in her own way; he copies her, a little clumsily.

FENWICK: Both ways. Agreed.

## PAGE 11

**Panel 1.** Montage, wide. Weeks of work on the rise in summer: settlers and townspeople hauling timber; Will showing Ohkwari how a spade works while Ohkwari shows Will how to set corn hills, both baffled by the other's method; Hobb building a neat palisade along the bay side; the first timber house rising among the oaks. The shell of the old store-house frame has been dragged up from the meadow and reused.

CAPTION: The colony starts again, uphill this time.

**Panel 2.** Medium panel. The first house is finished: timber frame, plank walls, a steep shingle roof, a stone chimney. Settlers and townspeople gathered in front. Fenwick stands before them all, holding his famous crate.

FENWICK: Friends. Neighbours. Since we left home, everyone has wondered what is in this box.

WILL: (loud whisper) I always guessed cake.

**Panel 3.** Close: Fenwick prises open the lid. Inside, nestled in damp moss, are a dozen small apple seedlings, green leaves just opening.

FENWICK: Apple trees. From my father's orchard. I carried them across the sea so the first thing we planted would be something we could share.

**Panel 4.** Kahentinetha leans in and looks at the seedlings with genuine interest, then turns and gestures to Ohkwari, who brings forward a small bark basket.

KAHENTINETHA: Then a trade. Your trees for our seeds. Corn, beans and squash. They grow together and they look after each other. Like neighbours should.

**Panel 5.** Close on the two pairs of hands exchanging the crate and the basket: Fenwick's big pale hands, Kahentinetha's brown, steady ones.

TOMAH (caption box, his voice): And that is how a colony is founded. Not with a flag. With a swap.

---

## PAGE 12

**Panel 1.** Wide, autumn now: trees on the forest edge turning red and gold. Fenwick and Kahentinetha plant the first apple seedling together at the edge of the rise, Fenwick kneeling with a trowel, Kahentinetha pressing the earth down. Will, Ohkwari and children of both peoples watch. Below, the meadow is green again, with the town's corn fields higher up the river beyond it.

CAPTION: Autumn.

**Panel 2.** Hobb at the new gate in the palisade, painting the colony's name on a board. We only see the back of the board; Will and Ohkwari read it over his shoulder.

WILL: You can't call it that!

HOBB: I can, and I have. The council voted.

**Panel 3.** Tomah, Fenwick and Kahentinetha come up to see. Fenwick looks at the board and bursts out laughing. Kahentinetha smiles widely for the first time in the story.

FENWICK: "Second Thought"! Ha!

KAHENTINETHA: (to Tomah) A good name. The second thought is usually the better one.

**Panel 4.** Tomah, mounting Biscuit, ready to ride on. Fenwick reaches up to shake his hand.

FENWICK: Where will you go now?

TOMAH: Inland. Over those hills. Somebody has to meet the people beyond them before your next ship lands somewhere silly.

**Panel 5.** Final panel, large and wide, bottom half of the page. Evening on the rise. The first lamp glows in the window of the timber house among the oaks; the young apple tree, tied to a stake, stands in front of it. Upriver, smoke rises from the longhouses; down below, the meadow shines silver in the low sun, completely and peacefully flooded again by the evening tide. Tomah and Biscuit are small figures riding along the ridge towards the blue hills. Kahentinetha and Fenwick stand side by side at the edge of the rise, watching the water come in, both perfectly dry.

FENWICK: (small balloon) She was right, you know. It visits.

KAHENTINETHA: (small balloon) It always does.

CAPTION: THE END.
