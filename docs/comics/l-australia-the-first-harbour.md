# The First Harbour

**Logline:** In 1788 the ships of the first convict fleet anchor in an Eora harbour. A hungry teenage convict "borrows" an Eora woman's bark canoe to catch fish, and it overturns when a southerly storm hits. He is fished out by the family he took it from. To put things right he has to learn from them how this harbour feeds people, and the young marine officer watching has to decide whose canoe it is.

**Cover moment:** Warrin, Naya and Tarri watch from the sandstone ledge above the cove as the longboats row in to the beach. It is the opening splash, **page 1** (panel 1), and the ledge view returns, reversed, in the final panel on page 12.

**Setting:** Warrane (the cove the newcomers call Sydney Cove) and the harbour around it, on the east coast of Australia, from late January to March 1788.

**Cast**
- **Warrin:** an Eora fisherman of about thirty. Tall, a kangaroo-skin wrap at the waist, two stripes of white clay on his upper arm, a long multi-pronged fishing spear. Patient, watchful, slow to smile and then smiles completely.
- **Naya:** an Eora fisherwoman, Warrin's wife. Dark curly hair tied back, a brown cloak around her shoulders, white clay stripes on her arm, a string around her neck. The best line-fisher on the harbour, and well aware of it.
- **Tarri:** their daughter, about ten. Curly hair, white clay stripes on her arm, endlessly curious, quick to laugh at newcomers who fall over.
- **Kit Pollard:** a convict boy of fifteen from London, sentenced to seven years' transportation. Thin, freckled, a grey canvas jacket and trousers too big for him, a battered round hat. Talks fast, thinks later.
- **Sam Ruddle:** a convict of fifty and a former Thames fisherman, who says he is the best in Wapping. Barrel-chested, grey stubble, a striped neckerchief. Has not caught a fish since he arrived.
- **Lieutenant Edwin Hale:** a young officer of marines. Red coat, white cross-belts, a black round hat with a cockade, a sketchbook always in a pocket. Draws birds when he should be counting crates.
- **Mr Josiah Pratt:** a commissary clerk. Black coat, spectacles, a ledger clamped under one arm. Believes anything not written in the ledger does not exist.

*The Eora names in this script are invented for the story. Where an Eora word is used it is kept to well-recorded ones, and the cultural detail stays general.*

## PAGE 1

**Panel 1.** Splash, two-thirds of the page: the cover moment. A broad sandstone ledge, warm and pitted, above a small sandy cove. In the foreground, Warrin stands with his fishing spear upright, Naya sits wrapped in her cloak beside a small fire, Tarri sits at her side hugging her knees. A basket of fresh fish and a bunch of green leaves lie by the fire. Below the ledge a bark canoe (a *nawi*), a long curved sheet of bark tied at the ends, is drawn up on the sand. Out on the deep blue harbour, three tall ships lie at anchor, sails furled, with plain red, blue and yellow pennants. Two longboats row towards the beach, where men in plain coats and marines in red unload crates beside white canvas tents. Rainbow lorikeets streak across the cove. Grey-green gum trees and sandstone cliffs; blue hills far off.
CAPTION: Warrane. The country of the Eora, by the harbour. Late summer, 1788.
CAPTION: Eleven ships had come in from the sea. These three anchored in front of Naya's favourite fishing rock.

**Panel 2.** Close on the three, side by side.
TARRI: Are they staying?
NAYA: Everyone who comes into the harbour leaves again. The wind always takes them.

**Panel 3.** Close on Warrin, eyes narrowed at the beach, where a man is chopping at a tree.
WARRIN: These ones are cutting trees. People who are leaving don't cut trees.

## PAGE 2

**Panel 1.** On the beach: busy chaos. Crates, casks, a goat on a rope, a pile of shovels. Marines in red. Convicts in grey canvas carrying loads. Kit Pollard staggers under a sack bigger than himself.
CAPTION: Down on the beach.
KIT: Mr Ruddle! What's in this, cannonballs?
SAM: Flour, lad. Treat it like a baby. It's all the dinner there is.

**Panel 2.** Mr Pratt at a crate-desk, ledger open, spectacles on his nose, stopping Kit with one finger.
PRATT: Sack of flour. One. Carried by convict Pollard, C. Ration of the same, one *quarter*.
KIT: A quarter? I carried the whole thing!

**Panel 3.** Lieutenant Hale sits on a rock nearby, not at all on duty, sketching a lorikeet in his book with great delight.
HALE: Extraordinary! Blue head, green back, scarlet breast... It looks as if it was painted by a committee.
PRATT: Lieutenant, the tents?
HALE: Hm? Yes. Tents. Terribly important.

**Panel 4.** Kit, sitting on a cask, looks up at the sandstone ledge. Tiny figures, the three Eora, sit up there watching.
KIT: Who's that up on the rock?
SAM: People who live here. Fair enough if they're wondering what we're doing in their front yard.
KIT: I wonder that myself.

## PAGE 3

**Panel 1.** Weeks later. Evening. A seine net is dragged up the beach by six convicts. It is almost empty: two small fish and a lot of weed. Sam holds them up, disgusted.
CAPTION: Weeks went by. The salt meat ran short, the flour ran shorter, and the gardens refused to grow.
SAM: Thirty years on the Thames! I've pulled more fish out of a London gutter than this!

**Panel 2.** Kit stares out across the darkening harbour. Out on the water, a single bark canoe glides along the rocks. In it, Naya sits with a tiny fire glowing on a pad of clay in the middle of the canoe, a fishing line over the side. Tarri paddles.
KIT: Mr Ruddle... look.

**Panel 3.** Close on Naya in the canoe. She sings softly, letting the line run through her fingers. A fish flashes up from the water on her line, the hook a curved gleam of shell.
NAYA (song, small italic letters): *...come up, come up, the fire is warm...*

**Panel 4.** Back on the beach. Kit and Sam, mouths open. In the canoe, the basket is filling.
KIT: She's caught six while we caught two!
SAM: Beginner's luck.
KIT: She doesn't look much of a beginner.

**Panel 5.** Kit, alone that night in a tent with three other snoring convicts, stares at the canvas ceiling. His stomach growls.
SFX: GRRRROWL
KIT (thinking): A boat like that. A line. One good night and we'd all eat.

## PAGE 4

**Panel 1.** Before dawn. Kit creeps along the shore around the point, past the sandstone ledge, to the little cove. There on the sand lies the bark canoe from the cover, a paddle beside it.
CAPTION: Before dawn, Kit Pollard had an idea. It wasn't a very good one.

**Panel 2.** Kit looks around. Nobody. He pats the canoe like a horse.
KIT: Nobody's using you. I'll only be an hour. Everybody gets fish, nobody gets hurt. Easy.

**Panel 3.** Unseen by Kit, at the top of the ledge, Tarri peeks over the rock, eyes wide, a half-eaten shellfish in her hand.
TARRI (whisper): ...That's Mother's canoe.

**Panel 4.** Kit pushes off. The canoe wobbles wildly. He sits down hard, just keeping it upright, and paddles away clumsily in a zigzag.
SFX: WOBBLE WOBBLE
KIT: Easy! Easy... it's just like... a very thin... tippy... ohh.

**Panel 5.** Tarri runs off along the path into the bush, legs flying.
TARRI: MOTHER! The skinny one took the canoe!

## PAGE 5

**Panel 1.** Out on the harbour, Kit drifts in the open water with a bit of line and a lump of salt pork on a bent nail. The sky to the south has gone a strange dark grey.
KIT: Come on, fish. Come up, the fire is... uh... I haven't got a fire.

**Panel 2.** The wind arrives all at once. The southerly buster: a wall of cold air, the water ruffling white, gum trees on shore bending. Kit's hat flies off.
SFX: WHOOOOOSH
CAPTION: In summer, the southerly comes up the coast like a door slammed shut.

**Panel 3.** A wave slaps the canoe sideways. Kit grabs both sides. Water pours in.
SFX: SPLASH!
KIT: No no no no no...

**Panel 4.** The canoe rolls over. Kit goes under. His hat floats away by itself, upside down, like a little boat.
SFX: SPLOOSH!

**Panel 5.** Kit surfaces, clinging to the upturned canoe, spluttering, drifting fast towards the open harbour.
KIT: HELP! Can't swim! Well, I can a bit! Not enough!

## PAGE 6

**Panel 1.** A second bark canoe cuts through the chop: Naya in the stern with a paddle, Warrin crouched at the bow, Tarri in the middle bailing with a scoop of bark. All three faces set, determined.
CAPTION: The Eora had been reading the harbour's weather for longer than anyone could count.

**Panel 2.** Warrin reaches out a long arm and grabs Kit by the back of his canvas jacket, as if lifting a fish.
WARRIN: Up.
KIT: Thank you thank you thank y...

**Panel 3.** Kit, dripping, lies across the middle of their canoe like a wet sack. Tarri looks down at him critically.
TARRI: He's very bad at canoes.
NAYA: He is. Now hold still, wet one, or you'll tip us too.

**Panel 4.** Warrin hooks the upturned canoe with his spear and they tow it behind them towards a sheltered beach in the lee of the point.
SFX: slap... slap... slap...

**Panel 5.** On the beach in the lee, out of the wind. Naya examines her own canoe, which has a long split down one side where it struck a rock. She looks at Kit. Kit looks at the sand.
NAYA: My canoe.
KIT (very small): ...I'm sorry. I thought nobody was using it.
NAYA: I was using it. Every day. You just didn't see me.

## PAGE 7

**Panel 1.** A shout from the bush. Mr Pratt, two marines and Sam Ruddle come stumbling down the path onto the beach. Pratt has his ledger held over his head against the wind.
PRATT: There! Convict Pollard! Absconded and... and captured!

**Panel 2.** Wide. The two groups face each other across the sand: Warrin, Naya and Tarri on one side, Pratt and the marines on the other, Kit dripping in the middle. Everyone tense, nobody moving. The marines' muskets stay shouldered, pointing at the sky.
PRATT: And those canoes. Unattended craft found on the shore. Crown property, by rights. Write them down, Corporal.
SAM: Mr Pratt, I don't think...

**Panel 3.** Warrin plants his spear point-down in the sand, a simple, firm gesture, and folds his arms. Naya puts a hand on the split canoe.
WARRIN: (No words, a level look.)

**Panel 4.** A third figure arrives at a run, red coat flapping, sketchbook under his arm: Lieutenant Hale, out of breath.
HALE: Mr Pratt! What on earth are you doing?
PRATT: Recording property, Lieutenant.

**Panel 5.** Kit steps forward between them, still dripping, voice cracking.
KIT: It ain't Crown property! It's hers! I took it. It tipped over in the wind and they came out and fished me out of the sea. That's all that happened!

---

## PAGE 8

**Panel 1.** Close on Hale, looking from Kit to the split canoe to Naya. He takes his hat off.
HALE: Is that true, Pollard?
KIT: Every word, sir. I'd be under the harbour if not for them.

**Panel 2.** Hale turns to Pratt, very quiet, very firm.
HALE: Mr Pratt, you will write nothing down. This canoe belongs to this lady. So does the other one. So, I suspect, does a great deal of what we have been calling "unattended".
PRATT: But, Lieutenant, the regulations...
HALE: Can sit in your ledger. Shut.

**Panel 3.** Hale faces Naya and bows slightly, awkward but sincere, hat in hand. Naya raises an eyebrow at Warrin.
HALE: I'm very sorry for your canoe. I don't know how to say it in your language, but I am.
NAYA (to Warrin): This one has better manners than the wet one.
WARRIN: The wet one said sorry too. Wetly.

**Panel 4.** Kit, chin up, faces Naya.
KIT: I'll make it right. I don't know how, but I'll work for it. Whatever it takes.

**Panel 5.** Naya looks him up and down, a slow smile spreading. Tarri giggles behind her hand.
NAYA: Good. Tomorrow we need a new canoe. You can carry the bark.
TARRI: He'll drop it.
KIT: I will *not* drop it!

## PAGE 9

**Panel 1.** Next morning, in the bush behind the cove. Warrin stands at a tall stringybark tree, marking an outline on the trunk with the edge of a stone axe. Kit and Tarri watch.
CAPTION: The next morning.
WARRIN: You take the bark from one side only. The tree lives. Next year it grows its skin back.
KIT: Like a haircut.
TARRI: Like a *what*?

**Panel 2.** The long sheet of bark eased away from the trunk with wooden wedges. Kit holds one end, straining. It is surprisingly heavy.
KIT: Not... dropping... it...

**Panel 3.** On the beach, a small fire. Warrin warms the bark over the flames so it softens, and he and Naya fold the ends together. Kit holds the ties while Tarri ties the knots. Her knots are better than his.
NAYA: Heat makes it bend. Hurry makes it crack. Some people are the same.
KIT: Are you talking about me?
NAYA: I'm talking about bark. Mostly.

**Panel 4.** Tarri shows Kit how to roll bark fibre into fishing line against her thigh, quick and even. Kit's attempt is a lumpy tangle.
TARRI: Twist, roll, twist, roll.
KIT: Mine looks like a dead worm.
TARRI: Fish like worms.

**Panel 5.** Lieutenant Hale sits nearby on the sand, sketching the canoe-building in careful detail. Beside his drawing he writes words as Tarri dictates them to him, pointing at things.
HALE: And the canoe is...?
TARRI: *Nawi*.
HALE (writing): Nawi. Wonderful. And that bird?
TARRI: That bird is *loud*.

## PAGE 10

**Panel 1.** Evening, on the harbour. The new canoe floats, glossy and red-brown. Naya sits in it, her fire glowing on its clay pad. Kit sits very still in the middle, holding his lumpy line. Tarri paddles.
CAPTION: The new canoe met the water at sunset.

**Panel 2.** Naya hands Kit a small curved hook of polished shell.
NAYA: Not a nail. Not pork. Shell, and bait the fish want. And patience, which you don't have. You can borrow mine.
KIT: I'll give it back this time. Promise.

**Panel 3.** Close on Kit's face, concentrating hard. The line twitches.
SFX: tik
KIT: Something's... something's...!

**Panel 4.** He hauls up a fat silver fish and nearly falls out of the canoe in joy. Tarri grabs his jacket to steady him.
KIT: I GOT ONE! I GOT ONE!
TARRI: Sit DOWN!
SFX: FLAP FLAP FLAP

**Panel 5.** On the beach of the cove, Sam Ruddle watches, arms folded, deeply jealous. Beside him Warrin is calmly spearing fish in the shallows.
SAM: A London boy, catching fish with a shell. It's against nature.
WARRIN: The fish don't know he's from London.

---

## PAGE 11

**Panel 1.** On the beach, the catch laid out on green leaves. Naya divides it into piles with firm, clear gestures while everyone watches.
NAYA: This for my family. This for the wet one and his people. This one goes back.
KIT: Back? Why?
NAYA: Because she's full of eggs. If you take all the fish, next summer there are no fish. Your big net takes everything. Tell your people.

**Panel 2.** Kit, solemn, nods. Hale writes in his sketchbook.
HALE: I'll tell the Governor's people about the nets. Whether they listen is another matter... but I'll tell them, and I'll keep telling them.

**Panel 3.** Back at the convict tents, by firelight. Kit hands out pieces of cooked fish to hungry, surprised faces. Sam takes his piece last.
KIT: From Naya's family. With their thanks. Well, with *my* thanks to them, really.
SAM: Best fish I've had in a twelvemonth. Don't tell anyone a boy caught it.

**Panel 4.** Mr Pratt, alone in his tent with a candle, hesitates over his ledger. Then, carefully, he writes a line. A small fish, wrapped in a leaf, sits beside his ink pot.
PRATT (writing): "Received: one fish. Given freely. Owner: not the Crown."

## PAGE 12

**Panel 1.** A wide, quiet panel. The harbour at dawn, the tents on the beach now joined by the first huts. The ships still at anchor.
CAPTION: The ships did not leave. In the years that followed, the newcomers spread far beyond this cove, and much of Eora Country, its water and its living, was taken from the people who had always cared for it.
CAPTION: That is a true and hard part of this story. But it is not the only part.

**Panel 2.** Kit and Tarri sit side by side on a rock at the water's edge, each rolling bark fibre into line. Kit's is almost straight now.
TARRI: Better. Still a bit like a worm.
KIT: A *straight* worm.

**Panel 3.** Lieutenant Hale's sketchbook lies open on the sand: drawings of the nawi, of Naya's shell hook, of a lorikeet. Under each, in his neat hand, a word in English and a word as Tarri said it.
CAPTION: Some of the newcomers chose to listen.

**Panel 4.** Naya and Warrin walk past along the shore with fishing gear. Naya calls over her shoulder.
NAYA: Wet one! Tonight we fish at the point. Bring your worm.
KIT: It's a LINE!

**Panel 5.** FINAL PANEL, wide, mirroring page 1 from the other side. Kit and Tarri sit on the sandstone ledge at sunset, a small fire between them, a basket at their feet. Below on the sand, the new nawi and the mended old one lie side by side. Out on the harbour, the ships' pennants hang still. A flight of rainbow lorikeets crosses the gold sky.
CAPTION: The Eora are still here. So is the harbour, and so are its names.
TARRI: Same time tomorrow?
KIT: Same time tomorrow.
