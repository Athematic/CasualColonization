# Liberty

**Logline.** The colony of Brightwater has cast its own bell and means to ring it at sunset, the day it declares that it will govern itself and seals a treaty of friendship with its Lenape neighbours as equals. Then the Crown's commissioner sails in and takes away the bell's clapper. Out of every trade's scrap iron, a lost envoy in the fog and a stubborn town crier who has shouted himself hoarse, the people of Brightwater find their voice anyway.

**Cover moment.** The crowd of every trade gathered at the town hall steps on the hilltop, the great bronze bell swinging in its open belfry, Obadiah Bellwether crying the news with his hand bell held high, hats flying, and young Daniel Fairfax hauling the new blue-and-white flag up its pole, with Wapimin and Amimi among the crowd and the red roofs, harbour and ships below. It is the splash panel on **page 11** (panel 1).

**Setting.** Brightwater, a mature colony on a wide bay of the North American Atlantic coast, beside the Lenape town of Shell Creek. A clear day at the end of summer, 1768.

## Cast

- **Obadiah Bellwether**, town crier. Sixties, round, white hair in a queue, black tricorn, red coat with gold facings, gold waistcoat, white stockings, a brass hand bell. The loudest man in the colony and proud of it.
- **Hal Thackeray**, blacksmith. Big, curly brown hair, sideburns, rolled sleeves, a heavy leather apron, always carries a hammer.
- **Martha Penrose**, weaver. Thirties, blue dress, white apron, white cap, a basket of cloth. Quietly runs most of the town.
- **Josiah Reed**, farmer. Brown breeches with braces, white shirt, a pitchfork. Says little, means all of it.
- **Reverend Silas Mott**, preacher. Grey-haired, black coat, black broad-brimmed hat. Gentle, persistent.
- **Ned Gully**, fisherman. Blue jacket, knitted blue cap, a net over his shoulder. Can smell weather.
- **Daniel Fairfax**, a young militiaman. Nineteen, blue coat with white cross-belt, tricorn, eager, a little too keen on "action".
- **Wapimin**, speaker of the council of the Lenape town of Shell Creek. Forties, long black hair, a beaded headband with two upright feathers, a wrap of tan trade cloth over one shoulder. Calm, dry wit, an expert waterman.
- **Amimi**, his aunt, an elder of Shell Creek. Fifties, long black hair, fringed deerskin dress, shell and bead necklace. Her name means "dove", and she is the most determined person in the story.
- **Mr Ambrose Pettigrew**, Royal Commissioner. Thin, nervous, an enormous grey wig, a crimson coat with more braid than coat, a dispatch case. A clerk at heart, sent to do a governor's job.

**Panel 1.** Wide opening panel, night. The yard behind the blacksmith's forge at Brightwater, lit orange by a roaring furnace (flat orange and yellow fills, no glow effects). Townsfolk stand in a ring around a pit dug in the earth. Hal Thackeray and two helpers, faces shielded, tip a crucible of molten bronze into the mould.

CAPTION: Brightwater, the last night of summer, 1768.

SFX: HSSSSSSSSS

**Panel 2.** Close on the faces of the watching townspeople, lit from below: Martha, Obadiah, Josiah, the Reverend, Daniel. Among them, Wapimin and Amimi of Shell Creek, watching with interest.

CAPTION: Three times, the colony asked the Crown for a bell. Three times, the Crown said no.

**Panel 3.** Obadiah leans to Martha, whispering loudly. Everyone turns to shush him.

OBADIAH: (whispering, which is still very loud) Do you think it will work?

EVERYONE: SHHH!

**Panel 4.** Close on a small heap of offerings on a bench near the pit: brass candlesticks, buttons, a cracked warming pan, a spoon, a little brass thimble.

CAPTION: So the people of Brightwater gave their candlesticks, their buttons and their warming pans, and made their own.

**Panel 5.** Small panel. Hal wipes his brow and grins.

HAL: Now we wait for it to cool. And we pray.

REVEREND MOTT: I've been praying since Tuesday, Hal.

## PAGE 2

**Panel 1.** Morning, wide. The hilltop of Brightwater: a timber town hall with a red tile roof and an open wooden belfry on one side. Ropes, pulleys and half the town haul the new bronze bell up into the belfry. Below the hill, the red roofs of the colony, a harbour full of ships, fields and forest to the mountains.

SFX: HEAVE! HEAVE!

**Panel 2.** The bell hangs in the belfry, gleaming. Hal, up on a ladder, fits a long iron clapper inside it and gives it a gentle test tap.

SFX: dongggg...

**Panel 3.** On the town hall steps, Martha unrolls a document (no readable writing). The crowd gathers.

MARTHA: At sunset today, the assembly of Brightwater will declare that this colony governs itself, by its own people, for its own good. And the bell will ring it.

**Panel 4.** She turns and gestures to Wapimin and Amimi, who stand at the foot of the steps.

MARTHA: And our first act as a free town will be the treaty our friends at Shell Creek and we have talked over all summer. Friendship, as equals. Shared fishing in the bay. Neither people to sell land that the other uses, without both councils agreeing.

**Panel 5.** Amimi nods, satisfied, to Wapimin.

AMIMI: Twelve meetings. Four arguments. One spilled pot of stew.

WAPIMIN: The stew was the best argument.

**Panel 6.** Small panel. Ned Gully, at the edge of the crowd, looks out to sea and sniffs.

NED: Ship coming. A big one.

## PAGE 3

**Panel 1.** Wide. The harbour. A large ship with a long crimson pennant drops anchor. A longboat rows ashore.

CAPTION: The Crown, it seems, has heard about the bell.

**Panel 2.** At the wharf, a thin man in an enormous grey wig and a crimson coat steps from the longboat onto the wet stones, slips, and sits down hard in a puddle, still holding his dispatch case aloft.

SFX: SPLUT!

PETTIGREW: I am perfectly all right!

**Panel 3.** On the hilltop, Pettigrew, damp, unrolls a scroll with a fat wax seal (no emblem, no readable writing) at the town hall steps. Two marines in red stand beside him, muskets shouldered. The townsfolk gather, uneasy.

PETTIGREW: By order of the Crown: there is no "assembly of Brightwater". There shall be no declaration. And there is a new tax. On bells.

**Panel 4.** Close on Obadiah, outraged, and Hal, hands on hips.

OBADIAH: A tax on BELLS?

HAL: It's the only bell we've got!

PETTIGREW: Exactly. A unique bell. A very large tax.

**Panel 5.** Pettigrew points up at the belfry. A marine climbs the ladder and unhooks the long iron clapper from inside the bell.

PETTIGREW: Until the tax is paid and loyalty is shown, the bell's tongue will remain in my keeping. A bell without a tongue cannot ring.

**Panel 6.** The marine carries the clapper away over his shoulder, down the hill to the ship. The crowd watches in stunned silence.

## PAGE 4

**Panel 1.** Inside the town hall: plain whitewashed walls, beams, benches. The townsfolk crowd in, everyone talking at once. Daniel Fairfax stands on a bench, his musket held upright beside him, very excited.

DANIEL: We row out tonight and take it back! A bold stroke! A daring raid!

**Panel 2.** Josiah the farmer takes the musket gently from Daniel's hands and leans it against the wall, without a word. Martha folds her arms.

MARTHA: No raids, Daniel. We are declaring that we can govern ourselves. Not that we can steal from ships.

REVEREND MOTT: And we are not starting our freedom with a fight in the dark.

**Panel 3.** Daniel sits down, deflated.

DANIEL: Then what? We can't ring a bell with no clapper.

HAL: I can make a new clapper. But he's put his seal on my iron stock too. Every bar in the forge.

**Panel 4.** Amimi, who has been listening quietly at the back with Wapimin, steps forward. The room falls quiet for her.

AMIMI: He took the bell's tongue. He did not take yours. A people's voice is not in a piece of iron.

**Panel 5.** Close on Amimi, a glint in her eye.

AMIMI: But, since you want a piece of iron anyway... how much do you need, Hal?

HAL: (blinking) About as much as a good-sized anvil's horn. Old iron will do. Any old iron.

## PAGE 5

**Panel 1.** Martha nods to Obadiah. He picks up his brass hand bell and goes to the door.

MARTHA: Obadiah. Cry it.

**Panel 2.** Obadiah on the town hall steps, swinging his hand bell, at full volume, the words in huge letters.

OBADIAH: OYEZ! OYEZ! ANY OLD IRON TO THE FORGE! BY ORDER OF NOBODY! BRING IT BEFORE SUNSET!

SFX: CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!

**Panel 3.** Wide panel. A stream of townsfolk down the lanes towards the forge, carrying iron: a broken plough share on Josiah's shoulder, horseshoes on a string, an old door hinge, a rusty cooking pot, Ned's spare anchor ring, a pair of fire tongs, nails in a bucket, a child with one bent fork.

**Panel 4.** At the forge, the pile of old iron grows. Wapimin arrives last and sets down a battered iron kettle with a hole in the bottom.

WAPIMIN: My grandfather got this kettle from the first people of Brightwater, the year they came. The first trade between our towns. It has a hole now. It can still help.

**Panel 5.** Hal looks at the old kettle and then at Wapimin, moved.

HAL: Then it goes right in the middle.

**Panel 6.** Small panel. Martha, meanwhile, gathers her basket and turns to leave.

MARTHA: And while you make the tongue, I have a flag to finish.

## PAGE 6

**Panel 1.** Night. The forge. Hal hammers glowing iron on the anvil, sparks flying in flat yellow stars; townspeople take turns at the bellows. Josiah pumps steadily; the Reverend, sleeves rolled up, pumps less steadily.

SFX: KLANG! KLANG! KLANG!

**Panel 2.** Martha's weaving room, candlelit. Martha and Amimi sit on either side of a long piece of cloth: wide stripes of indigo blue and clean white linen. Amimi sews with quick, even stitches.

AMIMI: Blue and white only? No beasts, no crowns?

MARTHA: Blue for the bay, white for the sails. Nothing that belongs to anyone else.

**Panel 3.** Outside on the hilltop, Obadiah practises his proclamation to the empty night, alone, at enormous volume.

OBADIAH: BE IT KNOWN! BE IT KNOWN TO ALL! BE IT... (cough) BE IT KNOWN...!

**Panel 4.** Later, very late. Obadiah, red in the face, opens his mouth and only a faint squeak comes out. A cat on a fence looks at him pityingly.

OBADIAH: (tiny lettering) ...be it known...

**Panel 5.** Close on Obadiah clutching his throat in horror.

OBADIAH: (tiny lettering) Oh no.

## PAGE 7

**Panel 1.** Dawn. Wide. Thick grey-white fog has rolled in over the bay, flat pale bands with only the tops of the ship's masts and the belfry poking out. Ned Gully on the wharf, nodding knowingly.

NED: Told you. Sea fog. Won't lift before noon.

**Panel 2.** On the tidal mudflats outside the harbour, in the fog: Pettigrew, alone, in his huge wig, carrying his dispatch case and a measuring rod, peering about. He is ankle-deep in mud.

PETTIGREW: An inspection of the colony's... coastline. For the tax on... on mud. Most important.

**Panel 3.** He turns around. Everything is fog in all directions. The ship is gone. The town is gone.

PETTIGREW: Hello? Which way is the sea? Which way is the land? Which way is... anything?

**Panel 4.** Close on his feet: the water is creeping in over the mud around his shoes, the tide coming in.

SFX: glup... glup...

**Panel 5.** Pettigrew, now knee-deep and sinking, waving his dispatch case wildly.

PETTIGREW: HELP! HELP! CROWN'S COMMISSIONER IN THE MUD!

## PAGE 8

**Panel 1.** Out of the fog, a dugout canoe glides silently over the flooding flats. Wapimin, out early checking his fish weir, steers it with a long paddle. He looks at Pettigrew with mild interest.

WAPIMIN: Good morning. You are standing on my fish weir.

**Panel 2.** Wapimin reaches out a paddle; Pettigrew grabs it and is hauled, mud-covered and wigless, into the canoe. The wig floats away on the water like a small grey sheep.

SFX: SHLOOOORP!

**Panel 3.** In the canoe, fog all around. Pettigrew, dripping, stares at the blank greyness.

PETTIGREW: But how will we find the shore? I can't see a thing!

WAPIMIN: Listen.

**Panel 4.** Both men silent, listening. Faint through the fog, from the hill, comes a steady ringing rhythm.

SFX (small, distant): klang... klang... klang...

WAPIMIN: The blacksmith. He has been hammering all night. That way is the hill.

**Panel 5.** Wapimin paddles steadily towards the sound. Pettigrew, huddled, looks at him sideways.

PETTIGREW: Why are you helping me? I came here to take your neighbours' bell. To stop their treaty with you.

WAPIMIN: You were in the mud. The mud does not care whose king you serve.

## PAGE 9

**Panel 1.** The forge, morning. The door opens and Wapimin walks in, followed by Pettigrew: mud to the chest, wigless, his thin grey hair sticking up. Everyone stops and stares. Hal is holding up a long, finished iron clapper, still dark from the quench.

**Panel 2.** Pettigrew looks around at the forge: the heaps of scrap iron with their labels of string, the sleepy townsfolk at the bellows, the old kettle's handle still lying on the bench.

PETTIGREW: What is all this?

HAL: Everybody's old iron. Plough shares, horseshoes, hinges, a fork. And the first kettle traded between Brightwater and Shell Creek. All in one tongue for one bell.

**Panel 3.** Martha hands Pettigrew a blanket and a cup of hot cider. He sits down heavily on an upturned bucket.

MARTHA: You'd better sit, Commissioner. You look like a man who has had a very long morning.

**Panel 4.** Pettigrew, holding the cup, slowly opens his dispatch case and takes out his orders, which are a damp, muddy mess. He reads them again, squinting.

PETTIGREW: "To collect the Crown's tax... and to ensure the peace and good order of the colony."

**Panel 5.** Close on Pettigrew, thinking hard, looking from the paper to Wapimin, to the townsfolk, to the clapper.

PETTIGREW: (slowly) A colony whose neighbours fish its officials out of the mud. A town where everyone gives their pots to make one bell. I have never seen better peace and good order in my life.

## PAGE 10

**Panel 1.** Late afternoon. The fog has lifted. Wide. On the hilltop, the whole colony and many people of Shell Creek gather before the town hall. Hal, on a ladder in the belfry, hangs the new clapper in the bell. Pettigrew, cleaned up, in a borrowed brown coat, stands at the side, holding the old confiscated clapper, which he has had brought back from the ship.

PETTIGREW: You may have the old one back.

HAL: Keep it, Commissioner. Take it home with you. This one has all of us in it.

**Panel 2.** Obadiah climbs the steps to read the declaration. He opens his mouth. Only a squeak comes out. The crowd groans.

OBADIAH: (tiny lettering) Be it known...

**Panel 3.** Martha steps up beside him, takes one end of the document, and reads in a clear, strong voice. Then Josiah joins her. Then the Reverend. Then the whole crowd, speaking together.

MARTHA AND CROWD: "...that the people of Brightwater will govern themselves, by their own choosing, in peace with all their neighbours..."

**Panel 4.** Amimi and Martha stand face to face on the steps. Amimi holds out a broad wampum belt of white and purple shell beads, its pattern showing two figures holding hands. Martha holds out the treaty paper, already signed with a row of names and marks.

AMIMI: Two towns. One path between them. We both keep it clear.

MARTHA: We both keep it clear.

**Panel 5.** Close on their four hands holding the belt and the paper together.

## PAGE 11

**Panel 1.** Splash panel, most of the page: THE COVER MOMENT. Late afternoon light on the hilltop. In the open belfry, the great bronze bell swings and rings. Obadiah on the town hall steps raises his brass hand bell, mouth wide open. Daniel Fairfax hauls on the halyard as the new flag of plain blue and white stripes climbs the tall pole and catches the wind. Hats fly into the air. The crowd of every trade fills the foreground: Josiah with his pitchfork, Hal in his leather apron with his hammer, Martha with her basket, the Reverend in black, Ned with his net, a girl in a straw hat, a militiaman with his musket shouldered, and Wapimin and Amimi at the left, smiling. Below the hill: red roofs, the harbour full of ships, fields and forest to the distant mountains.

SFX (the big bell): DONNNNG! DONNNNG! DONNNNG!

SFX (hand bell): CLANG-A-LANG!

**Panel 2.** Close on Obadiah. With the first great stroke of the bell, his voice has come back all at once, enormous.

OBADIAH: BE IT KNOWN! BRIGHTWATER IS FREE! AND FRIENDS WITH SHELL CREEK! AND I CAN TALK AGAIN!

**Panel 3.** Small panel. Wapimin, hands over his ears, grins at Amimi.

WAPIMIN: We should have kept the tax on bells.

AMIMI: We should put a tax on criers.

**Panel 4.** Small panel. Pettigrew at the edge of the crowd, startled, catches a flying tricorn. After a moment, he throws it back up in the air himself.

CAPTION: Every bell the colony rang that year rang for liberty. But none rang louder than the first.

## PAGE 12

**Panel 1.** The wharf at evening. Pettigrew, ready to board his longboat, holds a sealed letter. Martha, Hal, Wapimin and Amimi see him off.

MARTHA: Our letter to the Crown. We will trade with them gladly, as friends and neighbours. But we will choose our own laws.

PETTIGREW: I shall deliver it. I may even suggest they read it.

**Panel 2.** Pettigrew turns to Wapimin and holds out his hand, a little awkwardly. Wapimin shakes it.

PETTIGREW: Thank you. For the canoe. And for the listening.

WAPIMIN: Next time you inspect the mud, bring a guide.

**Panel 3.** Amimi hands him a small bundle wrapped in cloth. He opens it: his grey wig, retrieved from the bay, washed and dried, rather curlier than before.

AMIMI: The children found it on the weir. They wanted to keep it for a sheep, but I said no.

PETTIGREW: (holding it up, moved, ridiculous) Madam... you are very kind.

**Panel 4.** The longboat rows out to the ship in the sunset. Pettigrew sits in the stern with the old clapper across his knees, waving.

**Panel 5.** Final panel, large and wide, the bottom half of the page. Night has fallen over Brightwater. The new flag hangs still on its pole against a sky full of stars. Lamps glow in the windows of the red-roofed town below and in the houses of Shell Creek along the shore; the Crown's ship is a small shape sailing out of the bay. In the open belfry, the bell rests silent. On the town hall steps, Obadiah Bellwether sits fast asleep, propped against a post, hand bell in his lap, tricorn over his eyes. Amimi and Martha sit beside him, sharing the last of the cider, looking out over the bay.

OBADIAH: (small balloon, in his sleep) ...be it known...

MARTHA: (small balloon) It's known, Obadiah. It's known.

CAPTION: THE END.
