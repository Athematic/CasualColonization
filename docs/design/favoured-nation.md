# Design note: Favoured Nation

Status: **agreed** (October 2026). "Random" below always means drawn from the game seed, so the same seed gives the same values. These values are hidden from players.

## Goal
Peaceful nations that trade with each other can become **Favoured Nations**. They then get access to each other's home ports, so the economy rewards cooperation, which the original game never did. This is the first step towards the cooperation overhaul.

## What we have agreed
- **How it is unlocked:** years of peace plus a number of trade routes between the two nations. Once both are reached, either side can propose it.
- **Agreement:** both sides must agree. A computer player can turn it down.
- **Effect:**
  - Computer players never declare war on a nation they favour.
  - Favoured nations can trade, and buy ships (but not warships), in each other's home ports.
- **Prices and taxes:** each port has its own market, with prices driven by supply and demand in the same way as the player's port today. The **port nation's tax rate applies**, so every nation needs its own taxes, not just people.
- **Cancelling:** either side can cancel. Both suffer: relations turn hostile, the trade routes between them are cancelled, and so on.
- **Triangle trading:** with more than one European port, the smartest computer players buy where goods are cheap and sell where they are dear, when the prices allow it.

## Engine changes this needs
1. **A market per nation.** Prices currently live on the player. Every European nation gets a market. A colony's goods can be sold in its own home port or in a favoured port. A ship has to sail to a particular port, so "Europe" becomes a choice of port when a ship leaves for Europe.
2. **Taxes for every nation.** Computer nations get tax raises from their Crown and decide whether to accept or boycott, as people do.
3. **Trade routes between nations.** A trade route can stop at a foreign colony once that nation agrees to trade there (a trade agreement). Goods sold there pay that colony's prices. Until there are colony markets, a simple valuation such as a share of the port nation's price would do.
4. **Relations.** Track for each pair of nations: years of peace (turns since the last war), the number of active trade routes between them, trade volume, attitude, and favoured status.
5. **Computer players.** They need rules for proposing and accepting Favoured Nation, the "never declare war" rule, and port choice and triangle-trade planning (Creative level).
6. **Saving.** This is a breaking change to the state. `SAVE_VERSION` goes to 3 and **older saves are not converted**: loading one shows a clear message that saves from before 0.4 can't be loaded. This was agreed, since there is a single player so far. Migration code returns once others play.

## Decided details

### 1. Unlocking
- **Years of peace:** 10 years, plus or minus a random 0–2 years, so 8–12 consecutive years since the two nations were last at war.
- **Trade routes:** a random 2–4 active trade routes between the two nations (routes that stop at the other nation's colonies).
- Both thresholds are drawn **for each pair of nations** when the game starts, from the game seed. They stay hidden. The diplomacy screen shows progress only roughly, for example "relations are warming".

### 2. Cancelling
Either side can cancel at any time. Starting values, to be tuned with tournaments:
- **Trade routes between the two nations** are all cancelled at once.
- **Seizure:** all goods within one tile of the other nation's colonies are seized by that nation. This covers goods carried by ships and wagon trains in or next to one of its colonies.
- **Waiting period:** no new trade routes between the two for 10 years, plus or minus a random 0–3 years. Favoured Nation needs a new run of years of peace and routes after that.
- **Hostility:** for 15 years, plus or minus a random 0–5 years, computer players on either side treat the other as a likely target. They weigh attacking over trading, are more likely to declare war, and refuse new agreements. A person's nation gets the same treatment from computer players.
- Both sides suffer, whoever cancels. The exact lengths are random and hidden, so players can't time around them.

### 3. Independence
- **First release:** no special rule. When a nation declares independence, it loses its home port, and with it the Favoured Nation status. This lapse does not count as a cancellation, so no penalties apply (confirmed).
- **Later:** the outcome can depend on the history between the two nations. For example, France might keep its relationship with English colonies that rebel.

### 4. People in a hot-seat game
- Two nations played by people can become favoured nations **at any time**, without the thresholds, if both people agree.
- Either person can revoke it at any time. Goods are still seized when it is revoked. The waiting period and computer hostility do not apply: between two people they can't be enforced, so they can agree again straight away.

## Implementation (step 1, October 2026)

Engine and people's interface are built; the computer players' strategic use came in step 2 (below). Code: `packages/engine/src/relations.ts`, with the port rules in `europe.ts` and foreign stops in `routes.ts`; tests in `packages/engine/test/favoured.test.ts`. Decisions taken while building it, beyond the agreed details above:

- **Seeded values.** The hidden values (years of peace 8–12, routes 2–4, waiting 7–13 years, hostility 10–20 years, all whole years) come from a small dedicated random stream seeded by the game seed, the pair of nations and a purpose (`thresholds`, or `cancel N` for the Nth cancellation). They never draw from `GameState.rng`, so adding the feature did not change any other random outcome. The thresholds are drawn for every pair when the game starts and stored in `GameState.relations` (keyed `a|b`, alphabetical).
- **Years.** Years of peace and the waiting and hostility periods are counted in game years (two turns a year after 1600). A run of peace starts at the game start, at a peace treaty, or at a cancellation (so Favoured Nation needs a new run, as agreed).
- **Attitude** is not a separate number: `relationsMood` derives it from war, favoured status, hostility, the waiting period and the share of the years of peace reached ("Relations are cool / warming / friendly", "A long friendship", "Trust has to be rebuilt", "Relations are hostile"). `tradeTies` describes the routes the same way. The screen never shows the thresholds.
- **Trade volume** is the gold that has changed hands at each other's colonies, both ways, over the game.
- **Active trade routes between two nations** are routes of either nation with at least one stop at the other's colony and at least one ship or wagon train assigned.
- **Trade agreements.** A step before Favoured Nation: needed for foreign stops, and for Favoured Nation (except between two people, where Favoured Nation brings the agreement with it). Computer players accept when at peace, not hostile and not in a waiting period. Ending a plain agreement (`cancelTrade`) drops the foreign stops and has no other consequence; a favoured pair must cancel Favoured Nation instead, which ends both. One proposal per pair can be pending; a person answers it in the turn-start briefing or in Foreign Affairs, and conditions are checked again on acceptance.
- **Foreign stops.** A unit cannot enter a foreign colony, so it serves the stop from a tile next to it: ships from the water next to a coastal colony, wagon trains from the land. Unloaded goods fill the warehouse up to its capacity (or the rule's amount), paid at the owner's home-market sell price with no tax, as far as the owner's treasury allows; when it cannot pay at all the stop is skipped and reported. Loading buys only the surplus above 50 units of each good (or the rule's amount if higher) at the owner's buy price. Neither home market moves. A computer player's colonies take part passively; they do not yet build routes to foreign colonies.
- **Computer players and Favoured Nation.** (Step 1; step 2 below adds what they do on their own.) They accept when at peace, not hostile, and the relationship has paid: at least 300 gold traded between the two, or a peace twice as long as the threshold. One that has picked a colony of the proposer as its war target refuses. They never declare war on, or attack (not even with privateers), a nation they favour, and their privateers also leave trade partners' ships alone.
- **Hostility in war decisions.** A computer player hostile to a nation weighs its colonies 1.6 times as attractive targets, looks further for them (14 tiles instead of 10), and shows at least a small aggression (0.3) towards it even at the peaceful Competent level.
- **Partner ports.** A ship's port is part of its location (`{ kind: 'europe', port }`, and on the way `{ kind: 'sailing', port }`; missing means its own). In a partner's port the market, prices and tax rate are the partner's; sales there never trigger a tax raise from either Crown; neither the partner's boycotts nor the visitor's own apply (a boycott is between a nation's colonies and its own Crown). Treasure landed there pays the partner's tax. Only unarmed ships (caravel, merchantman, galleon) can be bought, at the partner's prices, and only while one of your ships is in that port; a ship bought there starts there. Colonists are recruited, trained, equipped and go ashore only in the home port, and trade routes call only at the home port. When the status ends (cancelled, war, or the partner's independence) ships in, or sailing to, the other's port go to their own.
- **War.** Declaring war ends a trade agreement; on a Favoured Nation it counts as a cancellation by the nation that declares war, with all the consequences.
- **Independence.** The rebel's ships in partners' ports are lost with everything else in Europe; partners' ships in the rebel's port go home. Trade agreements stay.
- **Hot seat.** Between two people no thresholds, waiting period or hostility apply, and agreements can be made again straight away; goods are still seized on revoking.
- **Saves.** `SAVE_VERSION` is 3; `migrate` refuses older saves with a `SaveVersionError` ("Saves from before version 0.4 can't be loaded."), which the title screen, the New game screen and file import show.
- **Telemetry** for the feature audit: `diplomacy:trade agreement made`, `diplomacy:favoured nation made`, `diplomacy:favoured nation cancelled`, `diplomacy:goods seized`, `trade:foreign stop sales` and `purchases` (units), `europe:partner port sales` and `purchases` (units), `europe:partner port ship bought`, and the new commands.

## Computer players (step 2, October 2026)

The Competent, Combative and Creative levels now use all of the above; Naive only answers proposals and accepts every tax raise. Code: `packages/engine/src/ai/foreign.ts` (proposals, taxes, ports, triangle trading, cancelling), the routes in `ai/logistics.ts`, hooks in `ai/advanced.ts` and `ai/fleet.ts`; tests in `packages/engine/test/favoured-ai.test.ts`. Every random decision draws from the seeded per-pair stream (`pairRandom` in `relations.ts`), never from `GameState.rng`. The numbers are constants at the top of `ai/foreign.ts`, to be tuned with tournaments.

- **When they think about it.** Every 5 turns, each nation on a different turn of the cycle. A tax raise is answered at the start of the nation's next turn.
- **Trade agreements.** Proposed to every European nation at peace with it, not hostile, not in a waiting period, not its war target, that is a *trade neighbour*: a colony of each within 8 tiles, both on the coast, or joined by land a wagon train crosses in 10 steps or fewer.
- **Favoured Nation.** Proposed once `favouredProblem` allows it. Like a person, the computer player learns only whether relations are close enough, never the hidden numbers. It must also pay: for a peaceful nation it always does (a second port, sales that bring no tax raise, a partner that will not attack); a nation bent on war (aggression 0.5 or more: Combative, or Creative set on war) does not offer it to a nation with a colony near its own that its spare army could beat.
- **No spam.** After a proposal that did not lead to an agreement (turned down by a computer, or sent to a person, who answers at the start of their turn), no new proposal of that kind to that nation for 10 to 20 turns, seeded. Nothing is proposed while hostile, in a waiting period, or with `GameConfig.noFavoured` (the tournament's `--no-favoured`).
- **Routes to partners' colonies** (`ai/logistics.ts`). The value of selling a good at a partner's colony is its owner's home price (untaxed, as the rule says) against what the good brings when shipped home: the home price after the nation's own tax, or nothing when the good is boycotted at home. A route sells only what the partner's colony needs: goods its staffed workshops use and it does not make enough of (filling the warehouse to its capacity less 20), or, to a partner with 4,000 gold or more, any good the colony does not make (to 100). It never sells goods it bought elsewhere, so two partners' routes cannot pass the same goods back and forth. It buys the colony's surplus above 50 when home pays at least 1 gold more per unit after tax. A stop must gain at least 1 gold per unit, or, while the nation is building ties with that partner (an agreement but not yet Favoured Nation, thresholds not reached, fewer than 4 routes between them), at least break even. The first tournaments, without these limits, showed partners' routes trading the same goods back and forth and draining each other's treasuries, so every level lost points.
  - Ship routes from the nation's ports to Europe get a stop at a partner's coastal colony within 6 tiles of one of their ports, just before Europe; what is not sold there goes on to Europe.
  - From turn 80, an idle wagon train with no inland colony to serve takes a route between one of the nation's colonies and a partner's colony within reach. Before that it trades with the natives, as before: a first version that took routes to partners from the start changed the early game of nations that never became Favoured Nations, for the worse.
  - From turn 80, with 5 colonies or more, a nation building ties with a partner and with no wagon train route to it asks its colony within 6 tiles of the partner with the most hammers (3 or more) to build a wagon train.
  - Routes to partners are reviewed with the others every 10 turns, and dropped when they no longer pay. They count towards the routes Favoured Nation needs.
- **Port choice.** A ship that sails for Europe compares what its cargo fetches in each port it may use: the price after that port's tax, nothing for goods boycotted at home (a partner's port ignores the boycott). All ports are reached from the same high seas in the same number of turns, since the engine has one Atlantic crossing, so distance does not count. What counts is that colonists board only at home: a partner's port must beat home by 25 gold plus 250 gold for each colonist the trip home could bring (those on the docks and up to four recruits the treasury pays for, at most one per hold). With 100 gold per colonist, Creative went abroad too often, left gold idle and recruited less, and lost about 13 points a game. While a ship is in a partner's port, up to two colonists may wait on the home docks for the next ship home. Trade routes still call only at the home port, as the rules say.
- **In a partner's port** the computer sells its cargo, buys tools, muskets and horses at that port's prices, buys a merchant ship there when it is cheaper than at home (prices are the same for every computer nation today, so it does not happen in tournaments), and sails back. Colonists, experts and warships are bought at home and wait on the docks for a ship there.
- **Triangle trading (Creative only).** With holds to spare in a port, a Creative nation buys a good that another port it may use (home, or a partner's) pays clearly more for after tax: at least 1 gold and 10% of the buying price per unit, and at least 100 gold per hold. Such gaps are rare: the markets drift back to their starting prices, and the nations mostly sell raw goods, which sit near their lowest price everywhere. At most two holds and half the gold above the reserve. The goods stay aboard until the ship is in that port (port choice takes it there, as their value is counted), or for 30 turns, after which they are sold wherever the ship is. A ship carrying such goods is not put on a route.
- **Taxes.** A raise on a good the nation buys (tools, muskets, horses, trade goods), or on food or lumber, is always accepted. For an export it weighs the two costs as shares of its export income (the exports are what its colonies hold and make of each good, at home prices): accepting costs the raise on everything else it sells; refusing costs the good's sales, or half of them when a Favoured Nation partner's port pays at least 70% of the home price after the new tax (free ships can sell it there). It refuses, and the good is boycotted, when refusing costs less than 70% of accepting; without a partner's port, only a raise of 3 points or more on a good under 10% of its exports. A first version refused whenever a good was under 10% of exports (or 20% with a partner's port); tournaments showed it boycotting too much, so the rule became the break-even one. A boycotted good is no longer loaded for Europe by routes (any aboard goes back to a warehouse); free ships still carry it to a partner's port. The arrears are paid when no partner's port takes the good, the nation has the arrears plus 1,000 gold above its reserve, and the good has become a real export (10% or more) or the colonies hold twice the arrears' worth of it.
- **Cancelling** is rare and needs a reason:
  - the partner is at war with a tribe the nation runs a mission among (a 25% chance at each review, seeded); or
  - the nation wants war (aggression 1: Combative, or Creative set on war), no other nation offers a target it would attack, and a partner's colony is a clearly good target (half as good again as the level at which it declares war). It cancels first, and declares war at the earliest two turns later, after the cancellation's penalties (routes ended, goods seized, waiting and hostility) apply, so the war itself is not a cancellation. With the hostility that follows, the former partner's colonies weigh 1.6 times as attractive. If no war follows within 20 turns, the plan lapses.
- **Never war on a Favoured Nation.** The war-target search leaves Favoured Nations out (`bestWarTarget` in `ai/foreign.ts`, used by `advanced.ts`), `startWar` refuses it for computer players, and privateers leave trade partners' ships alone.
- **Telemetry** added for the audit: `diplomacy:proposal turned down`, `diplomacy:war on a trade partner`, `diplomacy:war on a Favoured Nation`, `diplomacy:favoured nation cancelled over natives` and `for war`, `europe:sailed to a partner port`, `route:foreign route created`, `trade:triangle goods bought` (units) and `trade:triangle trade` (completed sales). The arena counts relations and trade in every game and prints them per level and per game (see [AI_FEATURE_AUDIT.md](../AI_FEATURE_AUDIT.md)).

## How we measure it
Run tournaments with and without the feature. They should show more trade, fewer wars between favoured nations, and no drop in score. `npm run tournament -- --no-favoured` gives the run without; the results of step 2 are in the Favoured Nation section of [AI_FEATURE_AUDIT.md](../AI_FEATURE_AUDIT.md): about five trade agreements and two Favoured Nation pairs a game, no war on a Favoured Nation, Combative and Competent at least as strong, Creative about 14 points lower (two standard errors).
