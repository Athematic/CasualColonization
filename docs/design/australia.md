# Design note: Australia, 1788–1901

Status: **decided** (19 November 2026): Simon agreed every recommendation in section 6 (A1–A23 as recommended); consultation waits until the scenario is being readied for release. Nothing is built. This note replaces the short idea note and keeps its decisions (section 2). It is the next scenario after Beta 4, built **on hexes from the start** (Simon, 11 November 2026). It builds on the [scenario framework](scenario-framework.md) and [scenarios.md](scenarios.md), on Beta 1, [cooperation.md](cooperation.md) (alliances, companies, concord, confederation), on Beta 2, [native-partners.md](native-partners.md) (treaties with peoples), on Beta 3, [native-nations.md](native-nations.md) (peoples as players, councils, arrivals, land health, hard years, the land deal, the peoples' victories) and on Beta 4, `docs/design/hex-maps.md` (hex grids, work rings as scenario data, stored maps on hexes; on the branch `claude/beta4-hex-maps` until Beta 4 is merged). **The history behind it** (the peoples and their Country, the colonies, convicts, the economy, droughts and fires, the council members, the events and the sources, with the items still to be checked and where community consultation is needed) is in its own file for Simon's review: [research/australia.md](../research/australia.md). This note keeps the game's decisions and numbers.

## Care with the subject

This section comes first and applies to every decision, table and word below.

The colonisation of Australia includes frontier wars, massacres, land taken under the "terra nullius" doctrine, and the Stolen Generations. The game must not present the land as empty or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples as obstacles. Their nations, languages and Country are shown with respect and based on reputable sources (for example, the AIATSIS map of Indigenous Australia). The cooperation focus helps here, but it needs care in the design and in the text, not only in the rules. In practice:

- **The land is never empty.** Every land tile is some people's Country from the first turn (A6). No European colony stands on land nobody holds: land is leased, bought or taken, and taking is named as taking.
- **The peoples' own names**, as communities use them today where that is clear; where a name is disputed (for example "Eora" for the peoples of the Sydney coast), the research file says so and the name is marked for consultation. Every people listed is a nation of its own, never "the Aborigines".
- **Nothing sacred as a mechanic:** no ceremonies, Dreaming stories, songlines, sacred sites or restricted knowledge as actions, buildings, map features or effects. Cultural burning, fish and eel traps, seed grinding, trade and seasonal movement are land work and are modelled as land work; their spiritual side is not modelled or named.
- **No violence played for effect.** No massacre, punitive expedition, "dispersal", Native Police, poisoning or abduction is an action, unit, event or effect. Wars are Beta 3's wars between players. The game's history text says plainly that frontier wars and massacres happened (research, section 4), without making them a mechanic.
- **The Stolen Generations, missions and "protection" laws are not modelled.** Child removal is mostly after 1901 and is never a mechanic, event or option. The research file records it as history the game does not play.
- **No colonial council member has an effect against the peoples** (A19). Aboriginal leaders sit in the peoples' council, not as helpers in the colonists' council.
- **Deceased persons:** many communities avoid the names and images of people who have died. The scenario's New game card and the council hall show the usual notice ("Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people are advised that this game contains names of people who have died"), and no member has a portrait: emblems only (Beta 3's T9 rule).
- **Consultation before release.** The drafted peoples, names, Country boundaries, council members and texts are reviewed with the communities and bodies named in the research file (section 15) before the scenario is offered on the New game screen. Until then it is registered hidden, as the North-east was.

## 1. Goal

A new setting on the same rules: the colonisation of Australia from the First Fleet (January 1788) to Federation (1 January 1901). Different land, peoples, goods, history and ending:
- **Distance, water and fire** instead of fighting over land: a sea route of eight months, a dry interior, droughts that last years, and bushfires.
- **Convicts** as the main source of colonists at first: the convict → ticket of leave → free path, as a mechanic shared with the Americas.
- **Wool, wheat, whaling and gold:** sheep stations that use very large areas of land, and the gold rush of 1851 as a turning point.
- **Aboriginal nations and Torres Strait Islanders** as Beta 3 people players and as peoples nobody plays, on their **Country**, with seasonal movement, cultural burning and trade networks, and their own ways to win.
- **A peaceful ending:** self-government and **Federation by negotiation** (conferences, conventions, referendums), built on Beta 1's alliances and confederation.

**Non-goals:**
- New Zealand (Aotearoa). It needs its own research and care (the Treaty of Waitangi, the iwi as nations) and doubles the map's sea; a later scenario if wanted (A2).
- Rebalancing the Americas, the Caribbean or the North-east. They stay bit-identical; the shared convict mechanic is off there unless switched on (A11).
- Railroads, telegraph and steamships as units (they are events or modifiers here; railroads do not exist in the engine).
- Network play, the interface redesign, locally generated hex terrain sprites.

## 2. What exists today (Beta 3, Beta 4 in progress)

**Decided in the idea note** (kept):
- **Rivals:** both. *Real rivals* (Britain, with others only exploring) and *alternative history* (a French Western Australia, a Dutch New Holland and so on) are setup options, as in every scenario.
- **Map:** both. A dedicated map of Australia, or a generated map in an Australian style. (A2 makes the dedicated map the default and the generator a later step.)
- **Convict transport:** a mechanic in its own right, shared with the Americas, where British colonies get a limited late-game version ([scenarios.md](scenarios.md#convict-transport-a-shared-mechanic)).
- **Placement:** after the cooperation betas (done: Beta 1–3), after Beta 4, on hexes (Simon, 11 November 2026).

**What the engine already has that Australia uses** (sections 2 and 7–19 of [native-nations.md](native-nations.md); [SCENARIOS.md](../SCENARIOS.md)):
- **Scenario data** for everything a setting needs: calendar eras of 1, 2 or 4 turns a year with named seasons and head starts; nation sets (real and alternative) with bonuses, ports and colony names; `NationEntry.arrives` (nations arriving by year, with their own entry points, `StoredMapEntry.entries`); goods and terrain lists; per-type catalogue selections (`nationTypes.european`, `nationTypes.people`); `Scenario.councils`; events by registered set; terms; mechanics switches; victory kinds with params; `ai` overrides including `dates` and `stretch`.
- **Peoples as players** (`kind: 'people'`, towns as colonies, no Europe), births from food, stores and hard years (`mechanics.hardYears`, drawn per region and year from the seed), land health per region (`mechanics.landHealth`, with renewal per region from the stored map), epidemics (`mechanics.epidemics`, the same rules for both sides), knowledge of the land (`knowsLand`), Beta 2's four treaty kinds both ways and between peoples, the `league` treaty, the land deal (offer, lease, take; `mechanics.landSales` with `pull`, trust grown in peace, levels), meetings in person (`mapKnowledge`), and the peoples' victories (`continuity`, `confederacy`, `good-life`, `military`) judged beside the Europeans' (`VICTORY_ORDER`, `gameOver.ways`), with per-people params and score weights.
- **Peoples nobody plays** keep Beta 2's settlements, braves, tension, treaties and land buying (`buyLand`, `takeLand`); their land is `landRadius` around settlements (`nativeLandOwner`).
- **Europeans:** Beta 1's alliances (with "old friends"), chartered companies (`kind: 'company'`, named by the scenario), shared bells, `concord`, and `confederation` (allies declaring independence together); Beta 2's `prosperity`; the Crown, taxes, the Royal Expeditionary Force and the War of Independence behind switches.
- **Units:** the petty criminal and the indentured servant (skill −2 and −1) and their promotion by schooling, the Americas' path the convict mechanic builds on.
- **Stored maps** with a regions grid (`StoredMap.regions`) and rivers as waypoints; a **preview map of Australia** on squares (`maps/australia.ts`, 81 × 75 tiles, about 0.5° a tile, with placeholder American peoples) played with the Americas' rules. Beta 4 reads it on hexes with one river lost (the Murrumbidgee), and the preview's peoples are explicitly placeholders.
- **Hex grids** (Beta 4 steps 1–3, 5, 6 built): `Scenario.map.grid: 'hex'`, `map.hex.orientation`, rivers and roads through tile centres, `map.workArea` with rings gated by `size` or `building` (at most two rings), the schematic style, Ligne claire and the placeholder sprite pack on hexes. **Not built yet:** step 4 (the conversion tool, Vinland, retuning the computer players on hexes, the Mac speed gate).

**What is missing** (the new work, sized in section 5): a hex map of Australia with Country and regions; the seasonal lifeway; Country as land held from the start; convict transport; self-government; the Federation process and victory; sheep stations; drought and fire; the gold rush; whaling and sealing; new goods, terrains, units and buildings; two councils; the events; computer players for all of it.

## 3. Proposal in brief

1. **A stored hex map of Australia** (pointy-top, about 50 km a hex, roughly 94 × 96 hexes, about 3,600 land hexes, as large as the North-east): the mainland, Tasmania, the Torres Strait islands, and the south coast of New Guinea as land closed to colonies; no New Zealand. Drawn directly on hexes from Natural Earth with a tool in `tools/maps/australia/`, as the North-east was on squares. Three layers: terrain and rivers (the Murray–Darling and about thirty others), **regions** for land health, drought and fire (about fifty, from the IBRA bioregions), and **Country** (each land hex's people, from the AIATSIS map, simplified and marked for review).
2. **Two turns a year**, summer and winter (in the north, the wet and the dry), from summer 1788 to summer 1901: 227 turns. Head starts to 1820, 1850 and 1880.
3. **Who plays the colonists' side:** in *real history* the **colonies themselves** are the players, each arriving in its year under one Crown (New South Wales 1788, Van Diemen's Land 1803, Moreton Bay (Queensland) 1824, Western Australia 1829, Port Phillip (Victoria) 1835, South Australia 1836), with French explorers as events; in *alternative history*, rival nations (Britain, France, the Netherlands, Portugal) with their own Crowns. Chartered companies from history (the Australian Agricultural Company, the Van Diemen's Land Company, the South Australian Company).
4. **Country:** every land hex belongs to a people from the start. Beta 3's land deal (lease, buy, take) works on all Country, for peoples nobody plays too. No unclaimed land.
5. **The seasonal lifeway:** a people's **camps** move between their seasonal places on Country each turn; Torres Strait Islanders and a few others live in **villages** with gardens (today's fixed lifeway). Seasonal movement rests the land, which land health rewards.
6. **Six playable peoples** (recommended): the peoples of the Sydney coast (name for consultation), the Wiradjuri, the Kulin nations, the Noongar, the Yolŋu and the Torres Strait Islanders; about forty-five more on the map as peoples nobody plays.
7. **Land health, drought and fire:** regions' health is drawn on by hunting and by **grazing** (sheep and cattle); **droughts** last one to three years and strike by aridity, with the Federation Drought (1895–1902) as a historical event; **cultural burning** by a people on its Country raises the land's recovery and lowers the risk of **bushfires**, which grows where burning has stopped.
8. **Convict transport** (shared): convicts arrive with the Crown's transports at the penal colonies while transportation lasts; a convict becomes a **ticket-of-leave** holder after years of service and then **free**; convicts are free labour but slow a colony's liberty; a colony may ask for more or petition to end them. The Americas get the late version (British colonies, 1718–1775) as a setup option, off by default.
9. **The economy:** **wool** from **sheep stations** (a station is a small colony that grazes two rings of land at once), **wheat**, **timber** (red cedar), **whale oil** and **sealskins** from the sea, **gold** from gold fields found from 1851 (with a rush of immigrants and diggers, and the miners' licence that led to Eureka), copper (ore) in South Australia, water works against drought.
10. **Self-government, then Federation.** In real history there is no War of Independence: a colony with enough liberty **petitions for self-government** (the Crown grants it, no war); self-governing colonies may form a **Federal Council**, call a **convention** and hold **referendums**; when enough colonies vote yes, the **Commonwealth is proclaimed** and its members win together. It builds on Beta 1's treaty record, alliances and confederation. In alternative history, independence by war stays possible beside Federation.
11. **Two councils:** the colonies' **Legislative Council** (governors, reformers, explorers, pastoralists, gold-rush and Federation figures; no effect against the peoples) and the peoples' council (roles and collective figures, a few named leaders with Australian Dictionary of Biography entries, each offered only to their own people).
12. **The peoples win their own ways:** continuity ("still on Country"), confederacy, a good life with Country cared for, the military victory as in Beta 3, and, proposed, **recognition** (Country secured by treaty with every colony that borders it).
13. **Events:** the First Fleet, the Rum Rebellion, the crossing of the Blue Mountains, the explorers, the gold rush, Eureka Stockade, droughts, bushfires (Black Thursday 1851, Red Tuesday 1898), the French ships; written soberly.
14. **Computer players** for the new pressures (convict policy, stations and grazing restraint, drought, gold, self-government, Federation votes, the seasonal round, burning), with tournament targets.
