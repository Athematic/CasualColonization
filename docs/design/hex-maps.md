# Design note: Hex maps (Beta 4)

Status: **step 1 built** (see "Implementation (step 1)" at the end); **design, decisions mostly made** (11 November 2026): H1–H4, H6–H9, H11 and H17 are decided; the rest are recommendations for Simon to confirm. Simon agreed the outline of Beta 4 on 11 November 2026 ([ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md#beta-4-hex-maps)): Vinland is hex-only; Australia is built on hexes after Beta 4; no map editor unless it helps make the maps; no locally generated terrain sprites, a placeholder sprite pack instead; Ligne claire is tried on hexes. On the same day he decided four of this note's questions: the scenario chooses the hexes' orientation (H1), rivers run through tile centres (H3), movement keys follow what players of Civilization-like games expect (H6), and a colony's work area may grow to two rings, gated by scenario data, with today's single ring the default (H7). The rest is proposed. The note builds on the 0.10 grid work (`packages/engine/src/grid.ts`; [SHIPPED.md](../SHIPPED.md#010-faster-turns-done); [SCENARIOS.md](../SCENARIOS.md#the-map-grid)) and on Beta 3, [native-nations.md](native-nations.md), whose North-east map (T19) becomes Vinland's map and whose Norse proposal (T17) is built here.

## 1. Goal

A scenario can be played on **hexagons**. Every tile has six neighbours, all sharing an edge with it: no diagonal shortcuts, distances that look like distances, and coasts and rivers that read more naturally.
- **A grid per scenario:** `Scenario.map.grid` is `'square'` or `'hex'`, and a hex scenario chooses pointy-top rows or flat-top columns. Squares stay the default.
- **Rules on hexes:** six directions, a colony work area of one ring that a scenario may let grow to two, six movement keys, rivers and roads on six sides, the path search and the braves.
- **Drawn on hexes:** the schematic style first, then the sprite style with a placeholder pack, and a try of Ligne claire.
- **Proved on Vinland**, with the Norse, on Beta 3's North-east map turned into hexes, with computer players retuned by tournaments and the Mac speed gate run on it.

**Non-goals:**
- Converting the Americas or the Caribbean. They stay square in Beta 4; converting them is later, one scenario at a time, each with tournaments.
- A map editor, unless it helps make the Vinland and Australia maps (section 4, H20).
- Locally generated hex terrain sprites (Beta 5 or 6, on the Mac).
- The interface redesign (Later).
- A third work ring, and railroads, except railroads as an optional last step (they do not exist yet).
- Australia (after Beta 4, on hexes from the start).

## 2. What exists today (0.10 to Beta 3)

**The grid** (`packages/engine/src/grid.ts`, 0.10). One `Grid` interface: `directions` (clockwise from the north), `workRadius`, `distance`, `inBounds`, `step`, `directionTowards`, `neighbours`, `tilesWithin`, `ring`, `workArea`, and allocation-free forms for hot loops: `neighbourOffsets`, `withinOffsets`, `ringOffsets`, `workOffsets` (flat `[dx, dy, …]` arrays that take the tile, because on hexes in offset rows or columns they depend on its row or column). Only `SQUARE` exists (Chebyshev distance, eight directions `n … nw`). `GridKind` is `'square'`. `gridOf(s)` and `gridIn(scenario)` return the game's grid; `Scenario.map.grid` is optional and absent means squares. The map stays a rectangle of rows (`y * width + x`) on every grid. Orders are part of the contract, because the computer players take the first of equals. `test/grid.test.ts` checks `SQUARE` against the loops it replaced.

**Already behind the grid.** 95 calls to `gridOf`/`gridIn` in 27 engine files: movement (`describeMove`, `moveUnit`, `findPath`, `continueOrders`, road-to), combat, colonies and their sites, natives and braves, rumours, events, routes, rebellion, independence, reports, the unit index, the map generator and stored maps, and the computer players (`ai/common.ts`, `advanced.ts`, `creative.ts`, `naive.ts`, `foreign.ts`, `logistics.ts`, `natives.ts`, `roads.ts`, `royal.ts`). These need a hex `Grid`, not new code. The path search's heuristic is `grid.distance` (`movement.ts` `findPath`), so it follows the grid too. 19 places read the work area (`workRadius`, `workArea`, `workOffsets`) and assume it is fixed per grid; H7 makes it depend on the colony.

**Still square** (SHIPPED's 0.10 row and SCENARIOS.md name three: rivers' direction masks, the movement keys and the colony screen's 3 × 3 layout). The full audit, by area:

## 3. Proposal in brief

1. **Hex grids** beside `SQUARE` in `grid.ts`: `GridKind = 'square' | 'hex'`, with `Scenario.map.hex.orientation` `'pointy'` (offset rows, odd rows shifted half a tile east) or `'flat'` (offset columns, odd columns shifted half a tile south). Both are stored row by row. Distance and lines go through cube coordinates inside `grid.ts`; nothing outside sees them. Vinland uses pointy-top rows (H1).
2. **Rivers and roads through tile centres** with a six-bit mask for rivers, as today's eight-bit one; the rules unchanged (H3, H4).
3. **Directions from the grid everywhere:** `RIVER_DIRS` and the renderers' copies give way to `grid.directions`; the opposite bit is `(d + n/2) % n`.
4. **A colony's work area can grow, if the scenario says so:** `Scenario.map.workArea` lists at most two rings and what unlocks each (a size, a building); absent, a colony works today's single ring. On hexes ring 1 is six tiles and ring 2 twelve more. Vinland is proposed to use two rings, opening at size 5 (H7). The Americas and the Caribbean set nothing.
5. **Movement keys as in other games on hexes:** the numpad without the two directions the orientation lacks, clicking and go-to as today, letters optional (H6).
6. **Geometry in the render API per grid:** tile centres, outlines, `tileToScreen` and `screenToTile` for both orientations, one camera in world units. Every style and the web shell use them.
7. **A hidden test scenario on hexes** (`hex-test`): the Americas' rules on a generated hex map, for tests and tournaments before Vinland exists.
8. **Stored maps read as offset rows or columns,** with a check that lists what changes (straits that close, isthmuses that break, river steps that are no longer neighbours) and a review pass per map. Vinland gets a hex copy of Beta 3's North-east map.
9. **Squares bit-identical** through steps 1–3: the golden test and `bench --verify` stay unchanged, so the hex core can be built beside Beta 3.
10. **Tests on both grids:** the key rule tests run once per grid (squares, pointy hexes, flat hexes).
11. **Styles:** the schematic style on hexes (P1), the sprite style with a placeholder pack and a missing-ids check (P2), Ligne claire tried (P2).
12. **Vinland on hexes** with the Norse; computer players retuned with tournaments, the second ring's balance among them; the Mac speed gate on a hex scenario.

### H7 in full: a work area that grows (decided: scenario data, at most two rings)

**The scenario data** (decided: the gates are scenario data; the shape is proposed). `Scenario.map.workArea?: { rings: WorkRing[] }`, where each `WorkRing` gives its gate: `size?` (the colony's colonists, inside and out, at least this many) and `building?` (a building id the colony must have). When a ring gives both, both must hold; a ring with neither is always open.
- **Absent: today's single ring,** the engine default. Every scenario that sets nothing (the Americas, the Caribbean, every square game today) stays as it is and bit-identical.
- **At most two rings** in the main game: the scenario check rejects a third. Ring 1 is always open; a scenario with one ring listed is the same as none.
- The shape does not depend on the grid. Beta 4 builds and tunes it on hexes, where it is wanted; a square scenario could use it later (its ring 2 would add sixteen tiles), but none does in Beta 4.

**What opens ring 2 in Vinland.** Options: (a) the colony's size; (b) a building; (c) both; (d) liberty or culture, as Civilization's borders (not offered: neither fits the data above). **Proposed: (a), size 5**: `workArea: { rings: [{}, { size: 5 }] }`, so a colony of five or more works up to 18 tiles. Size is visible on the map and the colony screen, needs no new building or art, and rewards growing a colony, which is the point. A colony of five already has more workers than ring 1 has tiles once a few work in buildings, so ring 2 comes when it starts to matter. The threshold is tuned in step 4; 4 to 6 are the likely range. A building gate (b) or (c) is one field away if tournaments show colonies reaching five too easily.
- **Losing the gate:** a colony that falls below the size (or loses the building) loses ring 2 at the end of its turn; workers on ring 2 tiles move to the best open job, as when a worked tile is lost to a claim today.
- **The same rules on both rings:** land claims, buying or taking native land, leases (Beta 2), and one colony per worked tile. Colony spacing stays `distance < 2` (no colony next to another), so ring 2 often overlaps a neighbour's; a tile is worked by one colony at a time, as overlapping squares are today, and the computer players' `SHARED_TILE_VALUE` already prices that.
- **The engine:** the grid gives rings (`ringOffsets` already exists); `Grid.workRadius` and `workOffsets` stay ring 1, unchanged. A new `colonyWorkArea(s, colony)` (and an offsets form) gives the tiles a colony can work now, from the scenario's rings and gates; `workReach(s)` gives the most any colony can reach. The 19 readers of the work area choose between "this colony now" (jobs, production, the colony screen) and "the most it can reach" (claims, native pressure, site scores, overlap checks). With no `workArea` in the scenario both are ring 1, read the same way as today, so the Americas stay bit-identical.
- **The colony screen:** in a scenario with two rings, the hex flower shows all 19 tiles; ring 2 is dimmed and marked with its gate ("opens at size 5", or the building's name) until reached, with tiles still showing their yields, so a player sees what growth brings. The tile size shrinks so five hexes fit across the panel (about 60% of today's tile), with the yields drawn as today; the centre tile stays highlighted. Placement comes from the render API (H13), in the scenario's orientation.
- **The computer players' job search** (`ai/advanced.ts` `optimiseWorkers`, `bestJobFor`): 18 tiles instead of 8 roughly doubles the tile candidates per worker. Proposed: tile values worked out once per colony and turn (as `optimiseWorkers` already does for the colony report), then only the best few tiles per good considered for each worker; checked against the plain search with `CC_AI_CHECKS=1`, as the job search is today. Site scores count ring 1 in full and ring 2 at a weight (proposed 0.5) because it opens later. Colonies near the threshold weigh growth a little higher. Timed by the Mac gate (H17).
- **Balance:** small colonies (up to four) have six tiles, fewer than the eight on squares, so early colonies are a little weaker; large colonies reach 18 tiles, far more than eight, so the game favours fewer, larger colonies, as Civilization does. More land per colony means more pressure on native land (claims, tension, Beta 2's leases), and in Vinland a thriving Norse colony reaching further is part of the story. Measured in step 4's tournaments on Vinland and on `hex-test`, against a ring-1-only run: colonies per nation, average and largest colony size, food and production per colonist, score per level, land claimed from peoples and tension, turns to first colony and to ring 2.

## Implementation (step 1)

Built on `beta4/step1` (from `claude/beta4-hex-maps`), 11 November 2026. Engine and tests, plus the movement keys; nothing is drawn on hexes yet (step 3).

**The grids** (`grid.ts`). `GridKind` is `'square' | 'hex'`; `Scenario.map.hex.orientation` is `'pointy'` (default) or `'flat'`. `HEX_POINTY` (odd-r) and `HEX_FLAT` (odd-q) are built by one `makeHex` from the orientation's cube conversion and cube direction steps (H2): distance is the largest cube difference; `withinOffsets` and `ringOffsets` are cached per parity and radius (row by row, as on squares); `neighbourOffsets` is one of two cached arrays. New on every grid: `directionIndex(x, y, dx, dy)` (H5's bit index), `line(a, b)` (cube interpolation with a tiny nudge on hexes; on squares the rounded steps `parseRivers` has always used, not yet used by it), and `centre(x, y)` in tile widths, used only to sample the generator's noise. `directionTowards` on hexes is the first tile of `line`, so it always gets one step nearer. `GridDirection.dx/dy` are the steps from column 0, row 0; on hexes callers use `step` or `neighbourOffsets`. `step` throws for a direction the orientation lacks.

**The work area** (`workarea.ts`, H7). `Scenario.map.workArea: { rings: WorkRing[] }`, each ring with `size?` and `building?` (both must hold), at most two (checked by the conformance test). `openRings`, `colonyWorkArea` and `inColonyWorkArea` give "this colony now"; `workReach`, `reachArea` and `reachOffsets` "the most it can reach". With no `workArea` both are exactly `grid.workArea`, so the squares are untouched. Readers changed: the colony's jobs (`colonyOccupancy`, so `jobOptions`, `bestJob` and the computer players' job search), `validateJob` (the old message on one-ring scenarios), leasable land (now), lease pressure (reach), the computer players' land buying (now) and site scores (reach: ring 2 at `OUTER_RING_VALUE` 0.5, water counted on ring 1 only, shared tiles and the nearby colonies within the reach). A ring that closes moves its workers at the start of `processColony` (before the colony produces), with a message, as a lost lease does. Left on the first ring, deliberately: the lumber from clearing a forest next to a colony (`movement.ts`), the unused `colonyOwningTile`, the colony-founding spacing (`distance < 2`), the events' bonus resource near a colony, and the end-of-game road statistics.

**The job search on 18 tiles.** `optimiseWorkers`, `bestJobFor` and the plain `bestJobByOptions` were already generic over the occupancy's tiles, with tile yields worked out once per colony and unit type; nothing needed changing. The pruning the note proposed ("only the best few tiles per good") was not needed: see the speed check. `CC_AI_CHECKS` (`setJobSearchChecks`) checks it in the hex game test.

**Rivers** (part of step 2, as far as movement and generated maps need). `riverLinked` and `linkRiver` take the grid (squares by default): the bit is `grid.directionIndex`, the opposite `(d + n/2) % n`. Movement (`landStepCost`), the river defence (`combat.ts`) and the computer players' roads pass the game's grid. `generateRivers` drains over the grid's neighbours; the edge-only mouths and the diagonal penalty stay square-only. Left for step 2: `parseRivers` with hex lines (stored maps), `RIVER_DIRS` and the renderers' copies, the conversion of stored masks; braves already move by `landStepCost`.

**Other square assumptions** from the audit: `ai/common.ts` `tilesAround` is indexed by direction (`directionIndex`), and the generator samples its noise at hex centres (H10). No other `for dy … for dx …` loop, Chebyshev formula or `RIVER_DIRS` use is left in the engine outside `grid.ts` and `rivers.ts`.

**Movement keys** (H6): `apps/web/src/keys.ts` `keyDirection` moves only in the grid's directions, which gives exactly H6's mapping (pointy: no Up, Down, numpad 8 and 2; flat: no Left, Right, numpad 4 and 6; the diagonals on both). Not checked against Civilization's or Freeciv's documentation (no source here).

**Hidden test scenarios** (`scenarios/hex-test.ts`): `hex-test` (pointy) and `hex-test-flat`, the Americas' rules on a generated hex map of the same sizes, with `workArea: { rings: [{}, { size: 5 }] }`.

**Tests.** `grid.test.ts`: hex directions and their order, neighbours symmetric on both parities and within one tile on both axes, `distance` equal to breadth-first steps, rings of 6r and 3r(r+1)+1 tiles row by row, lines and `directionTowards`, centres one tile apart, the work rings' offsets, the square `line` and `directionIndex`. `grid-rules.test.ts`, on squares and both orientations: a step in every direction, paths as long as the distance and a go-to followed, roads and rivers at road speed and crossing at the land's, generated rivers linked both ways and reaching water, the work area (one ring while small; ring 2 at five colonists, refused before, released when the colony shrinks; a building gate on squares). `hex-game.test.ts`: 200 rounds of four computer players (all four levels) on each orientation, every worked tile within its colony's open rings and worked once, ring 2 worked, a save and load compared every ten rounds, the last 30 rounds with the index and job search checks. `apps/web/test/keys.test.ts` for the keys. The scenario conformance test checks the work rings.

**Squares bit-identical.** `npm run bench -- --verify` and `--verify --scenario caribbean` "same"; golden tests unchanged; the 24-game Americas tournament (`--games 24 --turns 609 --play-on --events --seed 1000`) identical to the base.
