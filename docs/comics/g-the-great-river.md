# The Great River

**Logline.** A young mapmaker is sent up the great river to chart the way to a new trading post, with two voyageurs and an Anishinaabe brother and sister as guides. When a summer storm takes his notebook and three weeks of careful lines, the guides teach him that a river is better remembered than measured. By the time the post comes into sight, he has to decide what a map should show, and what it should keep quiet, before a greedy rival trader can use it to empty the country of beaver in a single winter.

**Cover moment.** The lead canoe sweeps round the last bend, Migizi at the bow pointing ahead at the earth-walled post with its white pennant on the bluff, Étienne sketching, Baptiste paddling, the second canoe following and a heron lifting off the water. It appears on **page 10**.

**Setting.** The upper Misi-ziibi (the Mississippi, "great river" in Anishinaabemowin), among cottonwood islands, sandbars and pale bluffs, in late summer 1784.

**Cast.**
- **Étienne Duval**: young mapmaker from Montreal. Blue coat with brass buttons, white shirt, red knitted cap, always holding a notebook and pencil. Earnest, precise, cannot draw a heron.
- **Baptiste Ricard**: veteran voyageur, steersman of the lead canoe. Dark beard, red cap, red sash across a white shirt. Sings when he paddles, and believes in every superstition there is.
- **Migizi**: Anishinaabe guide at the bow. Long black hair, red-and-white beaded headband, pale fringed hide shirt with beaded strips, a white pendant on a cord. Teasing, sharp-eyed, knows every bend.
- **Waabigwan**: Migizi's older sister, steering the second canoe. Braided hair, a beaded shoulder bag. She makes maps on birchbark and is unimpressed by anyone's paper.
- **Toussaint**: young voyageur in the second canoe, red cap, always hungry.
- **Aunt Biidaaban**: Migizi's aunt, met at the wild-rice beds. Grey braids, a sharp tongue, a canoe full of rice.
- **Lieutenant Armand Leclerc**: commandant of the post. Tidy grey coat, powdered wig slightly crooked, wants a map he can send home.
- **Hilaire Morel**: rival trader for a rival company. Fat, florid, mustard-yellow coat, a heavy boat full of trade goods and ambition.

*(Personal names are invented; Biidaaban means "dawn". Anishinaabemowin words used: boozhoo (hello), miigwech (thank you), zhashagi (great blue heron), manoomin (wild rice), Misi-ziibi (great river). Check with a language consultant before lettering, as dialects differ.)*

## PAGE 1

1. Big opening panel. Low over shining water, a great blue heron stands in the shallows at the edge of a cottonwood island, perfectly still, regal. In the foreground the bow of a birch-bark canoe slides into frame.
   CAPTION: The upper Misi-ziibi, the great river. Late summer, 1784.

2. Inside the canoe, close on Étienne, pencil poised over his notebook, tongue between his teeth, concentrating on the heron.
   ÉTIENNE (whisper): Don't move. Don't move. Just one more minute...

3. Same angle. The heron has lifted off in a great flapping of wings. Étienne's notebook shows half a heron: legs only.
   SFX: FWAP FWAP FWAP
   ÉTIENNE: EVERY time!

4. Migizi, at the bow, looks back over his shoulder, grinning.
   MIGIZI: Zhashagi doesn't pose for Frenchmen. He's heard about your portraits.

5. Baptiste, paddling in the stern, booms out a paddling song. Behind them the second canoe, with Waabigwan steering and Toussaint paddling, joins in.
   BAPTISTE (singing): ♪ En roulant ma boule roulant... ♪
   TOUSSAINT (singing, off key): ♪ ...en roulant ma bouuule! ♪

## PAGE 2

1. Medium shot of the canoe. Étienne turns the notebook round to show Migizi a careful page: a neat line for the river, numbers in leagues, compass bearings, little crosses for islands.
   ÉTIENNE: Three weeks of the river. Every bend, every bearing. The Lieutenant at the post wants it sent to Montreal before the ice.

2. Close on Migizi studying the page with polite doubt.
   MIGIZI: It's very straight, your river.
   ÉTIENNE: That is what a river looks like on a map.

3. Waabigwan's canoe draws alongside. She leans over to look and raises one eyebrow.
   WAABIGWAN: That island you drew yesterday. It was not there when I was a girl. In two springs it will be gone again.
   ÉTIENNE: Islands don't just leave!

4. Wide panel: the river ahead, braided into channels round islands and long pale sandbars just under the water, the current making smooth patches and rippled patches.
   MIGIZI: Here, everything leaves. The river is not a line, mapmaker. It's an animal. It moves in its sleep.

5. Étienne, stubborn, points his pencil confidently to the left-hand channel.
   ÉTIENNE: Well, my bearings say the channel is there.
   MIGIZI (calmly): And the river says it isn't.

## PAGE 3

1. The canoe runs firmly onto a sandbar. Everyone lurches forward.
   SFX: SKRRRUNCH

2. Baptiste climbs out to push, steps off the sandbar into a deep hole, and vanishes up to his red cap.
   SFX: SPLOOSH

3. Baptiste surfaces, spluttering, his cap floating beside him. Toussaint in the other canoe is weeping with laughter.
   BAPTISTE: Saint Christophe preserve me! This is the third river this summer that has tried to drown me!
   TOUSSAINT: Maybe the rivers like you, Baptiste!

4. Everyone in the water now, knee-deep, pushing the canoe off. Migizi points to the water's surface beside them, teaching.
   MIGIZI: See the smooth water? Sand under it, just below. See the little V-shaped ripples? Deep water, current running.
   MIGIZI: The river writes its own map on its skin. You only have to read it.

5. Étienne, wet to the waist, crouches and stares at the water as if seeing it for the first time.
   ÉTIENNE: Smooth for sand. Ripples for deep...

6. Small panel. Étienne back in the canoe, scribbling a note in the margin of his map. Migizi peers at it.
   MIGIZI: What did you write?
   ÉTIENNE: "Baptiste's Bath." Every good map needs a landmark.

## PAGE 4

1. Evening camp on a sandy point. Canoes turned over on the beach, a fire, a kettle, the bluffs going pink in the sunset.

2. Waabigwan unrolls a scroll of birchbark on her knee. Scratched into it with a bone point are lines, loops, small pictures: a heron, a beaver lodge, a canoe carried overland, a camp of lodges.
   WAABIGWAN: You wanted to see a real map.

3. Étienne kneels beside her, enchanted, holding his own notebook next to the bark for comparison.
   ÉTIENNE: But there is no scale! This bend is as big as that whole lake!
   WAABIGWAN: That bend takes a whole day, against the current. The lake takes a morning. My map measures in days, not in leagues.

4. Close on the bark. Waabigwan's finger moves along the scratched line.
   WAABIGWAN: Here, a place to carry the canoe. Here, good water to drink. Here, the families who will feed you, if you are polite.
   WAABIGWAN: Here, the place you should not camp. The ground floods in the night.

5. Étienne, sitting back on his heels, thoughtful.
   ÉTIENNE: My map is for people in Montreal who will never come here.
   MIGIZI (stirring the kettle): And ours is for people who will.

6. Small panel. Toussaint holds out his bowl, eyes huge.
   TOUSSAINT: Does your map say where the supper is?

## PAGE 5

1. Next day, early afternoon. Wide panel: the canoes on a broad open stretch of river. Behind them, enormous towering clouds have built up, white on top, dark and heavy underneath.
   CAPTION: The day had been hot and very still.

2. Migizi, at the bow, has stopped paddling and turned to look at the sky. His face is serious for the first time.
   MIGIZI: Baptiste. Shore. Now.
   BAPTISTE: It's only a cloud—
   MIGIZI: NOW.

3. The squall hits. Rain sweeps across the river like a grey curtain, the water whipped white, the canoes pitching. Paddles dig hard.
   SFX: KRAKOOOM
   SFX: SHHHHHHHH

4. Inside the lead canoe. A wave slops over the side. Étienne's notebook, tucked under the thwart, is lifted out by the water and swirls away.
   ÉTIENNE: My map! MY MAP!

5. Étienne lunges for it. Baptiste grabs him by the sash and hauls him back into the canoe.
   BAPTISTE: Paper floats! Mapmakers don't!

6. Small panel. The notebook, far off now on the grey water, spins once and goes under.

## PAGE 6

1. The shore, in pouring rain. All five have dragged both canoes up and turned them over on poles to make a shelter. Everyone crouches underneath, dripping.

2. Close on Étienne, soaked, head in his hands.
   ÉTIENNE: Three weeks. Every bearing. Every island. Gone.

3. Waabigwan, beside him, wrings out her braid and looks at him with something between sympathy and impatience.
   WAABIGWAN: You lost some paper. You didn't lose your eyes. Or ours.

4. Migizi pulls a large, flat piece of fresh birchbark from the bundle and hands Étienne a pencil stub.
   MIGIZI: Draw. We'll tell you. Start from the morning we left.

5. A panel of everyone remembering at once, crowded round the bark, pointing, overlapping balloons.
   BAPTISTE: The place I fell in! Don't forget the place I fell in!
   TOUSSAINT: The island with the good blueberries!
   WAABIGWAN: The creek where the old woman gave us fish. Her family's lodges are on the north side.
   MIGIZI: Then the long straight reach. Half a day with the wind behind.

6. Close on the bark under Étienne's hands. It is not a neat line any more, but a winding, living river with small pictures and notes: a blueberry, a fish, a little figure falling into the water.
   ÉTIENNE (quietly): This is better than the one I lost.
   MIGIZI: Of course it is. This one has people in it.

## PAGE 7

1. Next morning, washed clean and bright. The canoes glide through shallow beds of tall green wild rice near a backwater. Several Anishinaabe canoes are among the stalks; in each, one person poles and another bends the rice heads over the canoe and taps them with two sticks.
   CAPTION: Manoomin, the wild rice, was ripe.

2. In the nearest rice canoe, an older woman with grey braids, Aunt Biidaaban, straightens up and squints at the newcomers.
   AUNT BIIDAABAN: Migizi! Boozhoo! You've brought Frenchmen into my rice!
   MIGIZI: Boozhoo, Auntie. Only a few. They're house-trained.

3. The canoes rest side by side. Aunt Biidaaban hands Toussaint a birchbark basket of parched rice; he looks at it as if he has seen heaven.
   TOUSSAINT: Miigwech! Miigwech!
   AUNT BIIDAABAN: Good. This one has manners at least.

4. Aunt Biidaaban's face turns serious as she speaks to Migizi and Waabigwan. Étienne listens closely.
   AUNT BIIDAABAN: Another trader came up the river four days ago. A big yellow coat on a heavy boat.
   AUNT BIIDAABAN: He asked every family where the beaver ponds are. Every family. He says he'll send trappers to take them all this winter.

5. Close on Waabigwan, eyes narrowing.
   WAABIGWAN: All of them? In one winter?

## PAGE 8

1. On the water again. The canoes paddle side by side, everyone quiet. Waabigwan speaks across to Étienne.
   WAABIGWAN: You know what your Lieutenant wants on his map. Where the beaver are.
   ÉTIENNE: ...He did ask for "all the fur streams."

2. Migizi turns round at the bow to face Étienne, very serious.
   MIGIZI: A beaver pond is like a storehouse of corn. Families have taken from the same ponds for longer than anyone remembers, a few each year, so that there are always more.
   MIGIZI: If a man empties them all in one winter, nobody eats for ten.

3. Close on Étienne, looking down at the new birchbark map in his lap, where Waabigwan's family ponds are drawn as small pictures of beaver lodges.
   CAPTION: For the first time, Étienne understood that a map was not only something you drew. It was something you gave away.

4. Baptiste, in the stern, clears his throat.
   BAPTISTE: In my opinion, a wise mapmaker draws only what he is sure of.
   ÉTIENNE: Is that a voyageur superstition?
   BAPTISTE: No. That one is just good sense.

## PAGE 9

1. A narrow chute where the river runs fast between rocks and a fallen cottonwood. Jammed sideways on the snag is a heavy wooden bateau loaded with crates and kegs, water piling up against its side. On top of the crates, in his mustard-yellow coat, stands Hilaire Morel, red-faced and shouting at his two exhausted boatmen.
   MOREL: Push, you idle fellows! PUSH!

2. The two canoes pull into the eddy below. Migizi and Waabigwan exchange a long look.
   TOUSSAINT: It's the yellow coat.
   WAABIGWAN (sighing): And his boat is going to break in half and spill all his kettles into my river.

3. Everyone gets to work. Migizi and Baptiste take a line to the bank and round a tree; Waabigwan shows the boatmen where to lever with their poles; Étienne and Toussaint wade out and push.
   MIGIZI: Not against the river! With it! Let the front swing round!
   SFX: CREEEAK

4. The bateau swings free and slides into the eddy, safe. Morel nearly tumbles off his crates.
   SFX: SPLOSH

5. Morel straightens his coat and his dignity, and tips his hat with false grandeur.
   MOREL: Ah, Duval, the mapmaker! How fortunate. I shall look forward to studying your map at the post.
   MOREL: The Lieutenant has promised me a copy. All those lovely beaver streams.

6. Small panel. Étienne smiles very politely and says nothing at all. Behind him, Migizi and Waabigwan smile very politely and say nothing at all.

## PAGE 10

*(Cover moment.)*

1. LARGE PANEL, two-thirds of the page, matching the cover. Low over the bright water, the lead canoe sweeps towards us round a wide bend. Migizi at the bow points ahead with his whole arm. Étienne in the middle, in blue coat and red cap, sketching on a fresh notebook propped on his knee. Baptiste paddling in the stern. Bundles, a small keg and a rolled tent between them. The second canoe follows on the left with Waabigwan and Toussaint. Tall cottonwoods and willows on both banks; to the right, a pale bluff and on top of it an earth-and-timber trading post with a plain white pennant. A heron lifts off from the shallows on the right. Towering white summer clouds sit over the hazy distance.
   MIGIZI: There. The post.
   BAPTISTE: Saint Christophe be thanked. A roof, a bed, and no more rivers to fall into.

2. Close on Étienne, who looks up from his notebook at the heron as it lifts, and this time just watches it go, smiling.
   ÉTIENNE: Next time, zhashagi.

3. Small panel. Below the bluff, Morel's heavy bateau has already landed. Morel is climbing the path to the post, waving a hand.
   TOUSSAINT: He got there first!
   WAABIGWAN: He got to the door first. That is not the same thing.

## PAGE 11

1. Inside the post's main room: a long counter, shelves of trade goods, bundles of furs, a window onto the river. Lieutenant Leclerc sits at a table, wig slightly crooked. Morel stands at his elbow, rubbing his hands. Étienne unrolls the birchbark map on the table. Migizi and Waabigwan stand beside him.
   LECLERC: Birchbark? Duval, the Governor expects paper. With leagues.
   ÉTIENNE: The paper is at the bottom of the river, sir. This is better. It is measured in days, so a canoe can use it.

2. Close on the map. Morel leans in eagerly, a finger looking for the beaver ponds. Instead of beaver lodges, there are small pictures of family camps, each with a note in Étienne's neat hand.
   MOREL: Where are the beaver streams? It says here... "Lands of the family of Biidaaban. Furs by their leave, brought to the post."

3. Morel, outraged, turns to Leclerc.
   MOREL: This is useless! It hides the best country!
   ÉTIENNE (calmly): It shows exactly where the furs come from, sir. From the families. Every year, if nobody is greedy.

4. Waabigwan steps forward, speaking to Leclerc directly, as one trader to another.
   WAABIGWAN: Send trappers to empty the ponds, and you will have one rich winter and then an empty counter.
   WAABIGWAN: Trade with us, at an honest price, and the families will bring furs to this door every spring for as long as your post stands. We rest the ponds in turn. We have always done it.

5. Leclerc looks from Morel to Waabigwan, does a sum on his fingers, and straightens his wig with a decisive tug.
   LECLERC: A full counter every spring... rather than one rich winter.
   LECLERC: Monsieur Morel, the Company will not be needing your trappers.

6. Small panel. Morel, deflated, stomps out of the door. His yellow coat catches on a nail. He pulls. It rips.
   SFX: RRRIP

## PAGE 12

1. A few days later. On the bluff outside the post, Étienne rolls up a clean paper copy of the map, made from the birchbark, ready for the courier canoe to Montreal. Migizi and Waabigwan watch.
   CAPTION: The copy that went to Montreal had the families' names on it, the days of travel, and the places not to camp.

2. Close on the corner of the paper map, where Étienne writes a careful signature line.
   ÉTIENNE: "Drawn by É. Duval, with Migizi and Waabigwan of the Anishinaabeg, who know the river."
   WAABIGWAN: Hm. You spelled us correctly. You may come back.

3. Below, on the beach, Baptiste and Toussaint load the canoes for the journey home. Baptiste stands well back from the water's edge.
   BAPTISTE: This time I'm getting in from the dry side.

4. Étienne, sitting on the bank with his new notebook, looks up. A great blue heron has landed a few paces away in the shallows, and is standing perfectly still, looking straight at him.
   MIGIZI (whisper): Well. Now he poses.

5. Étienne draws quickly, a few clean lines. Migizi and Waabigwan lean in on either side to watch, impressed despite themselves.
   MIGIZI: Zhashagi poses for mapmakers who have learned to listen.

6. FINAL PANEL, wide. The heron, still standing calmly in the water, has dipped its beak, plucked Étienne's lunch, a smoked fish, off the rock beside him and is swallowing it whole. Étienne, notebook in one hand, a perfect heron drawing in it, stares with his mouth open. Migizi and Waabigwan are helpless with laughter. Behind them, the river winds away round its islands under huge white clouds.
   ÉTIENNE: ...A FEE?!
