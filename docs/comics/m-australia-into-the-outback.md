# Into the Outback

**Logline:** In a drought year a surveyor sets out west across the dry plains to find the great "inland sea" his maps promise. He relies on a Wiradjuri man, Burran, who is walking home to his uncle's people and knows where the hidden water lies. When a squatter's overseer tries to claim the last waterhole for his sheep, a sudden flood down a dry creek puts everyone in the same mud. The surveyor learns that a map of water is also a map of people.

**Cover moment:** Burran kneels in the red earth and points out the hidden rock-hole to Josiah Pell, who kneels beside him holding Biscuit's reins, while Alfie writes in his notebook and the bullock wagon comes up behind. It is the full-width splash on **page 4** (panel 3).

**Setting:** Wiradjuri Country west of the Lachlan River, inland New South Wales, in the dry summer of 1817.

**Cast**
- **Burran:** a Wiradjuri man of about twenty-five. Dark curly hair, a necklace of shell and seed, a pale grey possum-skin cloak sewn from many pelts and worn over the shoulders, a long wooden staff. Quiet, very funny when he decides to be, and quite certain about where he is going.
- **Josiah Pell:** a surveyor in his thirties. A dusty blue coat with brass buttons, a dark waistcoat, a loose white neckcloth, buff breeches, tall brown boots, a broad brown hat. Honest, proud of his instruments, and slow to admit that a compass cannot find water.
- **Alfie Brand:** Pell's assistant, eighteen. A red coat, a small brown hat, a leather satchel full of pencils, a notebook he never stops filling. Draws everything: beetles, clouds, Ned's snoring.
- **Ned Hollis:** the bullock driver, a ticket-of-leave man (a convict now allowed to work for wages) of about forty-five. Weathered, cheerful, missing a tooth. Loves his two bullocks, **Duke** and **Duchess**, more than most people.
- **Biscuit:** Pell's bay horse, with a white blaze down its face. Opinionated.
- **Mr Silas Cobb:** overseer for a squatter's flock pushing west. A tall man in a cabbage-tree hat and a grey coat, with a stockwhip he cracks for emphasis. Not wicked, but convinced that any water he finds is his.
- **Uncle Dalla:** Burran's uncle, an elder of the river camp. White beard, a possum-skin cloak like Burran's, eyes that miss nothing.

*Burran, Dalla and the other Wiradjuri names are invented for the story. Cultural detail is kept general; no ceremony or sacred story is used as a plot device.*

**Panel 1.** Wide establishing panel. A vast plain of red earth and pale yellow spinifex under an enormous, clean blue sky. A few white-trunked gum trees. Far away, a low range of red sandstone. In the middle distance, a tiny line: a covered wagon pulled by two bullocks, two pack horses, and three walking figures.
CAPTION: West of the Lachlan River. The country of the Wiradjuri people.
CAPTION: The summer of 1817, and no rain since the winter before last.

**Panel 2.** Closer. Josiah Pell walks beside Biscuit, compass held out flat in his palm, squinting at it. Alfie trudges beside him, notebook open. Ned walks at the head of the bullocks with a long whip he never uses.
PELL: Due west, Alfie. The rivers here all flow inland. Where can they be going? To a great inland sea, that's where!
ALFIE (writing): "Inland sea... still... not here."

**Panel 3.** Ned taps the water cask on the wagon. A hollow sound.
SFX: BONK. BONK.
NED: Mr Pell, sir, the sea can wait. The cask can't. That's the sound of nothing in a barrel.

**Panel 4.** Ahead of them, a little apart, Burran walks with his staff, cloak over one shoulder, looking not at a compass but at the ground, the trees, the sky. A small flock of zebra finches darts past him.
CAPTION: Burran had joined them at the river crossing a week before. He was walking home to his uncle's people anyway.
BURRAN (to himself): And somebody had to stop these three walking off the edge of the world.

## PAGE 2

**Panel 1.** Flashback panel, in a rounded frame: a river crossing, Pell's party stuck with a wagon wheel in mud. Burran stands on the bank, arms folded, watching them push.
CAPTION: A week before...
PELL (flashback): We're making for the west. Is there a road?
BURRAN (flashback): There are many roads. Only you can't see them.

**Panel 2.** Back to the present. Pell lifts his brass compass and spyglass to show Burran, proud.
PELL: With these, my friend, I can find any point on the face of the earth!
BURRAN: Can they find water?
PELL: ...Not as such.

**Panel 3.** Ned rubs Duchess's nose. The bullock leans into his hand, eyes half closed.
NED: Don't you listen to him, Duchess. You're not thirsty. You're just... dreaming of a puddle.
DUCHESS: MOOOOO.

**Panel 4.** Alfie, eager, falls into step beside Burran with his notebook.
ALFIE: Mr Burran, how do *you* find water, out here where there's none?
BURRAN: There's always some. It just hides from people who make too much noise.
ALFIE (whispering): ...Is this quiet enough?

## PAGE 3

**Panel 1.** Late afternoon, long shadows. Burran stops and crouches by a line of ants marching across a red rock. Pell, impatient, reins in Biscuit.
BURRAN: Ants. Busy ones. Look where they go.

**Panel 2.** Burran points up. Zebra finches flying low and straight, all in one direction, towards a small rise of broken red rocks.
BURRAN: Finches drink every day, morning and evening. When they fly low and straight like that, they're going home to water.
ALFIE (sketching fast): Finches... low... straight...

**Panel 3.** Pell looks at his compass, then at the finches, then at the compass again. A tiny storm cloud of doubt over his head (a little scribble).
PELL: My compass says the sea is due west.
BURRAN: Your compass is walking to the sea. The finches are walking to a drink. Which one are you?

**Panel 4.** Ned grins and turns the bullocks towards the rocks.
NED: Begging your pardon, Mr Pell, sir, but I'm with the birds.
BISCUIT (snorting): Hrrnf!
PELL: ...Even the horse?

## PAGE 4

**Panel 1.** At the rocky rise. Spinifex clumps, a flat slab of red stone, dry grass. No water anywhere. Pell spreads his hands.
PELL: Rocks. Grass. Red dust. I'll grant your finches have a fine view.

**Panel 2.** Burran sets down his staff and kneels. He brushes aside a clump of spinifex and a flat stone that someone, long ago, placed over a hollow in the rock.
BURRAN: My uncle's family keep this one covered. So the sun doesn't drink it first. So animals don't fall in.

**Panel 3.** FULL-WIDTH SPLASH, the cover moment. Burran kneels in his pale possum-skin cloak in the red earth, pointing down into a clear pool of water in a hollow of rock, fringed with green shoots. Pell kneels beside him in his blue coat and broad hat, holding Biscuit's reins, the bay horse with its white blaze looming behind him, pack saddles on its back. Behind them, Alfie in his red coat writes furiously in his notebook. Further back, Ned and the covered bullock wagon and two pack horses come up the track. Spinifex, white-trunked gums, and the red sandstone ranges glowing on the horizon. Under a tree at the right, a mob of kangaroos watches.
CAPTION: Water. Hidden in plain sight.
PELL (quietly): Good heavens.
BURRAN: Not heavens. Just looking down instead of up.

**Panel 4.** Close on Alfie's notebook: a neat sketch of the rock-hole, the stone lid, the finches.
ALFIE: Mr Pell, shall I mark it on the map?
PELL: ...Yes. Yes, of course.
BURRAN (frowning slightly): Hm.

## PAGE 5

**Panel 1.** Duke and Duchess smell water and charge towards the rock-hole, dragging the wagon. Ned hangs on to the yoke, boots skidding.
SFX: THUD THUD THUD THUD
NED: WHOA! Duke! Duchess! Manners!

**Panel 2.** Burran steps calmly in front of the bullocks and holds up his staff crosswise. The two great beasts skid to a stop, noses an inch from him.
BURRAN: People first. Then the casks. Then you, in a trough. If you put your feet in, nobody drinks for a month.

**Panel 3.** A neat sequence across the panel: Pell and Alfie dip water with a tin cup into a canvas bucket; Ned carries it to a trough made from a cut-down cask; the bullocks and horses drink from that, not from the rock-hole.
CAPTION: They took what they needed. Not more.

**Panel 4.** Burran puts the stone lid back over the hole and lays the spinifex back on top.
BURRAN: It refills from the rock slowly. Take too much, and it's gone. Then the next person who comes here thirsty finds nothing, because of your carelessness.
PELL: And if somebody comes who doesn't know about the lid?

**Panel 5.** Burran gives Pell a long look.
BURRAN: That's what I'm wondering about your map.

## PAGE 6

**Panel 1.** Evening. The camp by the rocks, a small fire, the wagon. Suddenly, a cloud of red dust on the eastern horizon, and a sound like a thousand complaints.
SFX: BAAAA BAAAA BAAAAA
ALFIE: What's that? A dust storm?
NED: Worse. Sheep.

**Panel 2.** A flock of hundreds of sheep pours over a rise, driven by two stockmen with dogs. At their head rides Silas Cobb on a grey horse, cabbage-tree hat, stockwhip coiled.
COBB: Evening, gents! Followed your wheel-tracks three days. Reckoned a surveyor knows where the water is!

**Panel 3.** Cobb leans down from his saddle, spots the stone lid, and grins.
COBB: And here it is! Lovely. I'll put a rail round this and claim the run. Two thousand head, this'll carry.
PELL: That hole would barely water two thousand *finches*, Mr Cobb.

**Panel 4.** Burran stands up beside the rock-hole, staff planted in the earth.
BURRAN: This water is my uncle's family's. They've looked after it longer than your sheep have had legs.
COBB: Nobody lives out here!
BURRAN: Somebody covered it with a stone. Do you think the stone did it by itself?

**Panel 5.** Close on Pell, uncomfortable, glancing at Alfie's notebook with the neat sketch of the rock-hole in it.
CAPTION: For the first time, Josiah Pell wondered who his maps were for.

## PAGE 7

**Panel 1.** Cobb, sulky, waves his stockmen down into the broad, dry creek bed below the rocks: soft sand, flat, sheltered from the wind. The sheep spill into it like a woolly river.
COBB: We'll camp in the creek. Soft sand, no wind. And in the morning, the water's ours.

**Panel 2.** Burran stands on the rocks, looking not at Cobb but far away, to the red ranges on the horizon. Above them, tiny flickers of lightning in a bank of dark cloud.
SFX (tiny, far away): ...rrrrmm...
BURRAN: Mr Cobb. Get your sheep out of the creek.

**Panel 3.** Cobb laughs, pointing up at the clear sky overhead, full of stars.
COBB: Out of a dry creek? Under a sky like that? You're having a lend of me, mate.
BURRAN: The rain isn't here. The rain is *there*. But the creek goes from there to here.

**Panel 4.** Pell looks from the lightning to the dry creek bed, and his eyes widen. He snaps his spyglass shut.
PELL: Good Lord. He's right. That creek is a channel from the ranges. If it's raining up there...
NED: ...then it'll be raining *here*, only sideways, and all at once.

**Panel 5.** Cobb rolls himself in his blanket in the creek bed, hat over his face.
COBB: Wake me if the sea comes. Haw haw.

## PAGE 8

**Panel 1.** Night. Burran sits awake on the rocks, cloak around him, listening. Pell can't sleep either and sits beside him.
PELL: You don't trust it.
BURRAN: I trust it. That's why I'm awake.

**Panel 2.** Close on Burran's hand, flat on the ground. A faint trembling.
SFX: ...rrrrrrRRRR...
BURRAN: Now. Wake everyone.

**Panel 3.** Pell runs down to the creek bank waving his hat, shouting. Alfie and Ned leap up from their blankets.
PELL: COBB! UP! OUT OF THE CREEK! NOW!
COBB (sleepy): Wha...?

**Panel 4.** Big panel. Down the creek bed, from the dark, a wall of brown water comes rolling, carrying branches and foam, filling the channel bank to bank. Cobb's stockmen scramble up the bank; the sheep bleat in panic.
SFX: ROOOOOOAAAAARRRR

**Panel 5.** Cobb's supply dray is stuck in the soft sand mid-creek, its horse rearing. Cobb clings to the wheel, water already to his knees.
COBB: HELP! Me tucker! Me dray!
NED: Forget the tucker, you galah! Duke! Duchess! Come on, my darlings!

## PAGE 9

**Panel 1.** Ned leads Duke and Duchess to the edge of the bank. Burran throws a rope to Cobb with one smooth swing. Cobb catches it and ties it to the dray's pole.
BURRAN: Tie it to the pole, not yourself! The bullocks pull the dray, the dray pulls you!

**Panel 2.** Ned at the bullocks' heads, gentle and firm. The two great animals lean into their yoke together, hooves dug into red mud.
NED: Steady... steady... Now, my beauties, HEAVE!
SFX: HHNNNGGHH

**Panel 3.** The dray rolls up out of the flood onto the bank, Cobb sprawled on top of it, soaked, his hat gone.
SFX: SKLOOOSH!

**Panel 4.** Meanwhile, Alfie and Burran, with the stockmen's dogs, drive the bleating flock up a side gully to high ground. Burran shows Alfie how to walk wide and slow so the sheep don't scatter.
BURRAN: Not behind them. Beside them. They go where you're *not*.
ALFIE: That's the opposite of how people work!
BURRAN: Is it?

**Panel 5.** On the high rocks, everyone safe, soaked, mud to the knees. Below them the creek runs bank-full and fast in the starlight. Cobb sits on his dray, dripping, staring.
COBB: ...Nobody lives out here, I said.
BURRAN: And here we all are.

## PAGE 10

**Panel 1.** Dawn. The plain below shines: the creek has spread into a broad, shallow sheet of water among the trees, reflecting the sky. Ducks have already arrived from nowhere.
CAPTION: By morning, the dry country was a mirror.
NED: There's your inland sea, Mr Pell!
BURRAN: It'll be gone by the next new moon. The country breathes in and out.

**Panel 2.** Pell, wet and humbled, crouches beside Burran on the rocks. His great map is spread on a stone, the words "INLAND SEA?" written across the blank west.
PELL: I came out here looking for a sea that isn't there. And I nearly walked past the water that is.

**Panel 3.** A shout from across the water. People of Burran's family, men, women and children, come walking along the far bank, cloaks over their shoulders, carrying nets and bags. At their head walks Uncle Dalla, white-bearded.
BURRAN (beaming, waving his staff): Uncle!

**Panel 4.** Burran gestures to Pell, Alfie, Ned and Cobb to stay where they are, on the near bank.
BURRAN: Wait here. You don't walk into someone's camp without being asked. Not even after a flood.
NED: Wait it is. Duchess, sit.
DUCHESS: Moo?

**Panel 5.** Burran and Uncle Dalla embrace on the far bank. Dalla looks over his nephew's shoulder at the soggy strangers, the sheep and the bullocks, and raises one eyebrow very high.
DALLA: You went to fetch news. You brought *sheep*?
BURRAN: I brought news *with* sheep.

## PAGE 11

**Panel 1.** Later. The family's camp under river red gums: bark shelters, fires, a fish net drying on a pole. The visitors sit where they were invited to. Dalla sits with Pell, drawing in the sand with a stick: the creek, the rock-holes, the river.
DALLA: Here, water after rain. Here, water in summer. Here, water only for the old ones and the little ones when it's very dry. You don't take that one. Ever.
PELL: I understand.

**Panel 2.** Pell looks at his own map, then at Dalla's drawing in the sand. His is mostly blank. Dalla's is full.
PELL (to Alfie, quietly): His map has more in it than mine. And he hasn't even got paper.

**Panel 3.** Cobb, wrapped in a borrowed possum-skin cloak, sits awkwardly among the children, who are inspecting his stockwhip with great interest.
COBB: ...I'll take the flock back east, to the river. There's grass there. I'll tell the boss this country's spoken for.
DALLA: It always was.

**Panel 4.** Alfie sits with two girls who are teaching him to make string from bark fibre. His is terrible. They laugh at him without mercy.
ALFIE: Is it... supposed to be lumpy?
GIRL: No.

**Panel 5.** A sombre, quiet panel. The camp at dusk from far away, small against the plain.
CAPTION: Maps like Pell's did bring more flocks west, and many more after them. In the years that followed, much of Wiradjuri Country was taken for sheep runs, and the Wiradjuri resisted and lost a great deal.
CAPTION: They endured. Their language is spoken and taught on that Country today.

## PAGE 12

**Panel 1.** Next morning. Pell sits on a log with his map and ink. He carefully crosses out "INLAND SEA?".
CAPTION: Next morning, Josiah Pell made the most important correction of his career.

**Panel 2.** Close on the map. Where the rock-hole is, he has drawn a small circle and written beside it, in neat copperplate: "Wiradjuri water. Ask Dalla's family first." Across the whole west he has written: "This country is lived in."
ALFIE (off): Mr Pell, the Surveyor-General won't like that.
PELL (off): Then he can come out here and ask Dalla himself.

**Panel 3.** Farewells at the camp's edge. Burran stays; the others are leaving. Burran and Pell shake hands; Biscuit nuzzles Burran's cloak.
PELL: Will you come back with us?
BURRAN: I am back. This is where I was walking to. You're the ones who are going somewhere.

**Panel 4.** Ned, on the wagon, waves his hat. Duke and Duchess, fat with water, plod east. Cobb's flock trails away behind them in a dusty line.
NED: Ta-ta, Mr Burran! Duchess says thanks for the trough!
DUCHESS: MOOO!

**Panel 5.** FINAL PANEL, wide and golden. Evening at the rock-hole, the stone lid back in place. A mob of kangaroos has come down from the shade to drink at the shallow rain-water nearby. Burran sits on the rocks with Uncle Dalla, looking east, where a tiny wagon disappears into red dust under a vast sky turning gold and violet. The red ranges glow.
DALLA: Do you think he'll remember the lid?
BURRAN: He wrote it down. Twice. And then he drew a picture.
