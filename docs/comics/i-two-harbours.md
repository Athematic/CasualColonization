# Two Harbours

**Logline.** Two merchants from two friendly nations have one day in the port of Westerhaven to swap cargoes, pass the Crown's new weighing laws and catch the evening tide together. A greedy rival has hired the only harbour crane to stop them, and is trying to pass off fake furs as well. Femke, a twelve-year-old ledger clerk, and Kitpu, the Mi'kmaq mate whose family trapped and traded the real furs, have to beat the clock with sums, sharp eyes and a great deal of walking in a treadwheel.

**Cover moment.** On the stone quay, Willem Brandt in blue and Henrik Falck in green shake hands over the open crate of furs while porters carry sacks of coffee, tobacco and casks up the gangplanks of the blue-pennanted and green-pennanted ships, and the great treadwheel crane swings a cargo net overhead. It appears on **page 10**.

**Setting.** Westerhaven, a busy North Sea home port of tall gabled warehouses, a customs house, a weigh-house and a church spire, with a lighthouse on the headland. One day in April 1734.

**Cast.**
- **Willem Brandt**: merchant of Westerhaven, owner of the blue-pennanted ship *Morgenlicht*. Blue coat with gold trim, black tricorn edged with gold, lace cravat, brown hair in a queue. Careful, honest, worries.
- **Henrik Falck**: merchant from the green nation's port of Nordkrona, owner of the green-pennanted ship *Grønne Due*. Dark green coat with gold trim, yellow waistcoat, black tricorn edged with gold. Cheerful, impulsive, easily tempted by a bargain.
- **Femke Brandt** (12): Willem's niece and clerk. Grey-blue dress, white apron and cap, ink-stained fingers, a ledger hugged to her chest. Thinks in numbers and is nearly always right.
- **Kitpu**: Mi'kmaq mate of the *Grønne Due* and a partner in the fur venture. Green wool sailor's jacket, black hair tied back, a beaded pouch at his belt. Dry humour, patient, knows fur better than anyone in Europe.
- **Joris**: big porter in a brown waistcoat and flat cap, usually under a sack of coffee.
- **Bram**: porter with a red kerchief on his head, quick and cheeky.
- **Collector Ambrosius Kraai**: the Crown's customs collector. Tall, thin, black coat, enormous spectacles. Loves rules, scales and the truth, in that order.
- **Gerrit Slijkhuis**: rival merchant. Stout, plum-coloured coat, many rings, a smile like an oily ladle.

*(Mi'kmaq words used: kwe' (hello), wela'lin (thank you). Check with a Mi'kmaq language consultant before lettering. Ship names are invented.)*

## PAGE 1

1. Wide opening panel. Dawn over the harbour mouth: the lighthouse on its headland, a ship with a plain green pennant gliding in under topsails past it, gulls over the water.
   CAPTION: Westerhaven, on the North Sea. April, 1734.
   CAPTION: The *Grønne Due* had come from Nordkrona, and before that from across the ocean.

2. On the quay, the blue-pennanted *Morgenlicht* is already moored, gangplank down. Femke runs along the cobbles towards it, cap flying, ledger clutched to her chest.
   FEMKE: Uncle Willem! Uncle Willem! She's in! The green ship's in!

3. On the *Morgenlicht*'s deck, Willem Brandt, quill in hand, nearly drops a sheaf of papers over the rail.
   WILLEM: Already? Then we have one day. One day and one tide.

4. Close on the *Grønne Due* coming alongside the quay. At her rail, a cheerful man in green waves his tricorn: Henrik Falck. Beside him, coiling a line, stands Kitpu in his green sailor's jacket, quietly taking in the town.
   HENRIK: Brandt! Old friend! I've brought you the best furs this side of the ocean!
   KITPU (aside): He has. My aunt made sure of it.

## PAGE 2

1. On the quay. Willem and Henrik embrace warmly. Femke holds up her ledger and reads out the deal in a clear voice. Kitpu stands beside them.
   FEMKE: Furs from the *Grønne Due* to the *Morgenlicht*. Coffee, tobacco and tools from the *Morgenlicht* to the *Grønne Due*. Then both ships sail on the evening tide.
   HENRIK: Perfect. Both our harbours richer by supper.

2. Willem introduces himself to Kitpu with a bow; Kitpu returns it.
   WILLEM: And you are Master Falck's mate?
   KITPU: Mate, and partner. Kwe'. These furs came from my family's hunting grounds. I have come a long way to see that they are sold fairly.

3. A shadow falls over them. Collector Kraai, tall and thin in black, peers down through enormous spectacles, holding a printed notice.
   KRAAI: Good morning. New ordinance from the Crown. All goods from foreign ships to be weighed at the town weigh-house, and duty paid, BEFORE they are moved to another vessel.
   KRAAI: Anything not weighed by the evening tide stays in port. One week.

4. Henrik and Willem look at each other in horror.
   HENRIK: A week! My crew will eat my profits!
   WILLEM: And the coffee must go west before the summer. Femke, how long to unload, weigh and reload?

5. Femke, pencil flying over a ledger page, does a lightning sum.
   FEMKE: With the harbour crane? Seven hours. We have nine. We can do it.

## PAGE 3

1. They hurry to the great timber harbour crane at the quay's end, with its huge treadwheel. A man in a plum-coloured coat is standing in front of it, rings glittering: Gerrit Slijkhuis.
   SLIJKHUIS: Ah, Brandt. Falck. Alas, I have hired the crane. For the whole day.

2. Close on Slijkhuis, smiling his oily smile. Behind him the crane stands idle, with nothing at all on its hook.
   WILLEM: But you're not lifting anything!
   SLIJKHUIS: I may decide to. At any moment. That is the beauty of hiring it for the whole day.

3. Slijkhuis sidles up to Henrik, all charm, and lowers his voice.
   SLIJKHUIS: Of course, Master Falck, if you sold your furs to ME instead, at half again Brandt's price, I would find I no longer needed the crane at all.

4. Close on Henrik, tempted, eyebrows up.
   HENRIK: Half again...?

5. Kitpu puts a quiet hand on Henrik's arm.
   KITPU: The furs are promised to Brandt. My aunt promised them, and you promised her.
   KITPU: Among my people, a broken promise costs more than half again.

6. Henrik deflates, ashamed, and turns back to Slijkhuis.
   HENRIK: ...Quite right. The furs go to Brandt.
   SLIJKHUIS (shrugging): Then good luck carrying it all by hand. I have a lovely chair in the tavern window. I shall watch.

## PAGE 4

1. Femke sits on a bollard, scribbling, Joris and Bram the porters looking over her shoulders.
   FEMKE: By hand, with the gangplanks only... eleven hours. The tide turns in nine. We miss it by two.
   BRAM: Two hours! We'd need wings.

2. Kitpu looks up thoughtfully at the ships' own rigging: the yards on the masts, the ropes and blocks.
   KITPU: We don't need wings. We have masts. A yard and a tackle can lift a load like any crane.

3. Wide panel. Both crews swarm up the rigging, swinging the lower yards out over the quay and rigging block-and-tackle at the yardarms. Barrels begin to rise from the *Grønne Due*'s hold.
   CAPTION: Every sailor knew the trick. Nobody had thought of it, because nobody wanted to do it.

4. Trouble at once. A blue sailor and a green sailor argue at the foot of the mast over a rope.
   BLUE SAILOR: That's not how you tie a hitch! In Westerhaven we do it THIS way!
   GREEN SAILOR: In Nordkrona we do it the RIGHT way!

5. Kitpu steps between them, takes the rope, and ties a third, simple, strong knot in one smooth movement. Both sailors stare.
   KITPU: In my village we do it this way. It holds, and it comes undone when you want it to.
   BOTH SAILORS (grudging): ...Huh.

## PAGE 5

1. Wide panel: the work going at full speed. Cargo nets swing down from the yardarms; porters carry sacks of coffee and bales of tobacco up and down the gangplanks; a cart trundles towards the weigh-house.

2. The weigh-house, a stone building with a huge beam balance hanging under its arch. Kraai stands beside it, but alone and harassed, a stack of papers sliding off his desk.
   KRAAI: My clerk is in bed with a cold, the ships are early, and the Crown wants every ounce in three copies.

3. Femke marches up, plants her ledger on his desk and dips a quill.
   FEMKE: Read me the weights. I'll write them. Three copies.
   KRAAI (peering at her): Are you old enough to do sums?
   FEMKE: Are you old enough to read a scale?

4. Ten minutes later. Kraai calls out weights; Femke writes as fast as he speaks; the porters move barrels on and off the balance like clockwork.
   KRAAI: Coffee, sack eleven, one hundred and four pounds!
   FEMKE: Coffee, sack eleven, one-oh-four. Running total, one thousand one hundred and sixty. Next!

5. Close on Kraai, his stern face cracking into a small, astonished smile.
   KRAAI: She is faster than my clerk.
   KRAAI: She is faster than ME.

## PAGE 6

1. Back on the quay. A cart pulls up, piled with bundles of dark, glossy furs. Slijkhuis, who has left his tavern chair, struts beside it.
   SLIJKHUIS: Master Brandt! If you won't buy Falck's furs elsewhere, perhaps you'll buy more! Prime beaver, my friend. Cheaper than his.

2. Willem, tempted in his turn, reaches for a pelt. It looks very fine.
   WILLEM: Hm. They are cheaper...
   FEMKE (arriving, panting): Uncle! The ledger says we can't afford "cheaper" today. We can only afford "true."

3. Kitpu walks over, picks up one of Slijkhuis's pelts, and examines it. He bends it, sniffs it, and blows gently into the fur against the grain.
   KITPU: May I?

4. Close on two pelts side by side in Kitpu's hands, his own family's beaver and Slijkhuis's "beaver". When he blows into his own, the dense, soft underfur opens like a deep grey cloud. When he blows into Slijkhuis's, the thin skin shows straight through.
   KITPU: A beaver wears two coats: long hairs on top, and underneath, fur so thick the water never reaches the skin. That's what the hatmakers pay for.
   KITPU: This one has only one coat. It's rabbit and muskrat, dyed dark and stretched.

5. Slijkhuis splutters, going the colour of his coat.
   SLIJKHUIS: Nonsense! How would a... a sailor know?
   KITPU (calmly): My aunt has scraped and stretched more beaver than you have eaten dinners. I learned at her elbow.

6. A tall shadow. Kraai has come from the weigh-house and stands peering at the furs through his spectacles.
   KRAAI: Interesting. Master Slijkhuis declared these goods to the Crown this morning as "prime beaver." The duty on prime beaver is high. The duty on rabbit is low.
   KRAAI: Either way, someone has been telling the Crown stories.

## PAGE 7

1. Kraai unrolls a long paper, sharp and precise.
   KRAAI: False declaration. A fine of fifty guilders. And, since Master Slijkhuis hired the harbour crane under the same false papers, the hire is void.
   KRAAI: The crane is free. First come, first served.

2. Femke, Willem, Henrik and Kitpu all spin round to look at the great crane.
   EVERYONE: The CRANE!

3. They rush to the crane. But the treadwheel is empty: the two big men who walk inside it to turn it are nowhere to be seen.
   BRAM: The wheel-walkers are gone!
   JORIS: Slijkhuis paid them to sit in the tavern all day. They'll be on their third pot of beer by now.

4. Femke, ledger under her arm, does another lightning sum.
   FEMKE: With the crane, even this late, we can still make the tide. But it needs walkers. Four people, at least.

5. A pause. Everyone looks at the two merchants in their fine long coats.
   HENRIK: ...Us?
   WILLEM: Us.

## PAGE 8

1. Comic panel. Willem and Henrik, in coats and tricorns, walk side by side inside the great wooden treadwheel like two hamsters, the wheel turning slowly. Femke and Bram walk at the edges.
   SFX: CREEEAK... CREEEAK...
   HENRIK: In Nordkrona, the merchants' guild would faint if they saw this.
   WILLEM: In Westerhaven they're watching from every window. Keep walking.

2. Outside, the crane's long arm swings round, lifting a whole cargo net of coffee sacks from the quay and lowering it into the *Grønne Due*'s hold.
   KITPU (at the hold, guiding the net): Down... down... good!

3. Townsfolk gather on the quay to watch, laughing and pointing at the two merchants in the wheel. A baker's boy, a fishwife and two apprentices climb in to help.
   FISHWIFE: Shift over, your lordships! My man walks this wheel every Thursday!

4. The treadwheel now spins faster, crowded with walkers of every kind, merchants and fishwives and apprentices all stepping together.
   SFX: CREAK CREAK CREAK CREAK

5. The church spire clock face, its hands close to the evening hour. The light is turning golden.
   CAPTION: The tide waits for no merchant, however well dressed.

## PAGE 9

1. The last big load: the main crate of Kitpu's family furs is lifted from the *Grønne Due* to swing across to the *Morgenlicht*. It rises high over the quay on the crane hook.

2. Close on the rope at the hook. One strand is fraying where it rubs on the crate's iron corner.
   SFX: tk... tk... tk...

3. Kitpu, on the quay below, sees it. He shouts up to the wheel.
   KITPU: STOP THE WHEEL! The rope's rubbing through!

4. Everyone in the wheel stops walking at once. The wheel jerks to a halt; the crate hangs swinging in mid-air.
   SFX: KA-CHUNK

5. Kitpu scrambles up the crane's timber frame, wraps a folded sack round the rope where it touches the iron corner, and lashes it tight with his own quick knot.
   KITPU: There. A coat for the rope too.

6. He slides down again and waves to the wheel.
   KITPU: Gently now. Walk like you're carrying eggs.
   HENRIK (from inside the wheel, tired): I've been carrying eggs all day.

## PAGE 10

*(Cover moment.)*

1. LARGE PANEL, two-thirds of the page, matching the cover. On the stone quay in the golden late light, Willem in blue and Henrik in green (coats creased now, but hats firmly on) shake hands over the open crate of furs, set safely down at last. On the left, the blue-pennanted *Morgenlicht* with her gangplank down; on the right, the green-pennanted *Grønne Due*. Joris carries a sack of coffee in the left foreground; Bram, in his red kerchief, carries a bale on the right. More porters carry casks and crates up both gangplanks. The timber treadwheel crane lifts one last cargo net in the middle distance. Behind them, the gabled warehouses in cream, brick red and slate blue, the customs house, the church spire, the lighthouse on the headland and sails heading west.
   WILLEM: Your furs, Henrik. Every one as promised.
   HENRIK: Your coffee, Willem. Every sack weighed in three copies.

2. Femke slips between them with her ledger open and a quill, and holds up a fresh sheet.
   FEMKE: And while you're shaking hands... sign this.
   WILLEM: What is it?
   FEMKE: The Two Harbours Agreement. I wrote it while I was in the wheel.

3. Close on the sheet as Femke reads it aloud, the two merchants listening.
   FEMKE: "Each harbour will take the other's ships as if they were its own. Each merchant will carry the other's goods at a fair rate. And neither will deal with anyone who tries to set them against each other."
   HENRIK (glancing at the tavern): I can think of someone.

## PAGE 11

1. Kitpu steps forward and lays one finger on the agreement.
   KITPU: One more line. My family's furs are paid for at the price Master Brandt promised, and the money goes back across the ocean to them, by Master Falck's ship, next voyage. Written down.
   KITPU: And my name on the paper as a partner, not as "cargo."

2. Willem looks at Kitpu with real respect and nods.
   WILLEM: Write it, Femke. Exactly as he says.

3. Femke writes, then turns the ledger round. Kitpu signs it in a clear hand; Willem and Henrik sign beside him.
   FEMKE: "Kitpu, partner, of the Mi'kmaq." Is that spelled right?
   KITPU: Better than the hatmakers will ever manage. Wela'lin.

4. Collector Kraai arrives with his seal and a small pot of red wax and, with ceremony, presses the town's customs seal onto the agreement.
   KRAAI: Witnessed. Weighed, duty paid, and legal in three copies.
   KRAAI (to Femke, quietly): If you ever want work at the weigh-house, young lady...
   FEMKE: I'll think about it. After I've had supper.

5. On the quay edge, the ship's bell of the *Grønne Due* rings. The tide is turning. Sailors cast off lines.
   SFX: DING DING DING
   HENRIK: The tide! All aboard!

## PAGE 12

1. The two ships ease away from the quay side by side, sails dropping from the yards. Willem stands at the *Morgenlicht*'s rail; Henrik and Kitpu at the *Grønne Due*'s.
   CAPTION: Both ships sailed on the evening tide, one blue pennant and one green, together.

2. On the quay, Femke waves her ledger above her head. Beside her stand Joris, Bram, the fishwife and the apprentices, all waving too.
   FEMKE: Write when you reach Nordkrona! In three copies!

3. On the *Grønne Due*'s deck, Kitpu takes a small beaded token from his pouch and gives it to Henrik.
   KITPU: For your cabin. So you remember what a promise weighs.
   HENRIK (grinning): Heavier than coffee, lighter than a treadwheel.

4. Close on Willem at the *Morgenlicht*'s rail, looking back at his town with quiet satisfaction.
   WILLEM: Two harbours, one road across the sea.

5. Back on the quay, in the long golden light. Collector Kraai stands beside the treadwheel crane, consulting a paper. Inside the wheel, red-faced and puffing in his plum-coloured coat, walks Gerrit Slijkhuis.
   KRAAI: Fifty guilders, Master Slijkhuis, or one evening walking the wheel for the fishing fleet. You chose the wheel.
   SLIJKHUIS (puffing): I am... reconsidering... the guilders...

6. FINAL PANEL, wide. The harbour mouth at sunset. The two ships, blue pennant and green pennant streaming the same way, sail out past the lighthouse into a golden sea where other sails are already heading west. In the lower corner of the panel, tiny but clear, the great treadwheel still turns.
   SFX (small, from the wheel): creak... creak... creak...
