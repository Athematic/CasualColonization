## 1. Historical and Textual Ground

The Norse presence in North America rests on three distinct kinds of evidence:

1. **Near-contemporary non-saga texts:**
   - **Adam of Bremen**, *Gesta Hammaburgensis ecclesiae pontificum* (c. 1075), Book IV (*Descriptio insularum Aquilonis*): earliest written mention of *Winland*, attributed to King Sweyn Estridsson of Denmark — an island in the ocean where vines grow wild.
   - **Ari Þorgilsson**, *Íslendingabók* (c. 1122–1133), ch. 6: states that Erik the Red settled Greenland 14 or 15 winters before Iceland adopted Christianity (c. 985–986), and that settlers found traces of earlier habitation by the same people who lived in Vinland, whom the Greenlanders called *Skrælingjar*.
   - **Icelandic Annals** (*Skálholt* and *Flatey* annals, entry for **1347**): record a small Greenlandic ship with 17 or 18 men returning from *Markland* (Labrador) with timber, blown off course to Iceland — evidence that Greenlanders continued sailing to North America for timber centuries after c. 1000.
2. **The Vinland Sagas** (written in Iceland in the 13th century, preserving oral traditions over two centuries old):
   - ***Grœnlendinga saga*** (*The Saga of the Greenlanders*, in *Flateyjarbók*): credits Bjarni Herjólfsson with sighting the lands first; describes five voyages (Bjarni, Leif, Thorvald, Thorfinn Karlsefni, Freydís); names the main base *Leifsbúðir*.
   - ***Eiríks saga rauða*** (*The Saga of Erik the Red*, in *Hauksbók* and *Skálholtsbók*): omits Bjarni, has Leif discover Vinland by accident on his way from Norway, and folds the main exploration into one large expedition led by Thorfinn Karlsefni (*Straumfjörðr* and *Hóp*).
   - ***Landnámabók*** (*Book of Settlements*) and ***Eyrbyggja saga*** corroborate the genealogies of Erik the Red, Herjólf Bárdarson, Gudrid, Karlsefni, and Snorri Thorbrandsson.
3. **Archaeology:**
   - **L'Anse aux Meadows** (northern tip of Newfoundland's Great Northern Peninsula), excavated by Helge and Anne Stine Ingstad (1960–1968) and later Parks Canada / Birgitta Linderoth Wallace, is the **only undisputed Norse settlement site in North America** outside Greenland.
   - **Exact date:** Kuitems, Wallace et al. (*Nature*, 2022 [online Oct 2021]) used the AD 992–993 cosmic-ray radiocarbon spike in cut wood from L'Anse aux Meadows to prove Norse woodcutting there in **AD 1021**.

## 2. The Thing (Norse Council)

Like Iceland, Norse Greenland organized public law and dispute resolution around an assembly, the **Thing** (*þing*; a general assembly *alþing* is attested in later Greenlandic records, e.g. a 1389 document). Early authority centred on Erik the Red's estate at **Brattahlíð** (and later at **Garðar**, the bishopric from 1124/1126, where assembly-booth remains have been excavated). Greenland remained a self-governing commonwealth of chieftains (*goðar*) until submitting to the Norwegian Crown in 1261–1262.

**Portraits (T9):** No contemporary likeness exists for any 10th–11th-century Norse figure; council icons use emblems or period-dress silhouettes rather than invented portraits.

### Proposed Members (18 figures across 5 categories)

#### Seafaring & Navigation (`seafaring`)
1. **Bjarni Herjólfsson** (*Bjarni Herjólfsson*, fl. c. 986) — *Grœnlendinga saga*. Icelandic merchant blown off course sailing to Greenland; sighted three unfamiliar lands to the west without landing, prompting Leif's voyage. *Saga tradition.*
2. **Leif Erikson** (*Leifr Eiríksson*, c. 970–c. 1020) — *Grœnlendinga saga*, *Eiríks saga rauða*, *Íslendingabók* (lineage). Son of Erik the Red; led the first intentional voyage to Helluland, Markland, and Vinland (*Leifsbúðir*); called *Leifr inn heppni* ("the Lucky"). *Almost certainly historical as a Greenlandic chieftain; voyage details differ between sagas.*
3. **Bjarni Grímólfsson** (*Bjarni Grímólfsson*, fl. c. 1005–1015) — *Eiríks saga rauða*. Icelandic merchant-captain who sailed with 40 men alongside Karlsefni; went down with his shipworm-holed vessel after giving his place in the ship's boat to a younger crewman. *Saga tradition.*
4. **Thorstein Eiriksson** (*Þorsteinn Eiríksson*, d. c. 1005–1010) — *Grœnlendinga saga*, *Eiríks saga rauða*. Son of Erik the Red and first husband of Gudrid; led a storm-tossed expedition that failed to reach Vinland, returning to the Western Settlement (*Lýsufjörðr*) where he died of a winter epidemic. *Saga tradition.*

#### Scouting, Timber & Craft (`craft`)
5. **Thorvald Eiriksson** (*Þorvaldr Eiríksson*, d. c. 1004–1007) — *Grœnlendinga saga*, *Eiríks saga rauða*. Brother of Leif; explored coastal Vinland, repaired a broken ship's keel at *Kjalarnes*, and was killed by an arrow in an encounter with Indigenous peoples. *Saga tradition; keel repair parallels ship-repair evidence at L'Anse aux Meadows.*
6. **Thorhall the Hunter** (*Þórhallr veiðimaðr*, fl. c. 1005–1015) — *Eiríks saga rauða*. Erik the Red's longtime wilderness hunter (*Norðrsetur*); accompanied Karlsefni to *Straumfjörðr* before splitting north and being blown to Ireland. *Saga tradition; reflects real Greenlandic specialist hunters.*
7. **Tyrkir** (*Tyrkir*, fl. c. 1000) — *Grœnlendinga saga*. "Southerner" (*suðrmaðr*, usually interpreted as German) foster-father in Leif's household who found wild vines/grapes inland, giving Vinland its name. *Saga tradition.*
8. **Haki and Hekja** (*Haki ok Hekja*, fl. c. 1005–1015; plural) — *Eiríks saga rauða*. Two Gaelic runners lent to Karlsefni; sent ashore south of *Furðustrandir* for three days to scout the interior, returning with grapes and wild grain. *Saga tradition; reflects Gaelic servants and settlers in Iceland and Greenland.*

#### Homestead & Kin (`homestead`)
9. **Gudrid Thorbjarnardóttir** (*Guðríðr Þorbjarnardóttir*, c. 980–c. 1050) — *Eiríks saga rauða*, *Grœnlendinga saga*, *Landnámabók*. Migrated from Iceland to Greenland, married Thorstein and then Thorfinn Karlsefni, lived three years in Vinland, bore Snorri there, later farmed at Glaumbær and made a pilgrimage to Rome. *Firmly attested in Icelandic genealogies; Vinland stay is saga tradition.*
10. **Snorri Thorfinnsson** (*Snorri Þorfinnsson*, b. c. 1005–1013) — *Grœnlendinga saga*, *Eiríks saga rauða*, *Landnámabók*. Son of Karlsefni and Gudrid, recorded as born in Vinland; ancestor of three medieval Icelandic bishops. *Historically attested progenitor; birthplace is saga tradition.*
11. **Thjodhild** (*Þjóðhildr Jörundardóttir*, fl. c. 985–1005) — *Eiríks saga rauða*, *Landnámabók*; corroborated archaeologically. Wife of Erik the Red; built a small turf-and-wood church (*Þjóðhildarkirkja*) at Brattahlíð c. 1000, excavated in the 1960s.

#### Trade & Ventures (`trade`)
12. **Thorfinn Karlsefni** (*Þorfinnr karlsefni Þórðarson*, fl. c. 1005–1020) — *Grœnlendinga saga*, *Eiríks saga rauða*, *Landnámabók*. Wealthy Icelandic merchant who led the largest documented Vinland colonization attempt (60–160 people and livestock); bartered red cloth and dairy for furs at *Hóp*, forbade trading iron weapons, and withdrew after hostilities. *Historically attested in genealogies; Vinland enterprise is saga tradition.*
13. **Thorhall Gamlason** (*Þórhallr Gamlason*, fl. c. 1005–1015) — *Eiríks saga rauða* (also noted in *Grettis saga* as "the Vinlander"). Icelandic merchant who co-captained a 40-man trading vessel alongside Karlsefni.
14. **Helgi and Finnbogi** (*Helgi ok Finnbogi*, fl. c. 1010–1015; plural) — *Grœnlendinga saga*. Two Icelandic merchant brothers who entered a joint timber-and-trade partnership (*félag*) to Vinland with Freydís. *Saga tradition.*
15. **Freydís Eiríksdóttir** (*Freydís Eiríksdóttir*, fl. c. 1005–1020) — *Grœnlendinga saga* and *Eiríks saga rauða* (contrasting portrayals: rallying defenders at *Hóp* in *Eiríks saga*; orchestrating a violent seizure of Helgi and Finnbogi's ship in *Grœnlendinga saga*). *Saga tradition.*

#### Law & Settlement (`law`)
16. **Erik the Red** (*Eiríkr rauði Þorvaldsson*, c. 950–c. 1003) — *Íslendingabók*, *Landnámabók*, both Vinland sagas. Explored and named Greenland and led the c. 985–986 colonization fleet (25 ships sailed, 14 arrived), establishing Brattahlíð. *Firmly attested historical founder.*
17. **Herjólf Bárdarson** (*Herjólfr Bárðarson*, fl. c. 985–1000) — *Landnámabók*, *Grœnlendinga saga*. Founding Greenlandic chieftain who settled *Herjólfsnes* (Ikigaat), the southern landfall harbour; father of Bjarni Herjólfsson. *Historical founder; Herjólfsnes is excavated.*
18. **Thorbjörn Vífilsson** (*Þorbjörn Vífilsson*, fl. c. 985–1005) — *Eiríks saga rauða*, *Landnámabók*. Icelandic chieftain who led his household to Greenland during hard years and settled at Stokkanes; father of Gudrid.

## 3. Geography and Place Names

- **Greenland (Home port / expanded map edge):**
  - **Eastern Settlement (*Eystribyggð*):** Southwestern tip — **Brattahlíð** (Qassiarsuk, Erik's estate), **Garðar** (Igaliku, later bishopric & Thing site), **Herjólfsnes** (Ikigaat, main landfall harbour), Hvalsey, Stokkanes.
  - **Western Settlement (*Vestribyggð*):** Near modern Nuuk — **Sandnes** (*Lýsufjörðr*, where Thorstein wintered).
- **North American landfalls (north to south):**
  - **Helluland** ("Land of Flat Stones"): Baffin Island (Dorset / Tuniit).
  - **Markland** ("Forest Land"): Labrador coast (ancestors of the Innu; timber voyages attested to 1347).
  - **Vinland** ("Wine Land" or "Pasture Land"): Northern Newfoundland (L'Anse aux Meadows) as gateway into the Gulf of St. Lawrence (New Brunswick / PEI / Nova Scotia, where wild grapes and butternuts grew).
- **Norse colony names:** Attested saga names first (*Leifsbúðir*, *Straumfjörðr*, *Hóp*, *Kjalarnes*, *Krossanes*, *Furðustrandir*, *Straumey*, *Bjarney*), followed by Old Norse topographic patterns (*Skógarvík*, *Járnnes*, *Viðarhólmr*, *Laxá*, *Hvítanes*, *Eyjafjörðr*, *Breiðavík*, *Grænabúðir*).

## 4. Archaeology, Economy, and Why the Norse Left

- **L'Anse aux Meadows:** Eight turf-and-timber buildings (three halls, four workshops/boathouses, one bog-iron smithy); peak population **~60–90** (Birgitta Wallace); a soapstone spindle whorl confirms women were present.
- **Economy:** Bog-iron smelting for **ship rivets and nails**, ship repair and iron-axe carpentry (wood dated to **AD 1021**), and southern exploration into the Gulf of St. Lawrence (proved by **butternuts** recovered at L'Anse aux Meadows).
- **Why the Norse left:** Tiny home population in Greenland (a few hundred c. 1000, peaking at ~2,000–4,000 later); hazardous Davis Strait crossing and scarce ships; larger, established Indigenous populations who resisted intrusion; timber could be fetched more cheaply on short summer voyages to Markland (Labrador) without maintaining a permanent Vinland base.

## 5. Encounters with Indigenous Peoples

Around AD 1000 the region's peoples included the **Dorset** (Tuniit; Baffin Island & northern Labrador), ancestors of the **Beothuk** (Little Passage complex; Newfoundland), ancestors of the **Innu** (Labrador & North Shore), and ancestors of the **Mi'kmaq** (Gulf of St. Lawrence & Maritimes).

- **Archaeology:** Pre- and post-Norse Indigenous occupations at L'Anse aux Meadows; Norse metalwork and woven woollen cloth found across Dorset and later Thule sites in Arctic Canada; the Norwegian **Maine Penny** (minted 1067–1093, found at the Goddard site in Maine) reached New England via Indigenous trade networks.
- **Saga accounts:** Trade of red cloth and dairy for furs (iron weapons forbidden), followed by violent clashes at *Krossanes* and *Hóp* that convinced the Norse they could not hold a permanent colony against far more numerous neighbours.
