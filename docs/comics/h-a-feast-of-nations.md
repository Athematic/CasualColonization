# A Feast of Nations

**Logline.** Two small rival colonies, one in blue coats and one in green, each want the Lenape town at the ford to trade with them alone, and their wandering cows keep trampling the Lenape cornfields while the governors blame each other. The town's elder invites everyone to a harvest feast on the meadow between them, at one long table. When both herds crash the party, it takes three children, two bands and every guest to round them up, and to see that the gap in the fence is the very place where the two quarrels meet.

**Cover moment.** At the long table under the autumn trees, Elder Mahtekan and the two governors raise wooden cups together over the turkey, corn, pumpkins and bread, children running between the benches and the fiddler and drummer playing, with the cream houses, the log houses and the bark houses standing side by side along the river behind. It appears on **page 11**.

**Setting.** A meadow on the Lenapewihittuk (the Delaware River) in Lenapehoking, the Lenape homeland, between the Lenape town at the ford and two young colonies. October 1702.

**Cast.**
- **Elder Mahtekan**: elder and speaker of the Lenape town at the ford. Long black hair streaked with grey, two eagle feathers, fringed tan deerskin shirt, shell necklace, red patterned woven sash. Patient, wily, enjoys a good joke at the right moment.
- **Ahkiwen**: clan mother of the town, Mahtekan's sister. Tan dress, woven sash, silver earrings. Runs the fields, the feast and, in practice, everyone.
- **Nunapi** (10): Mahtekan's granddaughter. Tan deerskin dress, red woven sash, shell necklace, long hair flying. Fast, fearless, a natural leader.
- **Pakuk** (10): Lenape boy, her cousin. Bare-chested in leggings and a red sash, necklace. Best cow-whisperer on the river, to his own surprise.
- **Governor Pieter Haverkamp**: governor of Haverhoek, the blue colony (cream houses, red roofs). Big, bearded, broad black hat with a white plume, blue coat with cream facings, lace cravat. Loud, generous, loves food.
- **Governor Anders Lindqvist**: governor of Lindholm, the green colony (log houses). Young, clean-shaven, black tricorn edged in gold, dark green coat with yellow facings. Stiff and correct, secretly shy.
- **Joost Haverkamp** (10): Pieter's son. Blue coat, cream breeches, fair hair. Nunapi's best friend and partner in mischief.
- **Klaas**: fiddler of Haverhoek, blue coat, tricorn.
- **Erik**: drummer of Lindholm, green coat, tricorn, a big drum.

*(The Lenape personal names are invented for this story. Confirm with a Lenape language consultant before lettering. "Wanishi" is "thank you" in Lenape.)*

## PAGE 1

1. Wide opening panel. Dawn mist on the river meadow. A Lenape cornfield, the corn stalks tall and dry, pumpkins and squash ripening between them. Bursting through the stalks: a dozen cows, half with blue ribbons tied to their horns, half with green.
   CAPTION: Autumn on the great river. The corn was ripe, the squash was fat, and the cows had found both.
   SFX: MOOO! CRUNCH CRUNCH

2. Nunapi and Joost, running flat out through the corn, waving their arms.
   NUNAPI: GET OUT! Out of my grandmother's squash!
   JOOST: Shoo! SHOO! Go home! ...Whichever home you belong to!

3. A blue-ribboned cow, unbothered, chews a whole pumpkin with great satisfaction.
   SFX: CRONCH

4. Pakuk walks calmly up to a green-ribboned cow, scratches it behind the ear, and it follows him out of the field like a dog. Nunapi and Joost stop, astonished.
   PAKUK: You shout. I scratch. Cows are like uncles.

5. The three children survey the damage: flattened stalks, broken pumpkins, hoof marks everywhere.
   JOOST: That's the third time this month.
   NUNAPI: And every time, your father says it's the green cows, and their governor says it's the blue cows.

## PAGE 2

1. The edge of the field, mid-morning. Ahkiwen stands with arms folded among the wrecked plants. Beside her, Elder Mahtekan crouches, turning a broken squash in his hands. Opposite them, the two governors have arrived and are already arguing, hats nearly touching.
   HAVERKAMP: Your cows, Lindqvist! I saw green ribbons!
   LINDQVIST: Your fence, Haverkamp! It has a hole you could sail a ship through!

2. Close on the two governors, nose to nose, each jabbing a finger.
   HAVERKAMP: My fence ends at the creek!
   LINDQVIST: So does mine! At the OTHER side of the creek!

3. Ahkiwen, unimpressed, speaks to her brother in a low voice without looking at the governors.
   AHKIWEN: Every month the same song. And each of them comes to our town with gifts, asking us to trade only with him.
   MAHTEKAN: Yes. They are like two dogs who both want the same bone and keep standing on it.

4. Mahtekan stands, sets down the squash and raises a hand. The governors fall silent.
   MAHTEKAN: In seven days, when the harvest is in, our town will hold a feast on the meadow between your towns.
   MAHTEKAN: You are both invited. Your people too. One table.

5. The two governors glance at each other, suspicious, each instantly calculating.
   HAVERKAMP (thought): A feast! A chance to impress them. Exclusive trade by spring!
   LINDQVIST (thought): A feast. If I am the most correct guest, they will surely choose us.

6. Both governors bow at the same moment, very deeply.
   HAVERKAMP AND LINDQVIST: We accept!
   AHKIWEN (aside, to Mahtekan): You did not tell them which end of the table.
   MAHTEKAN: No. I did not.

## PAGE 3

1. A split panel. Left: in Haverhoek, the cream-walled bakehouse, Haverkamp watches his baker slide an absurdly huge round loaf from the oven. Right: in Lindholm, among the log houses, Lindqvist inspects a row of tablecloths with a measuring stick.
   HAVERKAMP: Bigger! The Lenape must see that Haverhoek knows how to eat!
   LINDQVIST: A finger's width out of line. Again!

2. Meanwhile at the Lenape town. Bark-covered longhouses, drying racks, women and men preparing food on a grand scale: corn being pounded in a wooden mortar, beans and squash cooking in big pots, venison smoking, turkeys being plucked. Ahkiwen directs it all.
   CAPTION: At the Lenape town, people simply got on with cooking for three villages.
   AHKIWEN: Corn, beans and squash together, the three sisters. They grow better side by side. So do people, if you let them.

3. Under a tree, Nunapi, Joost and Pakuk sit with a stick, drawing a map in the dirt.
   NUNAPI: Here is the blue fence. Here is the green fence. Here's the creek in the middle.
   JOOST: And here's where the cows go through.

4. Close on the dirt map: two fence lines ending on either side of the creek, with a wide gap between them.
   PAKUK: Nobody's fence reaches the creek. Each governor says the creek is in his land, so the other one must not build there.
   NUNAPI: So nobody builds there. And the cows walk right through the middle of the argument.

5. The three children look up at each other with the same grin.
   JOOST: We have to tell them.
   NUNAPI: They won't listen to us. They don't even listen to each other.
   PAKUK: Then they need to see it.

## PAGE 4

1. Evening, at Mahtekan's longhouse door. Haverkamp arrives alone, glancing over his shoulder, carrying something bulky under a cloth.
   HAVERKAMP (whisper): A small gift, honoured Elder. Between friends. To remember Haverhoek by when you think of... trading partners.

2. He whips away the cloth: a shining new iron kettle. Mahtekan accepts it with grave politeness.
   MAHTEKAN: Wanishi. It is a very fine kettle.

3. One hour later, same doorway, lit by a lantern. Lindqvist arrives alone, glancing over his shoulder, carrying something bulky under a cloth.
   LINDQVIST (whisper): Honoured Elder. A correct and proper gift, from Lindholm.

4. Lindqvist removes the cloth: an identical shining new iron kettle. Mahtekan accepts it with exactly the same grave politeness.
   MAHTEKAN: Wanishi. It is also a very fine kettle.

5. Inside the longhouse. Mahtekan sets the two kettles side by side near the fire. Ahkiwen looks at them and laughs out loud.
   AHKIWEN: Two kettles. Two governors. Each paying us not to talk to the other.
   MAHTEKAN: One kettle is good for cooking corn. But a feast for three towns needs two kettles side by side over the same fire.
   MAHTEKAN: They just don't know yet that they have bought the same feast.

## PAGE 5

1. Big panel. The feast day. Bright October sun, red, orange and gold trees. A long trestle table runs diagonally across the meadow, benches on both sides, laden with food. People from all three towns arrive from three directions: blue coats from the cream houses, green coats from the log houses, Lenape families from the bark longhouses.
   CAPTION: On the day of the feast, the meadow was full.

2. Near the middle of the table, Lindqvist stops dead and stares at the ground. A wooden boundary stake, painted half blue and half green, sticks up right under the table.
   LINDQVIST: Haverkamp. Your bench is in Lindholm.
   HAVERKAMP: And yours is in Haverhoek. Hah! Well, it's only for the afternoon.

3. Close on Mahtekan, at the far end, watching this with the tiniest smile.
   AHKIWEN (beside him): You put the table there on purpose.
   MAHTEKAN: The meadow put it there. I only carried one end.

4. At the head of the table, a sticky moment. Both governors reach for the single big chair at the same time.
   HAVERKAMP: The head of the table, naturally, for the senior colony—
   LINDQVIST: —for the correctly established colony—

5. Mahtekan sits down calmly on an ordinary bench halfway along, among everyone.
   MAHTEKAN: A long table has two heads, and no head. Sit where the food is.
   Both governors look at the big chair, then at each other, then sit on the benches, embarrassed.

## PAGE 6

1. Klaas the fiddler (blue) strikes up a jolly jig at one end. At the same moment Erik the drummer (green) begins a stately march at the other end. The two tunes clash horribly in the middle.
   SFX: FIDDLE-DEE-DEE-DEE / BOOM. BOOM. BOOM.

2. Guests in the middle of the table, blue, green and Lenape alike, wince with their hands over their ears.
   LENAPE GUEST: Is it a war of music?
   GREEN COLONIST: I think it's a war of music.

3. Ahkiwen marches over, takes Erik's drumstick and taps a quick, steady beat on the drum, nodding at Klaas to follow it. Slowly, both men fall into the same rhythm.
   AHKIWEN: One heartbeat. Then play whatever you like on top of it.
   SFX: BOM-ba BOM-ba / FIDDLE-DEE-DEE

4. The food is served, the Lenape way, guests first. Ahkiwen offers Lindqvist a bowl of squash. Lindqvist, very formal, raises a hand and declines with a bow.
   LINDQVIST: Oh, no, no. I could not possibly.
   Ahkiwen's face goes still. Nearby Lenape hosts look offended.

5. Nunapi, sitting with the children, leans over to Lindqvist and whispers urgently.
   NUNAPI: When you say no to the first bowl here, it means you don't like her cooking.
   LINDQVIST (going pale): Oh! In Lindholm it is only good manners to refuse the first time—

6. Lindqvist seizes the bowl and eats a huge spoonful with tremendous enthusiasm. Ahkiwen, satisfied, gives him a second bowl at once.
   LINDQVIST (mouth full): Delicious! Magnificent! Two bowls! Thank you! Wanishi!
   HAVERKAMP (quietly impressed): He learned a word.

## PAGE 7

1. Joost, Nunapi and Pakuk, at the edge of the meadow, look towards the creek. Their faces drop.
   JOOST: Uh oh.

2. Wide panel. Down at the creek gap, BOTH herds, blue ribbons and green, have smelled the apples and pumpkins. They are coming through the gap at a trot, all together, heading straight for the feast.
   SFX: MOOO MOOOO MOOOOO
   SFX: THUD THUD THUD THUD

3. At the table, a cow's head pushes between two guests and takes an apple straight out of a bowl. A Lenape grandmother and a green-coated woman stare at it, then at each other.
   SFX: CHOMP

4. Chaos panel. Benches tipping over, a cow munching bread, a blue colonist running with a pie, a green colonist holding a basket of corn above his head, children shrieking with laughter, a calf licking a governor's hat.
   HAVERKAMP: The TURKEY! Save the turkey!

5. Haverkamp throws himself full length across the table and wraps his arms round the roast turkey just as a cow's nose reaches it.
   SFX: WHUMPF
   HAVERKAMP (muffled): Not this one, madam!

## PAGE 8

1. Nunapi jumps up onto a bench and takes command, pointing in all directions.
   NUNAPI: Don't chase them! They'll scatter! Pakuk, take the leader cow! Joost, apples!

2. Pakuk walks calmly up to the oldest cow, the one with the biggest bell, and scratches her ears. She sighs and leans on him like an old friend.
   PAKUK: There, Auntie. There.

3. Joost, holding an apple out in each hand, walks backwards slowly. The leader cow follows Pakuk, and the rest of the herd begins to follow her.
   JOOST: Come on, cows. Lovely apples. Follow the apples.

4. Wide panel. Everyone helps: blue coats, green coats and Lenape men and women form a long, wide funnel of people with arms held out, walking slowly, guiding the herd away from the table towards the Lenape town's empty, harvested field, where Ahkiwen holds open a gap in a fence of woven saplings.
   AHKIWEN: This way! Our field is empty now. Let them eat the stalks.

5. Klaas and Erik, without being asked, walk behind the herd playing a slow, swaying tune together. The cows sway along with it.
   SFX: BOM-ba BOM-ba / laa-dee-daa
   ERIK: They like it better than the march.
   KLAAS: Everyone likes it better than the march.

6. Small panel. The last cow walks into the field. Ahkiwen closes the gate behind it. All three villages cheer.
   SFX: HURRAAH!

## PAGE 9

1. Back at the feast table, which is a happy wreck: one bench broken, bread nibbled, apples gone. Everyone is muddy and laughing. Haverkamp sits proudly with the rescued turkey in his lap.
   HAVERKAMP: The turkey lives!

2. Lindqvist, his gold-edged tricorn chewed at one corner, actually laughs for the first time in the story.
   LINDQVIST: My hat does not. Ha! Ha ha!
   HAVERKAMP (staring): Lindqvist. You laughed.
   LINDQVIST: I am as surprised as you are.

3. Joost, Nunapi and Pakuk step up to the table, Nunapi holding a stick.
   NUNAPI: Governors. We need to show you something. Now. While you're both still laughing.

4. Down at the creek. The children, the two governors, Mahtekan and Ahkiwen stand at the gap. The blue fence ends on one side; the green fence ends on the other; trampled mud and hoof prints run straight between them.
   JOOST: Your fence stops here, Father.
   PAKUK: And yours stops there, Governor Lindqvist.
   NUNAPI: And here, in the middle, is your argument. The cows walk right through it.

5. The two governors look at the gap, then at each other, then at the gap again. Long silence.
   HAVERKAMP: ...Each of us thought the other would build it.
   LINDQVIST: Each of us thought building it would mean the creek belonged to the other.

6. Mahtekan steps between them.
   MAHTEKAN: The creek belongs to the creek. The gap is where your two quarrels meet. So close it together.

## PAGE 10

1. Back at the table. Mahtekan stands and unrolls a long belt of wampum beads: white shell beads with a design in dark purple of three human figures holding hands, a straight path joining them.
   CAPTION: Among the Lenape, an agreement of importance was remembered in a belt of shell beads.

2. Close on the belt, Mahtekan's finger moving along it.
   MAHTEKAN: Here is our town. Here is Haverhoek. Here is Lindholm. Three people, holding hands. And here, the path between them.

3. Mahtekan turns to the governors, firm now, the speaker of his town.
   MAHTEKAN: These are our words. You will build the fence at the creek together, so your cows stay out of our fields.
   MAHTEKAN: We will not trade with one of you only. We will trade with both, at the ford, at the same fair prices. And you two will trade with each other as friends, not as rivals.

4. Haverkamp and Lindqvist look at each other. Haverkamp sticks out his big hand. Lindqvist hesitates, then shakes it firmly.
   HAVERKAMP: Haverhoek and Lindholm, favoured friends. Our ships carry your goods, your mill grinds our corn.
   LINDQVIST: And both of us, friends of the town at the ford. Agreed. Correctly and properly agreed.

5. Small panel. Ahkiwen, aside, places both identical iron kettles next to each other on the table.
   AHKIWEN: Good. Because we were going to give you your kettles back.

## PAGE 11

*(Cover moment.)*

1. LARGE PANEL, two-thirds of the page, matching the cover. In the foreground at the long table, Elder Mahtekan on the left, Governor Haverkamp in the centre with his plumed hat, and Governor Lindqvist on the right in his green coat raise wooden cups together over the roast turkey, corn, squash, a great pumpkin, bread and a bowl of red apples. The table runs diagonally back into the meadow, lined with blue coats, green coats and Lenape guests sharing food. Nunapi, Joost and Pakuk run between the benches. Klaas and Erik play together at the left. Red and gold trees on either side. In the distance along the river, the cream houses of Haverhoek, the log houses of Lindholm and the bark longhouses of the Lenape town, smoke rising, and a ship at anchor.
   MAHTEKAN: To one table.
   HAVERKAMP: To one fence!
   LINDQVIST: To... one turkey, intact!

2. Close on three cups knocking together.
   SFX: TOK!

3. The children, flopped on the grass by the table, exhausted and happy.
   JOOST: Do you think they'll remember all this tomorrow?
   NUNAPI: That's what the belt is for. My grandfather will make them look at it every year.
   PAKUK: And the cows will remind them.

## PAGE 12

1. The next morning at the creek. Wide panel: blue-coated colonists, green-coated colonists and Lenape men and women together dig post holes and set rails across the gap, the two fences finally joined. The two governors carry one long rail between them, one at each end.
   CAPTION: The fence at the creek was built in a single morning, by three towns.

2. Haverkamp, at one end of the rail, puffs and grins. Lindqvist, at the other end, has taken off his chewed hat and rolled up his sleeves.
   HAVERKAMP: I never thought I'd carry anything with you, Lindqvist.
   LINDQVIST: Mind your end, please. It is drifting a hand's width out of line.

3. Ahkiwen inspects the work and taps the new rail.
   AHKIWEN: Strong. But you forgot one thing.
   MAHTEKAN: A fence between friends needs a gate in it. Otherwise how will anyone come to dinner?

4. Nunapi, Joost and Pakuk swing proudly on a new wooden gate set in the fence. On its post they have tied a blue ribbon, a green ribbon and a strip of red Lenape sash, knotted together.
   NUNAPI: Our gate. For visiting.
   JOOST: And for apples.

5. FINAL PANEL, wide. The joined fence and its gate across the creek, the three villages in the background under the autumn trees, people heading home in all three directions. In the foreground, on the far side of the fence, the old leader cow, Auntie, stands with her chin resting on the top rail, gazing longingly at the meadow. She is chewing, very slowly, the end of the blue-green-red ribbon from the gatepost.
   CAPTION: And all three towns agreed it was the finest feast the river had ever seen.
   CAPTION: Even the cows.
