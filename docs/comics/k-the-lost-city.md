# The Lost City

**Logline:** In the hills behind the Brazilian coast, a Tupi hunter who needs to know why his village's river is failing, a weathered scout and a young cartographer follow an old rumour to an overgrown stone city. A rival who wants to sell its carved stones comes too. When a cloudburst floods the ruins, the three of them save everyone by reading the ancient builders' water channels, and the river runs again.

**Cover moment:** Piatã holds back the curtain of vines and Tomé and Inês see the city for the first time, with a shaft of sun on the great stair, macaws on the wall and the waterfall at the right. It is the full-width splash at the bottom of **page 4** (panel 5).

**Setting:** Forested hills about twelve days' walk inland from a small Portuguese-speaking colony on the coast of Brazil, in the dry season of 1641. The city is invented: the work of a people long gone, whose name nobody remembers.

**Cast**
- **Piatã:** a young Tupi hunter from the village of Ybytu, by the River of Small Stones. Straight black hair cut at the shoulders, a red headband, a necklace of animal teeth, a white waist cloth with a red-and-white woven sash, a net bag on a cord across his chest. Quick, dry humour; he walks in the forest as other people walk down a street.
- **Tomé Ferraz:** a colonial scout in his forties. A grey felt hat with a white feather, a curly brown beard, a sun-faded blue coat, a crossed pair of leather straps and a satchel. Kind, stubborn, rather too fond of his own stories.
- **Inês Alva:** a cartographer of about sixteen, Tomé's niece. Black tricorn over brown hair tied back, a red coat that is slightly too big (it was her father's), a brass telescope and a map board under her arm. Precise, curious, keeps a pencil behind her ear.
- **Duarte Pinhal:** a merchant from the coast. Plum-coloured coat, lace cuffs gone grey with mud, a small pointed beard, a parasol that he will not give up. With him are two hired porters, **Bento** (tall, silent, hungry) and **Lopo** (short, nervous, talkative).
- **Grandmother Jacira:** an elder of Ybytu, white-haired and sharp, with a long carrying basket and a laugh like a parrot's.
- **Two scarlet macaws,** who follow the expedition for no reason anyone can explain.

## PAGE 2

**Panel 1.** Piatã sits at Tomé and Inês's table. He has put a small clay jar of water on the map. Inês looks at it, puzzled.
PIATÃ: This is water from the River of Small Stones. My village drinks it, waters its fields, fishes in it.
PIATÃ: This dry season it is half what it was. Last year, half again.

**Panel 2.** Close on Piatã's finger tracing the river upstream on the blank map into empty paper.
PIATÃ: The river comes out of the hills. Something up there has changed. My grandmother says the old stone houses are up there, where the water is born.
INÊS: Stone houses? You mean the lost city is *real*?

**Panel 3.** Piatã shrugs, amused.
PIATÃ: It is not lost. It is only far. Nobody has needed to go there.
PIATÃ: Now we need to. And you need a map. I need someone who can draw how the water runs. It's a fair trade.

**Panel 4.** Tomé and Piatã clasp forearms across the table. Inês is already sharpening her pencil.
TOMÉ: A fair trade. No gold-digging, no treasure-hunting. We go for the water.
PIATÃ: Good. There is no gold up there anyway. Only stones and mosquitoes.

**Panel 5.** Outside the window, Pinhal is talking to Bento and Lopo, pointing at the hills with his parasol. Lopo looks deeply unhappy.
PINHAL: We follow them. Quietly. When they find the city, we take what's worth taking.
LOPO: Quietly, senhor? With *that* parasol?

## PAGE 3

**Panel 1.** A wide panel of the forest trail at dawn: tall trunks, buttress roots, hanging lianas, ferns as tall as a man. The three walk in single file: Piatã first, Tomé, then Inês with her map board. Two scarlet macaws watch from a branch.
CAPTION: Twelve days into the hills.

**Panel 2.** Tomé swats at a cloud of mosquitoes, red-faced. Piatã, untroubled, rubs a crushed leaf on his arms and hands one to Tomé.
TOMÉ: How do they never bite *you*?
PIATÃ: They do. I just don't make so much noise about it.

**Panel 3.** A river crossing on stepping stones. Inês kneels on a rock with a string and a pebble, timing a floating leaf with a little sand-glass. Piatã watches with interest.
INÊS: Twenty paces in... nine grains. The current is slow here. Too slow for a river this wide.
PIATÃ: You measure the water with sand. My grandmother measures it with her feet. You get the same answer.

**Panel 4.** Night camp. Hammocks slung between trees, a small fire. Piatã shows Inês a dry, empty channel in the forest floor, lined with flat, squared stones under the moss.
PIATÃ: Look. Water used to come this way. Somebody laid these stones.
INÊS: A canal? Out here?

**Panel 5.** Behind them, far back in the dark trees, the glint of a lantern and a parasol tip. A macaw above them turns its head to look.
SFX (small, from the dark): AI! My foot!
TOMÉ (half asleep, in his hammock): Did you hear something?
PIATÃ: A tapir. A very clumsy tapir with a parasol.

## PAGE 5

**Panel 1.** The three walk slowly up the lowest terrace. Carved stones lie in the ferns: a broad face with a calm expression, a stone with a pattern of waves.
TOMÉ: Who built all this?
PIATÃ: People who lived here before my grandmother's grandmother's grandmother. They left. The forest moved in.

**Panel 2.** Inês sits on a step with her board, drawing fast. Her sketch shows terraces and lines of channels, all running towards the pool below the waterfall.
INÊS: Look at the pattern. Every terrace has a channel. They all lead down to one stone gate under the pool.
INÊS: The whole city is a machine for sharing water.

**Panel 3.** Piatã kneels at the edge of the green pool and puts his hand on a big stone gate, half open, with a dark slot below it. Water trickles through only a thin crack.
PIATÃ: And here is the River of Small Stones. Being born.
PIATÃ: Mostly *not* being born.

**Panel 4.** Wider. Above the gate, the hillside has slipped: a great slab of rock and a fallen tree have dropped into the main channel, sending most of the waterfall's water sideways into a gully that runs away to the north.
TOMÉ: A landslip. The rains last year must have brought it down.
INÊS: So the water is going the wrong way. Into another valley.

**Panel 5.** Piatã climbs up onto the slab, studies it, grins.
PIATÃ: Then we put it back the right way. Simple.
TOMÉ: That rock weighs as much as a ship.
PIATÃ: Then it's not simple. But it's still the plan.

## PAGE 10

**Panel 1.** Everyone, the three heroes and the three wet newcomers, stands below the landslip. The slab and fallen tree still block the main channel, though the storm has loosened them.

TOMÉ: Six of us. One ship-sized rock.

PIATÃ: Good. Then you push harder.

## PAGE 11

**Panel 1.** Days later. Wide panel of the village of Ybytu: large communal houses of poles and palm thatch around an open plaza, manioc gardens, canoes on a riverbank. The River of Small Stones runs full and fast. Children leap into it.

**Panel 2.** Grandmother Jacira, white hair, carrying basket on her back, stands thigh-deep in the river, laughing, holding Piatã's face in her hands.

PIATÃ: They found us, mostly. And a man with a parasol found the bottom of the pool.

JACIRA: There. And there. You drew the water well, but you forgot where the turtles sleep.
INÊS (writing fast): Turtles... sleep... here.

**Panel 5.** Tomé and Piatã sit side by side on a log, feet in the river.
TOMÉ: In the port they'll ask me if we found the lost city. What do I say?
