# The Growing Colony

**Logline.** On the busiest day of the year, with the Crown's inspector on the wharf demanding his share before the evening tide, merchant Samuel Ashby's ledger, the only record of everything his harbour town has made and traded, falls into the sea. With the town hall bell ringing everyone together, a whole colony rebuilds its accounts from chalk marks, knots, notched rulers and a Lenape trader's cedar tally sticks, and discovers that a town that counts together can stand up for itself.

**Cover moment.** The crew swinging the cargo net of barrels ashore from the moored three-master while colonists roll barrels and stack bales on the wharf, Samuel checking his ledger, Prudence carrying a bale of cotton, Mattanee arriving with her basket, and the town of cream houses, church, workshop and belled town hall climbing the hill to the stockade with its blue pennant. It is the splash panel at the top of **page 2** (panel 1).

**Setting.** Haverly, a young harbour town on the northern Atlantic coast of North America, beside the lands of the Lenape. Late summer, 1712.

## Cast

- **Samuel Ashby**, merchant and keeper of the town's ledger. Thirties, clean-shaven, black tricorn, white shirt, cream waistcoat, dark breeches. Honest, tidy, slightly too proud of his handwriting.
- **Prudence Ashby**, his older sister and partner, who runs the warehouse. Blue dress, white apron, white linen cap. Practical, quick-tempered, quicker to laugh.
- **Mattanee**, a Lenape trader from the town upriver who brings furs, corn and baskets to Haverly. Forties, long black hair, fringed deerskin dress with a beaded belt, a large woven basket on her arm. Keeps her own accounts with notched cedar sticks, and they are never wrong.
- **Tamaqua**, Mattanee's son, about twelve. His name means "beaver", and he swims like one. Breechcloth and leggings, hair tied back.
- **Kit Bramble**, the wharf's errand boy, about thirteen. Flat black cap, white shirt, brown breeches, never stops running.
- **Inspector Gideon Thorne**, sent by the Crown to collect its share of the colony's trade. Fifties, grey wig, black coat, spectacles, a writing box under his arm. Strict, pompous, secretly in love with arithmetic.
- **Captain Ruth Kettering**, master of the merchant ship *Heron*. Weathered, practical, wants only to catch the evening tide.
- **The townsfolk**: Amos Cobb the cooper, Mistress Hale the miller, old Jethro the carpenter, the fishermen and the children of Haverly.

**Panel 1.** Wide opening panel. From high above the harbour: the town of Haverly in bright morning sun, cream timber houses with red and slate-blue roofs climbing a green hill, a church tower, a town hall with a bell in its cupola, a wooden stockade along the hilltop with a plain blue pennant. A three-masted merchant ship, sails being furled, glides towards the timber wharf.

CAPTION: Haverly. Ten years ago it was six cabins and a wish. Now it has a wharf, a church, a bell and a ledger.

**Panel 2.** In the cupola of the town hall, the bell swings.

SFX: DONG! DONG! DONG!

**Panel 3.** Kit Bramble sprints down the cobbled lane between houses, scattering chickens and a surprised cat.

KIT: SHIP IN! The *Heron's* in! SHIP IIIIN!

**Panel 4.** On the wharf, Samuel Ashby opens a large leather-bound ledger with great ceremony. Prudence, beside him, rolls her eyes.

SAMUEL: Everything Haverly has made and traded this year, in one book. Every barrel, every bale, every plank.

PRUDENCE: In very beautiful handwriting. He'll tell you so himself.

**Panel 5.** Close on the gangplank of the *Heron*: a pair of polished black buckled shoes stepping onto the wharf, and the bottom of a black coat.

CAPTION: But the *Heron* has brought a passenger.

## PAGE 2

**Panel 1.** Splash panel, top half of the page: THE COVER MOMENT. The busy wharf seen from slightly above, the stern and side of the *Heron* filling the left. Sailors in red and blue headscarves swing a cargo net of barrels ashore from the ship's yard; a dock worker reaches up to guide it. Colonists roll barrels and stack crates; bales of cotton for the weavers sit on the planks. Samuel checks his ledger; Prudence, behind him, carries a bale. Mattanee arrives at the right with her basket. Behind: the carpenter's open workshop with stacked lumber, a horse and cart, the church, the town hall with its bell, orchards and the stockade on the hill.

SFX (the net): CREEEAK

**Panel 2.** Inspector Gideon Thorne stands on the wharf, writing box under his arm, spectacles glinting. Samuel bows, slightly alarmed.

THORNE: Gideon Thorne, inspector for the Crown. I am here for the Crown's share: one barrel in every five of all goods made or traded in this colony this year.

**Panel 3.** Thorne holds out a hand for the ledger.

THORNE: Calculated, naturally, from your ledger. I sail with the *Heron* on the evening tide. Everything must be counted, agreed and loaded by then.

**Panel 4.** Mattanee, setting down her basket, watches Thorne with frank curiosity. She speaks quietly to Prudence.

MATTANEE: Your king sends a man across the whole sea to count your barrels?

PRUDENCE: Every year.

MATTANEE: Doesn't he have barrels of his own?

## PAGE 3

**Panel 1.** Samuel proudly hands the ledger towards Thorne. Behind them, unnoticed, the cargo net swings back from the ship, empty but heavy with rope.

SAMUEL: You will find everything in perfect order, Inspector. I have a very clear hand.

**Panel 2.** The net swings straight into Samuel's back.

SFX: WHUMP!

**Panel 3.** The ledger flies from Samuel's hands, spinning high over the edge of the wharf, pages flapping like wings. Everyone's eyes follow it.

KIT: The book!

PRUDENCE: SAMUEL!

**Panel 4.** The ledger hits the green water between the wharf and the ship's hull.

SFX: SPLOSH.

**Panel 5.** Samuel, Prudence, Kit, Mattanee and Thorne all kneel at the edge of the wharf, staring down into the deep water. Only a few bubbles rise.

SFX: blup... blup...

**Panel 6.** Close on Thorne, adjusting his spectacles, with grim satisfaction.

THORNE: How very unfortunate. Then I shall have to estimate.

## PAGE 4

**Panel 1.** Thorne strides along the wharf, pointing with his pen at everything and scribbling in a notebook as he goes. Samuel and Prudence hurry after him.

THORNE: Barrels: a great many. Let us say one thousand. Planks: a forest's worth. Bales: two hundred...

SAMUEL: Two hundred? We have forty!

**Panel 2.** Thorne points up the hill at the town hall.

THORNE: ...and a bronze bell. Bells are goods. One fifth of a bell.

PRUDENCE: You can't take a fifth of a bell!

THORNE: Then the Crown will accept the whole bell in exchange for change.

**Panel 3.** Close on Thorne's notebook: a long column of scribbled numbers (unreadable squiggles) ending in a huge, heavily underlined squiggle.

**Panel 4.** Thorne snaps the notebook shut and looks at the harbour clock (a sundial on a post).

THORNE: By my estimate, the Crown's share is everything in your warehouse, the bell and a cart. Unless, by the evening tide, you can show me a true account.

**Panel 5.** Samuel slumps on a barrel, head in hands. Kit and Prudence stand by him helplessly.

SAMUEL: A whole year's work. It was all in that book.

---

## PAGE 5

**Panel 1.** Mattanee sits down on the barrel next to Samuel and pats his shoulder, kind but brisk.

MATTANEE: Samuel. Your book is in the water. But your book was never the only book.

**Panel 2.** She opens her basket and takes out a bundle of slim cedar sticks tied with sinew, each one cut with rows of notches of different sizes.

MATTANEE: Every trade I make with you, I cut here. Big notch, a bundle of furs. Small notch, a basket of corn. This one is the spring. This one is the summer.

**Panel 3.** Close on Samuel looking at the sticks, a light dawning. Prudence grabs his arm.

PRUDENCE: She's right! Amos chalks every barrel he makes on his workshop door! Jethro notches his rule for every plank! The fishermen tie a knot in a cord for every catch!

**Panel 4.** Samuel leaps to his feet, ledger-less but inspired.

SAMUEL: The whole town has been keeping accounts all along. We just need to put them all in one place! KIT!

**Panel 5.** Kit, already running.

KIT: Ring the bell?

SAMUEL: RING THE BELL!

## PAGE 6

**Panel 1.** Kit hauls on the bell rope inside the town hall, his feet lifting off the floor with every swing.

SFX: DONG! DONG! DONG! DONG!

**Panel 2.** Wide montage of the town answering the bell: Amos Cobb the cooper carrying his whole workshop door on his back, covered in chalk marks; old Jethro the carpenter with a notched measuring rule as long as himself; Mistress Hale the miller with a bag of tally-pebbles; fishermen with long knotted cords; children with slates.

CAPTION: In Haverly, when the bell rings, everyone comes.

**Panel 3.** The town hall interior: a simple whitewashed hall with timber beams, benches, a long table. The townsfolk crowd in. Samuel stands on a bench with a fresh, empty book and a quill.

SAMUEL: Friends! The ledger is lost, the Inspector wants a true account by the evening tide, and every one of you knows a piece of it! Who's first?

**Panel 4.** Amos Cobb drops his workshop door onto the table with a crash. It is covered in rows of chalk strokes.

SFX: WHAMM!

AMOS: Barrels. Every one I made since spring. Four strokes and one across makes five, like my father taught me.

**Panel 5.** Thorne stands at the back of the hall, writing box under his arm, watching with deep suspicion.

THORNE: Chalk marks. On a door. Most irregular.

---

## PAGE 7

**Panel 1.** Wide, busy panel: the hall in full swing. Townsfolk call out counts; Prudence tallies chalk strokes; Kit counts fishermen's knots, his lips moving; Jethro lays his long rule on the table; Mattanee lays out her cedar sticks in neat rows by season. Samuel writes at speed.

**Panel 2.** Two old men glare at each other across a barrel.

FARMER: That cider barrel was mine! I made the cider!

INNKEEPER: It was mine! I bought it!

PRUDENCE: Then who drank it?

BOTH: (pause, sheepish) ...We both did.

**Panel 3.** Thorne has drifted to the table despite himself. He peers at the chalk door and Kit's knotted cord, lips moving.

THORNE: You've miscounted, boy. There are forty-three knots, not forty-two. Forty-three. You skipped the double one.

KIT: Oh. Thank you, sir.

**Panel 4.** Close on Thorne, who looks surprised at himself, then pulls up a stool and opens his writing box.

THORNE: Well. If this is to be done at all, it must be done properly. Give me the miller's pebbles.

**Panel 5.** Mattanee, sitting beside Thorne, explains her sticks. He bends close, fascinated.

MATTANEE: Big notch, furs. Small notch, corn. A cross, a kettle. Two crosses, two kettles.

THORNE: Remarkable. No ink to spill, no pages to blot. It cannot fall in the harbour and run.

MATTANEE: Oh, it can fall in the harbour. Cedar floats.

---

## PAGE 8

**Panel 1.** Mattanee runs her finger along the autumn stick and stops at a group of notches. She frowns slightly and looks at Samuel.

MATTANEE: Samuel. Last autumn, I brought twelve bundles of furs. You gave me ten kettles. We agreed twelve.

**Panel 2.** Close on Samuel, quill frozen, going red to the tips of his ears.

SAMUEL: I... (thinking hard) ...the ship was late. The kettles ran short. I meant to send the other two. And then the winter came, and... I forgot.

**Panel 3.** The hall goes quiet. Everyone looks at Samuel. Thorne raises an eyebrow over his spectacles.

THORNE: The keeper of the perfect ledger, owing his neighbour two kettles.

**Panel 4.** Samuel climbs down, goes straight to a crate by the wall, takes out two shiny copper kettles and sets them before Mattanee, with a bow.

SAMUEL: Two kettles, a year late. And a third, for the waiting. I'm sorry, Mattanee.

**Panel 5.** Mattanee nods, satisfied, and cuts a neat new notch across the old ones with a small knife.

MATTANEE: Now the stick is happy. And a happy stick is a true stick.

**Panel 6.** Close on Thorne, watching all this. Something in his stern face softens.

THORNE: (quietly) A merchant who pays back what he forgot, in front of the Crown's inspector. In thirty years, I have never written that down.

---

## PAGE 9

**Panel 1.** Late afternoon light through the hall windows. Samuel holds up the new book, now full. Thorne adds the final column with his own quill.

CAPTION: Late afternoon.

THORNE: Barrels, three hundred and twelve. Planks, eleven thousand. Bales, forty. One bell, not for sale.

**Panel 2.** Thorne taps the final figure.

THORNE: One in five makes the Crown's share sixty-two barrels and a bit, two thousand two hundred planks and eight bales. A great deal less than my estimate.

PRUDENCE: (muttering) A great deal less than our warehouse, a bell and a cart.

**Panel 3.** But Thorne hesitates, closing the book.

THORNE: However. I must report that this account was rebuilt from doors and knots and sticks in one afternoon. My superiors will not believe it. Without the original ledger, I cannot sign.

**Panel 4.** The townsfolk groan. Captain Kettering puts her head round the door.

KETTERING: Tide's turning in an hour, Inspector. I'm not waiting for anybody's arithmetic.

**Panel 5.** Close on Mattanee, looking towards the window and the harbour beyond, and then at a gap at the end of the bench where her son should be sitting.

MATTANEE: Where is Tamaqua? And where is Kit?

## PAGE 10

**Panel 1.** Wide. The harbour, under the wharf, the water now lower and clearer with the ebbing tide. Kit kneels on the edge of the wharf holding a coil of rope. Below, Tamaqua surfaces in the green water beside the *Heron's* hull, shaking water out of his hair.

KIT: Anything?

TAMAQUA: Lots of fish. One boot. And... wait.

**Panel 2.** Underwater panel, flat blues and greens: Tamaqua swims down among the wharf pilings, among small fish and swaying weed. Wedged between two pilings, the dark shape of the ledger in its leather cover.

**Panel 3.** Tamaqua bursts up from the water, the ledger held high above his head, grinning.

SFX: SPLOOSH!

TAMAQUA: Found it!

**Panel 4.** Kit and Tamaqua run up the hill lane together, dripping, the ledger held between them. Chickens scatter again; the same surprised cat.

KIT: Make way! MAKE WAY! Crown's business!

**Panel 5.** The town hall doors burst open. Everyone turns. Samuel's face lights up.

SAMUEL: My ledger!

MATTANEE: My son.

## PAGE 11

**Panel 1.** The ledger opened carefully on the long table. The pages are damp and wavy, but the ink, in Samuel's very beautiful handwriting (shown as neat squiggles only), is still clear. Samuel peers at it.

SAMUEL: Oil-dressed leather cover. I always said a good cover was worth paying for!

PRUDENCE: You said it was for looking handsome.

**Panel 2.** Thorne places the old ledger and the new book side by side and runs his finger down both columns at once, lips moving. Everyone holds their breath.

**Panel 3.** Close on Thorne, eyebrows rising.

THORNE: Barrels, the same. Planks, the same. Bales, the same. Mistress Hale's flour, the same to the sack. The town remembered everything... except one cider barrel.

**Panel 4.** The farmer and the innkeeper look at each other, then at the ceiling, whistling innocently.

**Panel 5.** Thorne, standing, signs the new book with a flourish, then the old one too.

THORNE: The Crown's share: sixty-two barrels, two thousand two hundred planks, eight bales. And I shall write in my report that the colony of Haverly keeps the most honest accounts I have ever seen. Twice.

**Panel 6.** Cheers. Hats thrown. Kit and Tamaqua are lifted onto the shoulders of the fishermen.

SFX: HURRAAAH!

---

## PAGE 12

**Panel 1.** The wharf at sunset. Sailors load the last of the Crown's share. Thorne, about to board the *Heron*, stops in front of Mattanee and holds out a coin, pointing at a small round basket in her large one.

THORNE: For my wife. She will ask what I saw here. I shall show her.

MATTANEE: (taking the coin, then giving him a second, smaller basket as well) And this one is for you. Keep your counts in it. It floats too.

**Panel 2.** Thorne, almost smiling, steps onto the gangplank, then turns back to Samuel.

THORNE: Master Ashby. A word of advice. A town that can count itself like that will not need the Crown to count it for very much longer.

**Panel 3.** Samuel and Prudence look at each other, startled and thoughtful. Prudence gives a slow grin.

PRUDENCE: Was that a warning or a compliment?

SAMUEL: From a man who loves arithmetic? I think it was both.

**Panel 4.** Kit, at the bell rope in the cupola, waves down to the wharf.

KIT: Shall I ring it, Master Ashby?

SAMUEL: Ring it, Kit!

**Panel 5.** Final panel, wide, the bottom half of the page. The *Heron* sails out of the harbour on the evening tide, sails golden in the setting sun, Thorne a small figure at the stern rail with a basket under his arm. In the foreground, on the wharf, Samuel, Prudence, Mattanee and Tamaqua watch it go. Samuel holds the old damp ledger under one arm; in his other hand, held up to the light, a slim new cedar stick with its very first notch. Above the town, the bell swings in its cupola, and the blue pennant streams from the stockade on the hill.

SFX: DONG... DONG... DONG...

SAMUEL: (small balloon) Next year, I'm keeping two books.

MATTANEE: (small balloon) One that sinks and one that floats. Good.

CAPTION: THE END.
