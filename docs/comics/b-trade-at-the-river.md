# Trade at the River

**Logline.** Matthew Lisle, a young trader with more good intentions than river sense, makes his first honest bargain with Inali, a Cherokee trader of the town on the mound. Then a company ship arrives upriver with a royal licence, a dazzling bolt of cloth and a very shiny scale, and the new friendship is put to the test. It takes a rain shower, a sharp-eyed girl and a well-placed sandbar to show who is trading fairly.

**Cover moment.** Matthew and Inali kneeling across the woven mat, red cloth and axe head on one side, beaver furs and a basket of tobacco leaves on the other, hands meeting in a handshake, while the Pike brothers unload the flatboat and the women of the town work in the corn. It is the large panel at the top of **page 3** (panel 1).

**Setting.** A broad river below the blue hills of the southern Appalachians, at a Cherokee town on a low mound above the water. Early summer, 1735.

## Cast

- **Matthew Lisle**, trader. Mid-twenties, long brown hair tied back, rounded cheerful face, dark blue coat with gold facings and brass buttons, black tricorn edged with gold braid, white neck-cloth, buff breeches, white stockings. Keeps a little notebook of Cherokee words, most of them spelled wrong.
- **Inali**, Cherokee trader of the town. Thirties, long black hair, white cloth turban with a red stripe, long fringed buckskin hunting shirt, red sash, a string of blue and white beads and a silver gorget on his chest, leggings and moccasins. Patient, amused, a hard but fair bargainer. Speaks good trade English.
- **Saloli**, Inali's niece, about eleven. Her name means "squirrel", which suits her: she climbs everything and notices everything. Plain deerskin dress, hair in two braids.
- **Grandfather Atsila**, elder of the town. White-haired, wrapped in a woven mantle, slow to speak; his words end discussions.
- **Ezra and Jonah Pike**, brothers, Matthew's boatmen. Ezra: older, stocky, blue coat and black tricorn. Jonah: lanky, white shirt, blue waistcoat, round black hat. They argue constantly about which way is upstream.
- **Mr Crispin Vane**, agent of a large trading company. Thin, powdered wig, plum-coloured coat with too much lace, a silver-topped cane. Carries a royal licence on a ribbon and a gleaming brass scale.

## PAGE 1

**Panel 1.** Wide opening panel. A broad, sunny river between forested banks. A flat-bottomed river boat, loaded with crates, barrels and sacks, sits motionless in mid-stream, tilted slightly. Ezra and Jonah Pike lean on long poles, straining. Matthew stands on a crate at the bow, hand shading his eyes, completely confident.

CAPTION: Summer, 1735. Somewhere on a very large river.

MATTHEW: Nearly there, lads! One more push!

**Panel 2.** Close on the hull: the flat bottom firmly wedged on a pale sandbar just under the water.

SFX: GRRRNK.

**Panel 3.** Ezra and Jonah, red-faced, poles bent.

EZRA: We've been "nearly there" since breakfast, Master Lisle.

JONAH: That's because we're on a sandbar, Ezra.

EZRA: It's not a sandbar, it's the river being difficult.

**Panel 4.** On the green bank, a man and a girl sit comfortably in the shade of a sycamore, watching the boat with great enjoyment. It is Inali, eating a handful of berries, and Saloli, perched on a low branch above him, swinging her legs.

SALOLI: They've been stuck since the sun was there. (points low in the sky)

INALI: Don't tell them yet. This is the best thing that's happened all week.

**Panel 5.** Matthew notices them and waves his tricorn enthusiastically, nearly falling off his crate.

MATTHEW: HALLO! Good day! Er... (fumbles for his notebook) ...O-SEE-OH!

---

## PAGE 2

**Panel 1.** Inali stands, still amused, and calls across the water. Saloli hangs upside down from her branch to watch.

INALI: Osiyo! Very good. Now, trader: step to your left.

**Panel 2.** Matthew, puzzled, steps to his left on the crate. Nothing happens.

MATTHEW: Like this?

INALI: Not you. The BOAT.

**Panel 3.** The Pikes push the bow left with their poles; the boat slides off the sandbar into dark, deep water with a lurch. Matthew topples backwards onto a sack of corn.

SFX: SHLUUUP! / FLUMPH!

JONAH: Deep water! He was right, Ezra!

EZRA: I knew that.

**Panel 4.** The flatboat pulled up on the bank below the town. Matthew climbs out, brushing corn off his coat, and bows to Inali. Saloli runs down from the tree to inspect the boat's cargo.

MATTHEW: Matthew Lisle, sir, of the trading house at the river mouth. Thank you. I have a notebook full of your words, but none of them is "left".

INALI: Inali. Show me this notebook.

**Panel 5.** Close on Inali and Saloli peering at Matthew's notebook, which is full of scribbles and little drawings (no readable words, just squiggles). Saloli giggles.

SALOLI: (pointing at a drawing) He's written that he wants to buy a grandmother.

MATTHEW: I... what? No! That's "basket"!

**Panel 6.** Inali hands the notebook back, smiling broadly.

INALI: Come tomorrow, early, to the landing. Bring what you have to trade. And leave the grandmother alone.

## PAGE 3

**Panel 1.** Large panel, half the page: THE COVER MOMENT. The sunny riverbank clearing below the town. Matthew and Inali kneel facing each other across a woven cane mat as equals. Matthew offers a folded bolt of red cloth and an iron axe head; Inali offers a heavy bundle of beaver furs and a basket of tobacco leaves. Both smile; their hands are about to clasp. Crates, a barrel and a sack of corn beside Matthew. Behind him, the Pike brothers unload the flatboat. Behind Inali, the town of wattle-and-daub houses with thatched roofs on its mound, a palisade, women working in tall green corn. Far down the broad river, a small white sail.

CAPTION: The next morning.

**Panel 2.** Close on the goods, both men's hands pointing as they bargain.

INALI: Six good winter beaver for the cloth. Two more for the axe.

MATTHEW: Six for the cloth and the axe both. It is very good cloth.

**Panel 3.** Inali lifts the edge of the red cloth, rubs it between finger and thumb, sniffs it, holds it up to the light. Serious.

INALI: It is good cloth. It does not smell of lies. Seven, and you take a basket of tobacco as a gift.

**Panel 4.** Matthew leans forward to sniff the tobacco politely and is overcome by a gigantic sneeze. Saloli, sitting cross-legged nearby, falls over laughing.

SFX: AH... AH... AHHCHOOOO!

**Panel 5.** The handshake, at last, both men laughing.

MATTHEW: Seven. Agreed.

INALI: Agreed. Come back at the new moon. Bring the same good cloth, and some needles. The women of the town want needles more than they want you.

---

## PAGE 4

**Panel 1.** Wide, late afternoon. The trading done, Matthew sits with Inali and Saloli on the bank, sharing a bowl of bean bread. Ezra and Jonah snooze against the corn sacks.

MATTHEW: At the river mouth they told me trading here was hard. That your people drive a hard bargain.

INALI: We do. Traders who come once, we bargain hard with. Traders who come back, we bargain fair with. You have to come back to find out which you are.

**Panel 2.** Saloli suddenly stands, pointing downriver.

SALOLI: Uncle! Look! A great big boat with wings!

**Panel 3.** Wide panel. A three-masted merchant ship coming up the river under full sail with the breeze behind it, a plain red pennant at the masthead. It is far too big for the river. Sailors are lowering a launch.

**Panel 4.** Close on Matthew, his face falling.

MATTHEW: Oh no. That's a company ship. The company doesn't like anyone trading on "their" rivers.

INALI: Their rivers? (looks around at the river, amused) Did they bring it with them?

**Panel 5.** The ship's launch reaches the bank. A thin man in a plum coat with lace cuffs and a powdered wig steps out onto the sand, keeping his buckled shoes carefully out of the water. A sailor holds an enormous gleaming brass scale behind him.

VANE: Good day, good day! Crispin Vane, agent of the company, holder of the royal licence for all trade upon this river. Who is in charge here?

---

## PAGE 5

**Panel 1.** The town's open square in front of the council house, a round building with a conical roof. Grandfather Atsila sits on a bench in his woven mantle, townspeople gathered. Vane stands in the centre with his scale and a sailor holding out a dazzling bolt of crimson cloth. Inali stands to one side, arms folded; Matthew, behind, holds his hat in his hands.

VANE: I offer twice what this young fellow offers! Two lengths of the finest crimson for every beaver pelt! Weighed fairly on my scale!

**Panel 2.** Murmurs among the townspeople; several women finger the shiny red cloth with interest. A young man holds up a fur hopefully.

TOWNSWOMAN: It is very red.

YOUNG MAN: Twice as much...

**Panel 3.** Vane unrolls a parchment with a large ribbon and a red wax seal (no readable words, no emblem drawn, just a blob of wax). He holds it up proudly.

VANE: And this licence, signed by the Crown, says that only the company may trade on this river. This young man is, regrettably, a smuggler.

**Panel 4.** Close on Matthew, shocked, and on Inali, glancing at him sideways with a raised eyebrow.

MATTHEW: That's not true! Well... it's partly true. The company has a licence. But nobody asked you about it!

**Panel 5.** Grandfather Atsila raises one hand. The square goes quiet.

ATSILA: We will hear more tomorrow. Tonight, everyone may think. Thinking is free. (to Vane) Even on your river.

---

## PAGE 6

**Panel 1.** Evening at the landing. Matthew sits gloomily on his flatboat. Ezra and Jonah pack crates around him.

MATTHEW: Two lengths for every pelt. I can't match it. I'd be bankrupt in a month.

EZRA: Nobody could match it, Master Lisle. Not honestly.

JONAH: That's the point, Ezra.

EZRA: I know that's the point. I was MAKING the point.

**Panel 2.** Meanwhile, at Vane's camp further down the beach, Saloli lies flat in the long grass at the edge of the firelight, chin on her hands, watching Vane's sailors weigh a stack of furs that some young men have brought down. The brass scale gleams.

**Panel 3.** Close on the scale, from Saloli's point of view. The pan with the furs sinks very low, as if the furs were feathers on one side and stones on the other; Vane slides only a small weight onto the opposite pan and nods. Something dark and lumpy is visible underneath the fur pan.

VANE (small, off): Light furs. Very light. Shall we say... half a length each?

**Panel 4.** Saloli's eyes narrow. She mouths to herself.

SALOLI: Twice of half is... not very much at all.

**Panel 5.** Saloli creeps away through the grass towards the town, quick as her name.

---

## PAGE 7

**Panel 1.** Night, Inali's house: a wattle-and-daub house, thatched roof, a fire in the middle. Saloli bursts in, breathless. Inali is mending a moccasin; Matthew sits awkwardly on a low bench, his knees up near his ears.

SALOLI: Uncle! The shiny scale is a liar!

**Panel 2.** Saloli acting it out with two bowls and a stone, cheeks puffed with outrage.

SALOLI: He promises two of cloth for each fur. Then he weighs the furs and says they are light, so each fur is only half. There's a lump under the fur pan. I saw it.

**Panel 3.** Inali and Matthew exchange a long look. A slow smile on Inali's face.

INALI: So he gives twice as much of almost nothing.

MATTHEW: That's what the company does. They make the numbers look grand and the deal look small.

**Panel 4.** Thunder rumbles outside. A flash at the door.

SFX: BRRROOOM...

**Panel 5.** Inali gets up and looks out at the rain beginning to fall, thoughtful.

INALI: And his cloth, Matthew. It is very red. Do you know what happens to cloth that is too red, too cheap, in the rain?

**Panel 6.** Matthew starts to grin.

MATTHEW: Oh, I know exactly what happens.

---

## PAGE 8

**Panel 1.** Morning after the storm. Wide. The town square, wet and glistening, puddles reflecting the sky. People are gathering again. Several townspeople who took Vane's crimson cloth as a "free sample" yesterday are standing in a row with pink hands, pink arms, and in one case a pink dog.

CAPTION: The next morning.

**Panel 2.** Close on a townswoman holding up the crimson bolt, which has run into blotchy pale pink, the colour dripping onto her moccasins.

TOWNSWOMAN: It was red. Now it is... a sunset that is sorry for itself.

**Panel 3.** A young man holds up his pink-stained arm next to Matthew's red cloth, which a child is dunking in a puddle. Matthew's cloth stays perfectly red.

YOUNG MAN: His cloth bleeds. Yours stays.

MATTHEW: It's dyed with madder and fixed properly. Costs twice as much to make. That's why I can't sell it at twice the length.

**Panel 4.** Vane arrives, umbrella held over his wig by a sailor, followed by the scale. He sees the pink people and stops.

VANE: Ah. A small... a small colour issue. Nothing a second washing won't cure.

**Panel 5.** Inali steps forward, calm, carrying one of his own beaver pelts.

INALI: Then let us talk about weight instead. Grandfather, may we test the shiny scale?

ATSILA: Test it.

---

## PAGE 9

**Panel 1.** The square. The brass scale on a bench in the middle. Townspeople crowd around. Vane stands stiffly beside it. Inali places a single beaver pelt on one pan.

INALI: Everybody in this town knows what a good winter beaver weighs. Our hands know it.

**Panel 2.** Several townspeople lift identical pelts in their hands, weighing them by feel, nodding. An old woman hefts one and holds up her hand with fingers spread, decisively.

OLD WOMAN: About this much. (indicates a hand-weight with a gesture)

**Panel 3.** Matthew places a small brass weight from his own steelyard on the other pan. The fur pan should balance, but it sinks low with a thump.

SFX: KLONK.

MATTHEW: That's odd. By this scale, a winter beaver weighs about as much as a pine cone.

**Panel 4.** Saloli darts under the bench like a squirrel before anyone can stop her. Vane reaches out, too late.

VANE: Child! Don't touch the company's —

**Panel 5.** Saloli pops up from under the bench holding a lump of grey lead, clumsily tied on with string, triumphantly over her head.

SALOLI: The company's lump!

**Panel 6.** Wide reaction panel. The whole square erupts in laughter. Even Grandfather Atsila's shoulders shake. Vane turns as plum-coloured as his coat.

SFX: HA HA HA HA!

---

## PAGE 10

**Panel 1.** Vane, flustered, waving the licence with its blob of wax like a shield.

VANE: This changes nothing! The Crown has decreed that only the company may trade here! I have the licence!

**Panel 2.** Grandfather Atsila rises slowly from his bench. Everyone falls quiet again. He is not angry; he speaks gently, like a teacher.

ATSILA: Your Crown may decide who trades in his house. This town is our house. The river at its foot is where our grandmothers fished. We decide who sits on our mat.

**Panel 3.** Atsila turns to Matthew and Inali.

ATSILA: This one's cloth stays red in the rain. This one's scale has no lump. Inali says he comes back. That is our licence.

**Panel 4.** Vane gathers his scale and his dignity and stalks off towards his launch, sailors trailing behind with the sad pink cloth.

VANE: The company will hear of this!

INALI: (calling after him, friendly) Tell them to bring better cloth!

**Panel 5.** Wide, later. Out on the river, the big ship has weighed anchor and is turning to go downstream with the current, sails filling... and comes to a sudden, shuddering halt with a lurch of masts.

SFX: GRRRRRRNK!

---

## PAGE 11

**Panel 1.** The landing. Everyone has come down to look. The great ship is stuck fast on the same sandbar that caught Matthew's flatboat, leaning slightly, sailors running about the deck. Vane stands at the rail, hopping with fury.

JONAH: Same sandbar!

EZRA: It's not a sandbar, Jonah, it's the river being —

JONAH and MATTHEW: — difficult. Yes.

**Panel 2.** Saloli tugs Inali's sash, grinning wickedly.

SALOLI: Leave them there, Uncle. Like yesterday. It's the best thing that's happened all week.

**Panel 3.** Inali considers it, tempted, then sighs and smiles.

INALI: The river taught them a lesson. We don't need to teach them a second one. Besides, a ship on a sandbar frightens the fish.

**Panel 4.** Inali paddles out in a dugout canoe to the ship's side. Vane leans over the rail, wig askew. Inali points.

INALI: Your rudder to the left, and kedge your anchor that way, to the deep side. One thing in payment.

VANE: (sulkily) What?

**Panel 5.** Close on Inali, pointing up at the scale glinting on the deck.

INALI: The shiny scale. Without its lump. The town will keep it at the landing, so everybody's trade is weighed the same. Even yours, next time.

**Panel 6.** The ship slides free into deep water, sails filling, heading away downstream. The brass scale sits on the bank, sparkling. Vane, small at the stern, gives a stiff, reluctant wave.

SFX: SHLUUUUP.

---

## PAGE 12

**Panel 1.** The new moon, a month later. Wide. The trading ground at the landing, busy and festive. The brass scale now stands on a wooden post under a little thatched roof of its own. Townspeople weigh furs on it; Saloli supervises sternly, arms folded.

CAPTION: At the new moon.

**Panel 2.** Matthew and Inali kneel on the woven mat again, as on the first day. This time Matthew sits cross-legged, comfortably, and has a fat new notebook. Between them: red cloth, a packet of needles, a cooking kettle; furs and a basket of dried beans.

MATTHEW: Wado, my friend. Did I say it right?

INALI: You said it right. Your grandmother would be proud.

**Panel 3.** Grandfather Atsila comes down to the mat and places a string of white beads in Matthew's hands. Matthew looks up, moved.

ATSILA: White is for peace and for friends. Your trading house is a friend of this town now. When others come, we will remember who came back.

**Panel 4.** Close on Matthew holding the white beads carefully, and Inali beside him, nodding.

INALI: That means you get the first look at the winter furs. And the hardest bargains. Friends always get the hardest bargains.

MATTHEW: (laughing) I'd expect nothing less.

**Panel 5.** Final panel, wide, the bottom half of the page. Evening on the broad river, the blue hills behind. Matthew's flatboat pulls away from the landing, loaded with furs, the Pike brothers poling, Matthew at the bow waving his tricorn. On the bank under the sycamore, Inali and Saloli sit and wave back. In the foreground, just under the water, the pale shape of the famous sandbar. The flatboat is heading straight for it.

SALOLI and INALI: (together, big balloon) LEFT!

CAPTION: THE END.
